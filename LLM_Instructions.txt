================================================================================
PROJECT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

"Pędzący Piotrek" is a digital board game in Python + pygame. It is a fan-made,
non-commercial extension of the board game "Pędzące Żółwie" (Schildkrötenrennen
/ Ribbit), built for one specific person's friend group.

The base game: each player secretly owns one coloured pawn; movement cards move
named colours forward or backward; a pawn landing on an occupied field STACKS on
top of whoever is there, and moving the pawn at the BOTTOM of a tower carries
the whole tower with it.

The extension: one player is PIOTREK. He secretly owns one pawn colour and wins
by getting it to the finish. Everyone else is a HUNTER. Hunters win by stacking
ALL pawns onto one field and then checking whether the pawn at the BOTTOM of the
resulting tower is Piotrek's. A wrong guess eliminates that colour from
suspicion and play continues.

Turn order is fixed and unusual: Piotrek acts on every third slot.
    Piotrek → Hunter1 → Hunter2 → Piotrek → Hunter3 → Hunter1 → Piotrek → ...
This is implemented in engine/turn_order.py and MUST NOT be "simplified" into a
round robin.

Two goals, in the owner's words: (1) make it easy to test and balance the
mechanics, (2) eventually play online with friends.


================================================================================
LONG TERM GOALS
================================================================================

1. A polished digital board game that feels like a board game, not a debug tool.
2. Online play with friends anywhere: everyone connects OUTWARDS to one small
   dedicated server, found by a six-character room code. No matchmaking, no
   accounts, no database, one lobby at a time. The "host" is a player with the
   Start button, not a machine anyone connects to. (This entry used to say
   "host-authoritative, no dedicated server"; that model cannot cross NAT and
   was abandoned in stage 11 — see N37.)
3. Distributable from GitHub, launchable as a .exe (PyInstaller).
4. Content (cards, characters, board themes) editable in JSON without touching
   Python, because balancing means hundreds of small content changes.
5. Room for external art, music and sound effects without a rewrite.

CURRENT IMPLEMENTATION (what actually works today)
================================================================================

WORKING:
- Full game setup: 3–6 players, character dealing with a guaranteed Piotrek,
  starting hands (5 for Piotrek, 3 for everyone else), secret pawn assignment.
- Menu validation: if every seat picked a character by hand and none of them is
  Piotrek, the game refuses to start and says so in red.
- Five decks with draw/discard/reshuffle: movement, mods, chest, characters,
  piotrek_skills.
- CHEST TIMING (stage 22): five or six players are dealt a chest card every
  eligible round as before; a table of FOUR OR FEWER is dealt one every SECOND
  eligible round, and the rota steps once per hand-out so no hunter is starved.
  The ribbon marker is FILLED on a dealing round and OUTLINED otherwise, and it
  keeps moving either way.  See "HOW THE CHEST ROTA WORKS".
- CHEST RECIPIENTS (stage 23): a dealing round feeds TWO seats — Piotrek every
  time, plus the hunter the rota has reached.  Piotrek had never been dealt one
  by the engine despite the ribbon marking him since the chest existed.  The
  chest deck was SIXTEEN cards (eight titles, two of each) because two cards a
  round need more supply than eight could give a full table.
- NIE MASZ ROSJI (stage 36): a Chest card granting one block of one opponent
  movement, for one full round (variant 2) or two (variant 1).  A blockable
  movement is PAUSED before it happens — the card stays in the hand and nothing
  resolves — so a block cancels nothing, it simply never runs.  See "NIE MASZ
  ROSJI".
- AKO (stage 35): while it is in the rack every MOVEMENT CARD brings one
  neighbouring pawn along the same move — the pawn on the far side of the
  direction of travel, which follows the mover into the space it leaves.
  Variant 1 lets the ordinary tower rule carry whatever stands on it; variant 2
  ("TYLKO jednego") lifts that one pawn out of the stack and leaves the rest.
  See "AKO: THE PAWN THAT COMES ALONG".
- CARD VARIANTS (stage 34): a card may declare two or more readings that differ
  in text, passive and effect but NOT in title, artwork, count or deck.  Which
  one a match plays is chosen in the lobby ("Warianty" tab) or in the Card
  Library, travels as configuration, and is in the snapshot.  "Sesja na PG" has
  two: variant 1 locks abilities (what shipped), variant 2 also CANCELS every
  effect a character ability has already put into play — identified by
  `Status.origin`, never by kind or name.  See "CARD VARIANTS".
- CHEST DECK COMPOSITION (stage 27): SEVENTEEN cards, and no longer uniform —
  Dzieckorolka 2, Rage Quit 2, Balbinka 2, Nie masz Rosji 2, Gambit Patusa 3,
  Shady 2, Gejtos 3, Gamechanger 1.  Stage 23's doubling was uniform ON PURPOSE
  so that fixing the supply did not move the odds; this composition moves them
  deliberately, so the supply guarantee no longer follows from every title
  having the same count and is asserted on its own.  These are DEFAULTS from
  cards.json and are therefore also what the lobby shows.
- CHEST CARD EFFECTS (stages 27-28): the Chest is FINISHED.  Dzieckorolka moves
  a chosen pawn three forward and sweeps up the top pawn of every field it walks
  through; Rage Quit swaps every occupied Mod Patusa slot for a fresh draw;
  Balbinka moves every pawn two forward or back with no tower rule; Gambit
  Patusa reverses every movement card for the WHOLE OF THE NEXT ROUND; Gejtos
  pulls both neighbouring fields onto a pawn or pushes them one further away;
  Alter Ego hands Piotrek's hidden identity back to him mid-match; and since
  stage 46 KINGMAKER — Alter Ego's other face — moves the Piotrek ROLE to the
  hunter who played it, who then chooses a new identity.  See "HOW THE CHEST
  CARD EFFECTS WORK" and "ALTER EGO, KINGMAKER, AND HOW TO TOUCH THE HIDDEN
  IDENTITY".
- EVERY CHEST CARD IS PLAYABLE (stage 28), including the ones whose rule is
  still settled at the table (Shady, and Nie masz Rosji's chest copy).  They
  declare ``manual``, which resolves to nothing and says so.  Kingmaker was one
  of them until stage 46 and is no longer.  A card with NO ``effect``
  field is not playable at all — ``Card.is_playable`` reads exactly that — so
  "undesigned" used to mean "unclickable", which at the chest limit meant a
  player could hold two dead cards.  Never leave a card without an effect.
- Procedurally generated horizontal board with guaranteed field spacing,
  rivers, bridges, villages, forest, rocks, a starting camp and a finish banner.
- Board positions vs fields: a widened row is ONE position holding two fields
  labelled 12a / 12b.  Landing there stops the move and asks which half.
- Configurable frequency of widened rows (menu, SessionConfig, CLI, theme).
- Surrounding countryside tiled behind the board so it never floats on panel
  colour, with parallax as the camera moves.
- Camera: smooth zoom, pan, scrollbars, zoom slider, and left-drag on empty
  ground to grab the map (unsmoothed, so it tracks the cursor exactly).
- Hand as an animated Hearthstone-style fan with hover, drag and drop.
- A GENERIC EFFECT ENGINE (stage 4).  Cards and abilities declare an effect in
  JSON; a registered handler turns it into a Plan of Operations; the executor
  applies it.  No card-title branching anywhere.
- PLAYABLE movement cards: 26 of 30 definitions.  Deterministic ones act at
  once; select-a-token ones (Janek, Kolos z paki, Astral 2019, Astral 2022)
  ask through the generic choice system, which shows the pawns themselves.
- MOD PATUSA EFFECTS (stages 24-25): SEVEN of the eight mods now change the
  game.  Speedrun lets a backward movement card be turned around; Masa solna
  caps a movement card at one field; Halloween pins any pawn with nobody in
  front of or behind it; Sesja na PG locks character abilities without spending
  their charges; Paczka shows every player the same window listing who holds
  which Chest cards; Squid Game replaces checking with one automatic check of
  the leading pawn per round; Shady takes the leading pawn off the map for a
  round.  AKO is the only placeholder left.  Every rule is DECLARED in
  cards.json as a ``passive`` and read through ``GameState.mod_rule`` — no
  titles in the engine.  See "HOW THE MOD PATUSA EFFECTS WORK".
- TABLE COMPOSITION IS A SETTING (stage 26): the pre-game panel has FOUR tabs —
  Karty ruchu, Mody Patusa, Karty Skrzyni and Umiejętności — with ``[-] n [+]``
  for every card in the first three decks and for every character ability's
  charges in the fourth.  Defaults are whatever the data files print; an empty
  mapping means "as printed", so an older client still gets real decks.  All
  four replicate like every other setting.  See "THE SETTINGS PANEL".
- THE CARD LIBRARY (stage 32): a BOOK in the bottom-right corner of the board
  opens an encyclopedia of every card, drawn as REAL cards through the ordinary
  ``CardRenderer`` — Signature artwork, hover reveal and all.  Four tabs mirror
  the lobby's four categories.  The three table decks show how many copies the
  match holds and can be changed mid-game; the ability tab shows each ability's
  owning character, a restore action and its remaining uses.  Since stage 33
  the grid is FOUR cards across and every deck card carries a "Dobierz kartę"
  button that fetches that exact card into the clicking player's hand.
  Everything it changes leaves as a Command.  See "THE CARD LIBRARY".
- MOD DECK COMPOSITION (stage 24): thirteen cards, not one of each — Speedrun 2,
  Masa solna 2, AKO 1, Halloween 1, Sesja na PG 2, Paczka 2, Squid Game 1,
  Shady 2.  Those are DEFAULTS from cards.json; the lobby sets any count per
  title through the "Skład talii" panel on both settings screens, and the
  numbers replicate like every other setting.
- MOD PATUSA SELECTION (stage 21): every second round from the third (both
  numbers set in the lobby) the table PAUSES and both factions choose a mod.
  Piotrek picks one of three; the hunters vote on three of their own.  See
  "HOW THE MOD PATUSA SELECTION WORKS" below.
- Thunderfuck replaces the active mods: the new card takes the LEFT slot, the
  old LEFT slides RIGHT, and the old RIGHT is discarded.  With an EMPTY rack it
  does nothing at all.  Three copies in the movement deck.
- Gamechanger really becomes Alter Ego / Kingmaker: the card in the hand is the
  transformed one, and it goes back to the deck as a Gamechanger.
- EDIT MODE: on (default) anybody at the keyboard plays every seat, as the
  prototype did; off, only the local seat may act — enforced in the engine, so
  it is the rule the network will need.
- CHARACTER ABILITIES: Big D Randy, Lubin, Mitoman, Norbur, Dziad, Ondrej,
  Dziubdziuch and Atencjusz all execute.  Glockboy still reports that it has
  nothing to do and spends nothing — checking DOES exist (stage 17, see
  VICTORY AND THE HIDDEN IDENTITY), so what Glockboy and Ice Block are waiting
  on now is a decision about how they should interact with it, not the
  mechanic.
- PIOTREK'S SKILLS: dealt at game start.  ChatGPT (passive: two movement cards
  fewer and one chest slot; active: +1 range on the next movement card, five
  times) and Dług u Tomasza (records its state) work; Ice Block still reports
  that it has nothing to do (see the note on Glockboy above).
- PERSISTENT GAMEPLAY STATES: frozen, skip turn, linked, extra turn, movement
  bonus, restricted movement, forbidden adjacency, check refusal.
- CHEST HAND LIMIT with a keep/discard overlay (Piotrek 2, hunters 1).
- SPECIAL PRESENTATIONS: Gamechanger transforms into Alter Ego or Kingmaker on
  the way to the hand; Seks z pedałami announces itself, then reveals and plays
  a random movement card.
- "Recently played" strip: the last three played cards, held ~11 s, and
  hovering one enlarges it above the whole interface and pauses its fade.
- Two-slot "Mody Patusa" rack, character panel with abilities and Piotrek's
  skills, hunters' colour-elimination notepad, manual round counter, player
  renaming, free pawn dragging with snapping and stacking.
- GENUINELY RESPONSIVE UI from 1280×760 to 4K (stage 29).  THREE scales, not
  one: ``ui_scale`` for geometry, ``type_scale`` for type, ``panel_scale`` for
  the side columns — plus card type, which is a fraction of the CARD and of
  nothing else.  Named breakpoints (wide / medium / compact) decide rules; the
  pixels interpolate continuously so a dragged window never jumps.  When space
  runs short the board gives up height first, then the columns, then the
  margins, and the hand LAST — its share grows on a small screen, because the
  movement cards are what is actually being read.  2560×1440 is the reference
  and is byte-identical to stage 28.  See "HOW SCALING WORKS".
- MULTIPLAYER over the internet, CLIENT/SERVER: a dedicated server process
  (``python -m pedzacy_piotrek.server``) owns the game; every player, the one
  who opened the room included, is a WebSocket client that connects OUTWARDS.
  Friends join with a six-character ROOM CODE — no address, no port, no router
  configuration, so two people in different countries can play.
- Reconnection: a dropped player keeps their seat for a grace period, comes back
  on a new connection and is replayed up to date automatically.
- Turn ownership: outside hot-seat games only the active player may move, and
  only for their own seat; the interface says whose turn it is.
- THE TURN LOOP RUNS ITSELF: playing (or discarding) a movement card refills the
  hand to its proper size and hands the turn on, the round advances when its
  slots run out, and the chest card for that round is dealt automatically.
  There is also an explicit "Zakończ turę" button (bottom-right of the board).
- RESOLUTION INDEPENDENT: type and cards are RENDERED at the size they are seen,
  never enlarged after rasterising; the columns are measured from their contents
  and everything left over goes to the board.
- Each machine shows ITS OWN hand, whoever's turn it is.
- Network debug panel (F3, off by default): server, room code, connection state,
  ping, sent/received counters, in-flight actions, reconnect/resync counts, and a
  state fingerprint for spotting desyncs.
- ONE VISUAL LANGUAGE for hover, selection, focus, active and pressed, across
  every screen, dialog, panel, tile, slot and button (stage 12). Highlights
  follow the shape of what they highlight; buttons and steppers measure their
  own captions instead of being given a width.
- HOSTING A GAME ACTUALLY WORKS (stage 13). It did not before: the host screen
  sent the table settings before the handshake finished, the server refused
  them, and the fatal refusal threw the host out of a room they never created.
  Every outbound message now waits for the server's WELCOME.
- ONE TEXT EDITOR behind every text field (stages 13-14). Selection, caret
  placement, word jumps, held-key repeat, placeholder and Ctrl+A/C/X/V work in
  the menus AND in the in-game rename box, which previously had none of it.
  The one gap is mouse selection in the rename box — see TEXT FIELDS below.
- THE ADDRESS IS TYPED ONCE (stage 14). A server that a room was actually
  created on or joined through is remembered in the user's own config
  directory and pre-fills the field next time.
- ROOM CODE AND SERVER ADDRESS ARE COPYABLE (stage 14). A button beside each,
  with a two-second confirmation, because the code is going into a chat
  window and reading it aloud is the fallback, not the plan.
- NO PROTOCOL TEXT REACHES A PLAYER (stage 13). net/messages.py translates
  anything technical into a Polish sentence; the server's own prose survives.
- DEPLOYABLE IN A BROWSER-VERIFIABLE WAY (stage 13): SERVER_SETUP.md,
  railway.json, requirements-server.txt (websockets only, no pygame),
  .railwayignore, and an HTTP health page at / so a first-time deployer can
  tell "server down" from "address wrong".
- WIN CONDITIONS, SERVER-SIDE (stage 17). Piotrek picks his colour privately at
  the start; the server is the only machine told, and it is the only one that
  judges. Piotrek wins by walking that colour to the meta; the hunters win by
  stacking every pawn on one field and having his at the bottom. A wrong tower
  crosses that colour off every notepad at once. Two different endings, with a
  reveal, a way back to the poczekalnia and a way out.
- SIGNATURE CARDS (stage 30): a card with a picture in ``assets/card_art`` is
  drawn as the ARTWORK, with the title overlaid near the bottom and the
  description revealed on hover behind a darkening gradient; a card without one
  is drawn exactly as it always was.  The link is the FILENAME — ``Troll.png``
  finds the card titled "Troll" — so illustrating a card is dropping a file in
  a folder, with no JSON and no renderer change.  Troll is the worked example
  and the only illustrated card so far.  See "HOW SIGNATURE CARDS WORK".
- CARD BACKS ARE ARTWORK (stage 47): every deck's back is a picture in
  ``assets/card_backs``, and which picture belongs to which deck is one table,
  ``settings.CARD_BACKS``.  The drawn cover that used to be painted per deck
  colour survives only as the fallback for a missing file.  See "CARD BACKS".
- 1243 automated tests.

================================================================================
PROJECT ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

Strict one-way flow:

    ui/ ──builds──> engine/commands ──applied by──> engine/game_state
     ^                                                    |
     └──────────── engine/events <────emitted by──────────┘

RULES THAT MUST NOT BE BROKEN:

R1. engine/ imports NO pygame. Verify with:
        grep -rn "import pygame" pedzacy_piotrek/engine/   # must find nothing
R2. The UI never mutates GameState. It builds a Command and calls submit().
    If you find yourself writing `state.something = ...` in ui/, add a command.
R3. Rendering contains no rules. render/ draws what it is told.
R4. All randomness comes from SessionConfig.seed. Never call the module-level
    random.* functions; use the injected random.Random. This is what makes
    host and clients agree without transmitting state.
R5. Every rectangle on screen comes from ui/layout.py. No coordinate literals
    in panels.
R6. Anything a player does that changes the game must be a Command, so that
    multiplayer only has to synchronise Commands.

HOW THE BOARD WORKS
================================================================================

BoardModel.generate(cell_count, seed) builds everything. The board is DATA
DERIVED FROM TWO NUMBERS; it is never stored (to_dict emits cell_count, seed,
theme and pawn placement only).

Coordinate names: "along" = direction of travel, "across" = perpendicular.
BoardLayout.orientation is "horizontal" (start on the left). _to_world() maps
along/across to world x/y; switching to a vertical board is that one value.

Generation steps:
1. make_rows(): which rows hold one field and which hold two. One row per
   board POSITION (see "THE BOARD SIZE IS A LOGICAL LENGTH" below), keeping the
   prototype's rule that "the board always ends on a single field".
2. _fit_path(): find a road of exactly the needed length that satisfies THREE
   constraints (see IMPORTANT DESIGN DECISIONS D3). Returns BoardGeometry.
3. _place_tiles(): fields at fixed arc-length intervals, single rows on the
   centre line, double rows offset by ±lane_offset.
4. _place_camp(): waiting slots behind the start.
5. _carve_rivers(): rivers run ACROSS the road; the crossed field becomes a
   BRIDGE tile.
6. _scatter_decor(): rejection sampling with ProximityGrid; keeps clear of the
   road, the water, the camp and the finish banner. HILL props are background
   and may pass under everything.

POSITIONS VS FIELDS (added stage 3, corrected stage 45, important):
A widened row holds two fields that share one board position.  They are 12a and
12b: both are twelve steps from the start.  Therefore:
  * ``BoardModel.tiles``     — every field.  A doubled position contributes
    TWO of these, so ``len(tiles) >= cell_count``.
  * ``BoardModel.positions`` — logical steps.  MOVEMENT COUNTS THESE, and
    ``cell_count`` COUNTS THESE: ``position_count == cell_count`` always.
  * ``Tile.number`` is the position number, ``Tile.variant`` is "a"/"b" or "",
    and ``Tile.label`` is what players see.
Queries: position(i), position_count, last_position, meta_number,
position_of_pawn(pawn), tiles_at_position(i), tile_by_label("12a").
Never go back to numbering fields sequentially — the a/b pair is a rule, not a
label, and effects.route_between() depends on it.

THE BOARD SIZE IS A LOGICAL LENGTH (stage 45, load-bearing):
``cell_count`` / ``SessionConfig.board_cells`` / the lobby's "Liczba pól
planszy" is a count of POSITIONS, and therefore THE NUMBER THE META STANDS ON.
Ask for 24 and the meta is 24 — whether the generator widened no rows, two, or
twenty-three.  ``double_frequency`` changes how many FIELDS the board holds; it
must never change how long the board is.
  * ``make_rows()`` emits exactly one row per position: ``len(rows) ==
    position_count``.  It must NOT spend the budget on fields.
  * ``meta_number`` is the query for "which number is the meta" and equals
    ``cell_count``.
  * The last row is always ``single``, so the meta is never half of a pair.
  * Do not "correct" a short board with an offset after generation — the
    budget itself is what carries the meaning.
This was a real bug until stage 45: the budget counted fields, so every 4a/4b
pair ate two of it and a board asked for 24 finished on 19.  Regression tests
live in ``test_board.py`` ("logical positions vs physical fields") and
``test_engine.py`` (lobby size → meta, end to end).

Row types come either from ``BoardTheme.row_pattern`` (a fixed cycle) or from
``double_frequency`` (probability per row, drawn from the seeded RNG).  The menu
sets the frequency; ``--doubles N`` does the same from the command line.

Stacking API on BoardModel: place_pawn, remove_pawn, pawn_tile, stack_depth,
stack_position, pawn_position, carried_pawns, tower_of.
carried_pawns() is the turtle-tower rule and is load-bearing.

verify_spacing() returns None for a good board or a Polish description of the
worst offending pair. Call it in any test that generates boards.

HOW THE EFFECT ENGINE WORKS  (stage 4 — read this before adding gameplay)
================================================================================

    card / ability
        ↓  EffectSpec from JSON  {"type": "...", ...params}
    handler registered with @effect("type")     ← engine/effects.py
        ↓  returns one of:
    Plan(operations)  |  Choice(key, kind, options)  |  Refusal  |  NotAvailable
        ↓
    GameState._execute() applies the operations   ← the ONLY mutation point
        ↓
    events  →  the view animates

OPERATIONS (the vocabulary an effect may use):
    MovePawn(pawn, route, tiles, carried, teleport)
    GrantStatus(status) / ClearStatus(kind, subject) / SpendStatus(...)
    PlayRandomCard(deck_id)      — resolved by the executor, needs the RNG
    Announce(text)

HANDLERS ARE PURE.  They read the state and return a description; they never
mutate.  That is what lets the interface call resolve() while a card is being
dragged and show exactly what will happen, using the same function the engine
will use to do it.

TARGET SELECTION.  A handler that needs a pawn returns Choice(kind="pawn"),
and the interface renders the pawns as coloured tokens (ui/overlays.py,
ChoicePrompt._draw_pawn_option) plus rings them on the board; the player may
click either.  This is the ONE selection system — cards and abilities share it,
and a new effect that needs a pawn needs no interface work at all.

CHOICES.  A handler that needs a decision returns Choice(key, kind, options).
The interface asks, then resubmits the same command with the answer added to
``choices``.  ``kind`` is "pawn", "tile" or "option"; one modal handles all
three.  An effect may ask several times (Dziad: which pawn → how far → which
half of a doubled field); each answer accumulates in the same map.

TO ADD A NEW CARD OR ABILITY:
    1. add "effect" / "ability" to the JSON entry;
    2. if its ``type`` already exists, you are done — no code at all;
    3. otherwise add one @effect("type") handler that returns existing
       Operations.  Only a genuinely new *kind* of mutation needs a new
       Operation plus one entry in GameState._OPERATIONS.

NEVER add a card-specific branch anywhere. tests/test_abilities.py asserts that
every ability and effect in the data has a registered handler.

HOW GAMEPLAY STATES WORK
================================================================================

engine/statuses.py.  A status has a kind, a subject (PAWN / PLAYER / TABLE), a
payload and an expiry measured in TURNS (GameState.turn_counter, which advances
ONCE PER TURN TAKEN — not only when the seat changes; with a single hunter the
cadence really does give one seat two slots in a row, and a turn that did not
count was a status that never expired).  "For one full turn" is duration_turns=1.

Kinds: FROZEN, SKIP_TURN, LINKED, EXTRA_TURN, MOVEMENT_BONUS,
RESTRICTED_MOVEMENT, FORBIDDEN_ADJACENCY, CHECK_REFUSAL.

Rules read the tracker rather than testing flags:
    statuses.pawn_has(FROZEN, pawn)          → movement refuses
    statuses.linked_partners(pawn)           → they travel together
    statuses.movement_range()                → Norbur's restriction
    statuses.movement_bonus_for(player)      → ChatGPT's +1, spent by one card
Statuses are in the snapshot, so they cross the network with everything else.
NEVER add a one-off boolean to GameState for a new mechanic — add a StatusKind.


================================================================================
THE TURN CURSOR  (stage 20 — read before touching the turn loop)
================================================================================

WHAT WENT WRONG
---------------
The game looped over the first three slots of round one for ever:

    Piotrek -> Glockboy -> Atencjusz -> Piotrek -> Glockboy -> Atencjusz -> ...

Everybody further down the round never played. Rounds never ended, so the round
counter froze, chest hand-outs stopped, statuses never expired, and Troll and
Stańczyk could not be reached at all.

It was reported as a regression from stage 19. IT WAS NOT. The same three-seat
loop reproduces on the stage-18 tree, byte-identical code, and thirteen of the
new turn tests fail there too. Stage 19 only made it VISIBLE, by adding the
first mechanics that depend on a round ever finishing. Worth remembering: "the
last change broke it" and "the last change revealed it" look identical from the
outside, and the way to tell them apart is to run the reproduction against the
previous tree before touching anything.

ROOT CAUSE
----------
`turn_slot` is a CURSOR into the round's order, but `_end_turn` recovered it by
SEARCHING for the seat that was about to play:

    self.turn_slot = order.index(seat) if seat in order else 0     # WRONG

`list.index` returns the FIRST match. Piotrek occupies every third slot, so
round one's order

    [Piotrek, Glockboy, Atencjusz, Piotrek, Dziubdziuch, Mitoman, Piotrek, ...]
       0         1          2         3          4           5        6

put the cursor back to 0 every single time his turn came round. The cursor
advanced 0, 1, 2, then reset to 0 and did it again for ever.

The reason it is a search at all is the underlying mistake: A SEAT DOES NOT
IDENTIFY A SLOT. Any seat may hold several slots in one round, so the seat
number simply does not contain enough information to say where in the round the
game is standing.

THE CORRECTED FLOW
------------------
`next_turn()` returns a `NextTurn(seat, round_number, slot)` — the slot travels
WITH the seat so that no caller can reconstruct it wrongly — and `_end_turn`
moves the cursor to the slot it was handed:

    upcoming = self.next_turn()
    if upcoming.round_number != self.round_number:
        self._begin_round(upcoming.round_number)      # cursor back to 0
    self.turn_slot = upcoming.slot                    # MOVED, never searched
    self.active_player_index = upcoming.seat
    self.turn_counter += 1                            # every turn counts
    self._begin_turn(depth)                           # interrupts may take it

INVARIANT, and the whole point:  seat_order()[turn_slot] == active_player_index

`current_slot()` is the only reader, and it repairs the cursor when that
invariant is broken from outside — a seat set directly in edit mode, a round
jumped to, a state built before the cursor existed. `_slot_for_seat()` searches
FORWARD from where the round already stands and only falls back to the first
occurrence, so a repair can never rewind a round either.

The cadence in `turn_order.py` was never wrong and was not touched. Piotrek
still takes every third slot, hunters still cycle continuously, and a round
still ends once every hunter has appeared in it — which is why rounds differ in
length and why a hunter may legitimately appear twice in one round. Do not
"simplify" that into a plain round-robin: the chest rota and Piotrek's tempo
both hang off it.

MODIFIED
--------
  engine/turn_order.py   + NextTurn (seat, round_number, slot)
  engine/game_state.py   next_turn(), current_slot(), _slot_for_seat() new;
                         next_seat() kept as a look-ahead that does not move
                         the cursor; _end_turn() and _set_active_player()
                         rewritten to move it; snapshot() carries turn_slot.

SYNCHRONISATION
---------------
`turn_slot` is now IN THE SNAPSHOT, and that is the sharpest part of the fix.
The cursor is real turn state that cannot be recomputed from the seat, so two
machines could stand on different slots, agree perfectly on whose turn it was,
and disagree about the entire rest of the round — with nothing in the
fingerprint able to see it. It is in there now, so that drift is a detected
desync rather than a silent one.

No new commands, and no change to how turns are transmitted: `EndTurn` still
carries a seat and every machine derives the cursor by replaying the log. That
is also why reconnection needs nothing — the cursor is rebuilt with everything
else.

WHY IT CANNOT COME BACK
-----------------------
`tests/test_turn_progression.py` walks REAL TURNS and asserts on the seats that
actually played. That distinction is the lesson: `compute_round_turn_order` had
been tested since stage 4 and was always correct, and 683 tests passed against a
game that was playing three seats in a circle, because every one of them tested
the FUNCTION THAT COMPUTES THE ORDER and none of them tested the cursor that
walks it. Thirteen of the new tests fail against the old code; the online one
needs six players, because with three Piotrek holds only one slot and the bug
cannot appear.

HOW THE CARD SYSTEM WORKS
================================================================================

cards.json -> CardDef (immutable, shared) -> Card (one physical copy, has uid).
Decks are built by expanding "count" into that many Card objects.

A card may declare:
  "badge":  {"pawn": "żółty"|"rainbow", "sign": "+"|"++"|"-"|"--",
             "arrow": bool, "count": N}
            Purely visual. "count" is how many pawn dots are drawn, so a card
            that moves two pawns says so on its face (Plagiat!). NOTE: the
            prototype inferred badges by string matching the title. Never go
            back to that.
  "effect": {"type": "move_pawn",
             "target": "fixed"|"hindmost"|"choice",
             "pawn": "<pawn id>",         (only for target=fixed)
             "steps": 1, "direction": "forward"|"backward"}

engine/effects.py turns a card + state into a Plan (tuple of PawnRoute) or a
Refusal(reason). It is a PURE FUNCTION: the UI calls preview() while dragging
and the engine calls resolve() when applying, so the highlight and the outcome
cannot disagree. Card.is_playable is True when an effect exists and does not
need a choice.

A card may also declare:
  "ability": {...}   an activated ability (character cards, Piotrek's skills)
  "uses": N          how many times it may be used.  The description still says
                     "2x" for humans; this is the number the rules use, and the
                     counter lives on the Card (uses_left), so it travels with
                     the physical card.
  "passive": {...}   always-on modifiers granted by holding the card
                     (ChatGPT: movement_cards_delta -2, chest_limit 1)
  "presentation": {...}  how the card announces itself when drawn
                     (Gamechanger's role_reveal)
  "on_draw": {...}   an effect resolved THE MOMENT THE CARD ENTERS A HAND,
                     through the same registry as "effect".  Troll and Stańczyk
                     use it.  Not run by the opening deal — see "locked".
  "locked": true     the card may not be played or discarded BY HAND.  The
                     engine refuses both commands, so a client that simply does
                     not draw the lock is still told no.  A locked card is
                     unlocked by whatever armed it (an on_draw interrupt), and
                     is therefore NEVER dealt at setup — CardDef.opens_a_hand
                     derives that from "locked" rather than trusting the JSON,
                     because a locked card in an opening hand can never be
                     played, discarded or resolved and costs its owner a hand
                     slot for the whole game.  Stańczyk shipped that way for
                     one commit; test_no_locked_card_can_be_dealt_at_the_start
                     is what stops it happening again.
  "art": "<name>"    OPTIONAL Signature Card artwork.  A NAME, not a path, and
                     almost never needed: leave it out and the name is derived
                     from the TITLE, so ``assets/card_art/Troll.png`` illustrates
                     Troll with no JSON at all.  Set it when the file cannot be
                     named after the card, or when a title is shared by two
                     decks ("Shady" is a Mod AND a Chest card — scope it as
                     ``"chest/shady"``).  ``"art": ""`` or ``"art": false`` is
                     the OPT-OUT: this card keeps its parchment face even if a
                     matching file exists.  NOT the same field as ``image``.
  "in_opening_hand": false
                     keep the card out of the opening deal for a reason OTHER
                     than being locked.  Troll declares it explicitly as well,
                     because the rule is its own ("nobody begins the game
                     holding a Troll") and should not silently depend on the
                     lock.

ADDING A NEW DETERMINISTIC CARD = one JSON entry. No Python.
See HOW THE EFFECT ENGINE WORKS above for anything more involved.


================================================================================
TURN INTERRUPTS, FORCED PLAYS AND MULTI-SELECT  (stage 19)
================================================================================

Four movement cards — Troll, Stańczyk, Spy, Plagiat! — needed four things the
effect engine could not yet express. None of them was implemented as a special
case; each became a general mechanism, because the Chest cards and Patus Mods
still to come will want the same four.

READ THIS BEFORE IMPLEMENTING ANY CHEST CARD. Most of what a Chest card wants
to do already exists here.


A CARD THAT ACTS WHEN IT IS DRAWN
---------------------------------
`_after_draw` used to know about Gamechanger BY NAME. That was already one
branch too many and Troll and Stańczyk would have made it three, so a card now
declares `"on_draw"` and `effects.resolve_on_draw()` sends it through the same
registry, the same Plan and the same executor as a card that is played.

Draws can cascade — Troll draws its own replacement, and the replacement can be
another Troll — so `GameState._draw_depth` bounds the chain at MAX_DRAW_CHAIN.
Every draw in the game now goes through `_draw_one()`, which owes the same
debts wherever it came from: reshuffle when the pile runs out, report the draw,
run `on_draw`.


A CARD THAT TAKES OVER A TURN
-----------------------------
StatusKind.TURN_INTERRUPT. The status CARRIES ITS OWN EFFECT SPECIFICATION in
`data["effect"]`, plus `data["card_uid"]` so it can point back at the physical
card that started it. `GameState._begin_turn()` runs at the start of every turn
and asks the registry to resolve whatever the status is carrying — so it knows
nothing whatever about Troll or Stańczyk.

A future card that hijacks a turn is therefore JSON and no Python at all.

Two things can take a turn, in this order:

  SKIP_TURN       — the seat simply loses its move.
  TURN_INTERRUPT  — a card takes the turn over and says what to do with it.

IMPORTANT, and the reason Stańczyk needed almost no new code: SKIP_TURN has
existed since stage 4 and until stage 19 NOTHING READ IT. Lubin and Dziubdziuch
both granted it and both were silently doing nothing. If you find a status
nobody consumes, assume the same.

Both consume the turn: the hand refills by the ordinary rule and play passes
on, exactly as if a card had been played. That is why the end-of-turn draw
lives in `_begin_turn` and not inside the operations — an interrupt IS a turn,
and a turn ends the same way whoever spent it.

The recursion is real (a skipped turn hands on to a seat that may also be
skipped) and bounded by MAX_TURN_INTERRUPTS. A table where every seat is
interrupted for ever is a content bug and must not become a hang.

`_begin_turn` is called from `_end_turn` AND from `_set_active_player`. Both:
a turn handed over directly in edit mode is still a turn beginning, and without
the second call a seat holding a Troll could be given the turn and keep it.

GrantStatus(..., stack=True) queues rather than replaces. The default replaces,
which is right for nearly everything — using an ability twice should refresh a
freeze, not queue a second one — but a second Troll is a second hijacked turn.


FORCED PLAY (Troll)
-------------------
`ForcedPlay` is an Operation, not a handler decision, because choosing the card
needs the seeded RNG and A HANDLER MUST NEVER CONSUME RANDOMNESS — the UI calls
handlers to preview while dragging, so a handler that rolled a die would change
what the card does every frame. Same reason PlayRandomCard is an operation.

The executor picks with `self.rng.choice()` from a list SORTED BY UID. Hand
order is not something two replicas are obliged to agree on, and relying on it
would desync the moment one of them differed.

`priority_decks` then `fallback_decks` is the whole of Troll's rule ("a Chest
card if you have one, otherwise a Movement card") expressed as data.

A card whose effect is unimplemented, or which would need a decision nobody can
be asked for mid-execution, is played, discarded, and does nothing. That is the
rule and not a shortcut: a forced play must NEVER be able to stop the game.


LOOKING INTO ANOTHER HAND (Spy)
-------------------------------
`steal_card`. Only cards of the named deck are ever offered, which is both the
rule and the secrecy — Piotrek's Chest cards stay face down.

A hunter always robs Piotrek (there is one of him and he is worth robbing);
Piotrek picks which hunter, because from his side they are interchangeable and
the choice is the interesting part.

The end-of-turn draw needs NO special case, and it is worth understanding why
before "fixing" it: the thief holds `wanted` movement cards including Spy,
plays Spy (`wanted - 1`), steals one (`wanted`), so `_refill_movement_hand`
draws nothing of its own accord. The victim is one short and `DrawCards`
replaces it. Both halves of the spec fall out of the existing rule.


MULTI-SELECT, AND ORDER (Plagiat!)
----------------------------------
`Choice` gained `count` and `ordered`. More than one turns the prompt into a
multi-select with a confirm button that enables at EXACTLY `count`; `ordered`
makes the interface number the picks ①②, and clicking a picked one removes it
and renumbers the rest.

The answer travels as the ids joined by commas in the existing
`PlayCard.choices` dict — `Dict[str, str]`, unchanged, survives JSON — rather
than as a new command field. `effects.split_ids` / `join_ids`.

`move_pawns` is parameterised by count, distance and direction, so "move three
pawns one forward" is a JSON entry and no code.

THE HARD PART, and the reason `MoveBySteps` exists: the second pawn departs
from a board the first one has already rearranged. It may have been carried
along inside a tower. `MovePawn` carries a finished route, which is correct for
one pawn and wrong for two, so `MoveBySteps` has the EXECUTOR recompute the
route against the board as it is when that operation's turn comes. Stacking,
towers, widened rows and the walk animation then need no special case at all,
because by then it is an ordinary move of a pawn that really is where the
engine thinks it is.

The one thing that cannot wait is the 12a/12b question — nobody can be asked
halfway through applying a plan — so the handler works out the destination
POSITION (which is pure, via `MoveProjection`) and asks in advance, one keyed
question per pawn (`pawns_tile0`, `pawns_tile1`) so two answers cannot
overwrite each other. `MoveBySteps.preview_tiles` is the handler's projection,
for the board highlight only; the executor never reads it.


NEW OPERATIONS (engine/effects.py, all registered in `_OPERATIONS`)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  MoveBySteps        move a pawn, routed at execution time (above)
  DrawCards          draw into a hand through the ordinary draw path
  TransferCard       move one card between hands (Spy)
  HighlightHeldCard  point at a card for N seconds; no state behind it
  ForcedPlay         choose a card from a hand and play it (Troll)
  TurnLost           report that a seat got no move out of this turn

NEW EFFECT TYPES: `turn_interrupt`, `skip_turn`, `forced_play`, `steal_card`,
`move_pawns`.

NEW EVENTS: CardDrawEffect, CardSpotlighted, CardStolen, TurnSkipped.


THE SPOTLIGHT, AND WHY THE ENGINE STILL DOES NOT WAIT
-----------------------------------------------------
Troll and Stańczyk must show the chosen card for two or three seconds BEFORE
anything appears to happen. The engine does not wait for it and must not (see
rule N36). The state changes at once; the PICTURE is staged, exactly as
CardRevealed already did for Gamechanger:

  `CardSpotlighted` carries `seconds`, and the game screen both holds the card
  up (RevealOverlay, with a halo) and sets `BoardView.walk_delay`, which is
  added to every walk started while it is counting down.

So the pawn moves after the card has been shown, while the engine finished the
whole thing in one command. If you ever need this again, reach for walk_delay
rather than making the engine sleep.


MULTIPLAYER
-----------
NO NEW COMMANDS WERE ADDED. Every one of these mechanics replays from the
commands that already existed, which is why none of them can desync:

  Troll / Stańczyk  resolve inside whatever command ended the previous turn.
  Spy / Plagiat!    are ordinary PlayCard with `choices` filled in.

The rules that keep it that way, all of which already existed:

  - The forced-play RNG is the seeded game RNG, consumed in the same order on
    every replica, from a uid-sorted list (above).
  - Spy's hidden information rides on N40: ChoiceRequired is delivered to the
    peer that asked and to NOBODY else. The card titles in that question are
    the opponent's hand, so this is load-bearing — do not broadcast it.
  - CardStolen names the seats, the uid and the deck. NEVER the title: it goes
    to the whole table and only the thief was shown the hand.
  - Reconnect: TURN_INTERRUPT is a Status, so it is already in the snapshot and
    already restored. This is exactly why N18 says new mechanics become a
    StatusKind rather than a boolean on GameState.


================================================================================
HOW THE MOD PATUSA SELECTION WORKS
================================================================================

Added in stage 21.  This is the mechanic that puts mods into play; before it,
mods only ever reached the rack by being played from a hand or drawn by
Thunderfuck, neither of which is how the physical game does it.


THE SCHEDULE
------------
A round is a MOD ROUND when

    round >= mod_round_first  and  (round - mod_round_first) % interval == 0

Defaults are first=3, interval=2, so rounds 3, 5, 7, 9 — "every 2 rounds
starting on round 3".  Both numbers are SessionConfig fields set in the lobby
(local menu and the network host screen), carried in LobbyState, and accepted
by Room.set_settings.  `GameState.is_mod_round()` answers the question and
`next_mod_round()` says when the next pause is due; the mod rack heading uses
the second one to show "następny wybór: runda 5".

Setting mod_round_first to 10_000 is how a test opts out.  Several suites do —
see "TESTS AND THE PAUSE" below.


WHAT HAPPENS ON A MOD ROUND
---------------------------
`_begin_round` calls `_open_mod_selection`, which deals THREE cards to each
faction (RULES.mod_choices) and parks a `ModSelection` on
`GameState.pending_mod_selection`.  Six cards leave the deck at once; the four
losers are returned when their side settles, so nothing is lost from the deck.

While that object exists the table is PAUSED: `_mod_selection_refusal` refuses
every _TURN_BOUND command with "Najpierw wybierzcie Mody Patusa", from both
`_authorise` (local) and `authorise_remote` (the host).  It is in the engine and
not in the interface for the usual reason — a client that simply does not draw
the overlay must still be unable to play.

ChooseMod and VoteMod are deliberately NOT in _TURN_BOUND.  They are how the
pause ends, and voting is not a move — the same reasoning that keeps
KeepChestCards out of that tuple.


PIOTREK'S HALF
--------------
Three cards, seen on Piotrek's machine only.  He clicks one; it becomes the
LEFT active mod (slot 0) and the other two are discarded.  One command:

    ChooseMod(player_index, card_uid)

The engine checks the uid against the candidates IT dealt, so a client cannot
name a card it was never offered, and refuses a second choice.


THE HUNTERS' HALF
-----------------
Three different cards, and every hunter votes:

    VoteMod(player_index, card_uid)

  - one vote per hunter — the command REPLACES that seat's entry rather than
    being refused, which is what makes changing your mind work;
  - a vote may be changed until the last hunter has voted;
  - all hunters see all votes, so the tally is public and lives in the snapshot;
  - each ModVoteCast carries the WHOLE tally, so no two screens can disagree
    about the count — the engine owns it and a screen that recomputed it would
    be a second opinion.

When every hunter has voted the winner is the card with the most votes.  ON A
TIE THE LEFTMOST TIED CARD WINS.  That falls out of `max` over the cards in
DEALT order, because `max` keeps the first maximum it meets and the dealt order
is the on-screen order.  Do not "fix" that into a sort — the tie-break is the
iteration order.

The winner becomes the RIGHT active mod (slot 1); the other two are discarded.


SLOT LOGIC — READ THIS BEFORE TOUCHING THE RACK
-----------------------------------------------
There are now TWO ways a card reaches the rack and they behave DIFFERENTLY:

  SELECTION      writes its slot directly (`_settle_mod_side`).  Piotrek owns
                 slot 0, the hunters own slot 1, for the rest of the game.
                 It must NOT push, or Piotrek choosing would shunt the
                 hunters' card along the rack.

  PLACE / THUNDERFUCK  push through `_install_mod`: into slot 0, everything
                 shifts right, whatever falls off the right is discarded.

`_install_mod` used to take a `prefer_free_slot` flag for Thunderfuck, which
filled the first EMPTY slot instead.  That put a new mod on the right while the
left one stayed put — the opposite of the rule — and it was invisible until the
rack was half full.  The flag is gone; do not reintroduce it.


THUNDERFUCK
-----------
Movement card, three copies (was one).  Text: "Dobierz nowy Mod Patusa".

  - NO active mods  → does NOTHING.  The card is played and discarded normally.
    This is deliberate: Thunderfuck REPLACES what is in play, and before the
    first selection there is nothing to replace.  It must not seed the rack
    with a mod nobody chose.
  - ANY active mod  → draw one mod; new card to LEFT, old LEFT to RIGHT, old
    RIGHT discarded.  Works on anybody's turn.

Implemented as the `draw_into_mods` effect → `DrawIntoMods` operation →
`_op_draw_into_mods`, which gates on `any(self.mod_slots)` and then hands the
card to `_install_mod`.


EVENTS
------
    ModSelectionStarted    the round paused; carries both candidate lists, the
                           Piotrek seat and the hunter seats
    ModVoteCast            one vote, plus the whole tally and voted/voters
    ModSelectionResolved   one faction settled: faction, slot, uid, title,
                           the discarded uids, and whether a tie was broken
    ModSelectionFinished   both settled; the table may move again


THE SNAPSHOT
------------
An open selection IS in the fingerprint: candidates, votes and the two done
flags.  Two machines that disagree about who has voted disagree about which mod
is about to be in play.  It carries UIDS, not titles, so nothing in the
fingerprint says what Piotrek was offered.


THE INTERFACE
-------------
One overlay, `ModChoice` in ui/overlays.py, serves both factions — they are the
same picture with a different rule underneath.  It is built on the card-picker
geometry (`mod_choice_*` in layout.py) so three mods look like three chest
cards rather than a fourth kind of card lineup.  Vote ticks sit in each card's
upper-right corner, the running count under it.

`GameScreen._sync_mod_overlay` decides which half this machine is looking at,
FROM THE STATE rather than from the event that got there — so a client
replaying several commands in one frame, or reconnecting mid-selection, lands on
the right side.  A machine is shown whatever it can still act on: Piotrek's
cards while he has not picked, then the hunters' while a seat it owns has not
voted, and a read-only "waiting" view otherwise.

HOT-SEAT.  Edit mode controls every seat, so the overlay walks one person
through both halves: Piotrek picks, then the panel hands the same player the
vote, and `_voting_seat()` routes each click to the next hunter who has not
voted.  Getting this wrong is not cosmetic — the first version left the panel
sitting on Piotrek's spent cards and the selection could never finish.

Unlike the chest limit the pause is NOT modal.  `_handle_mod_choice_event`
returns whether it consumed the event, and anything that is not a click on one
of the three cards falls through to the board — a hunter waiting on four other
votes can still pan and zoom.

SINCE STAGE 44 it is nonetheless the TOP of the modal stack whenever it is
open, above the chest limit and everything else a round change can raise.
"Not modal" means the wheel and the middle drag reach the board; it does NOT
mean another window may take a click.  A chest limit dealt in the same round
stays PENDING underneath — drawn, unanswerable — and becomes the input owner
by itself the moment the selection resolves.  See THE MODAL STACK.

    tests/test_turn_progression.py   make()   mod_round_first=10_000
    tests/netkit.py                  seated() mod_round_first=10_000 unless
                                              a test passes mods=True
    tests/test_abilities.py          the random-reveal test builds its own game

================================================================================
HOW THE MOD PATUSA EFFECTS WORK  (stage 24)
================================================================================

A mod in the rack changes the rules for as long as it is there.  That is what a
``passive`` is — the same field ChatGPT uses to shrink Piotrek's hand — so the
rules are DECLARED IN cards.json:

    Speedrun     {"reverse_backward": true}
    Masa solna   {"movement_cap": 1}
    Halloween    {"require_neighbour": true}
    Sesja na PG  {"abilities_locked": true}          + variant 2 (stage 34)
    AKO          {"carry_neighbour": true}           + variant 2 (stage 35)

A VARIANT'S passive is a declared rule exactly as the printed card's is, and
``test_every_declared_mod_rule_has_a_reader`` walks both.  A rule that exists
only on a card's second reading is just as unimplemented if nothing reads it,
and easier to miss.

``GameState.mod_rule(key)`` scans the rack; ``movement_cap``,
``abilities_locked``, ``requires_neighbour`` and ``reverses_backward_moves``
are named wrappers.  NOTHING IN THE ENGINE ASKS A MOD WHAT IT IS CALLED, and
nothing may start: card titles are content, not code.  A future mod that caps
movement is a JSON entry and no Python at all.

The LEFT slot wins if two mods ever disagree.  Nothing declares a conflicting
pair today; the rule exists so both machines resolve it the same way rather
than by dictionary order.


WHICH EFFECTS THE MODS APPLY TO
-------------------------------
Masa solna and Halloween are rules about MOVEMENT CARDS.  They must not touch:

  * character abilities — Dziad's ability is an ordinary ``move_pawn`` and
    would otherwise be caught by both;
  * chest cards — none has a movement effect today, and when one does it must
    not silently inherit the movement deck's restrictions.

``EffectContext`` carries ``origin`` ("card" / "ability" / "on_draw") and
``deck_id`` for exactly this, and ``ctx.from_movement_card`` is the test.


``can_ask`` — READ THIS BEFORE ADDING A MOD THAT ASKS ANYTHING
--------------------------------------------------------------
Seks z pedałami and Troll's forced play choose a card from
``resolves_without_asking``.  That is a property of the PRINTED card, computed
from its spec, and it CANNOT know that a mod has just given the card a question
to ask.  A mod that opens a prompt therefore breaks both cards: they pick a
card that looks self-resolving, it returns a Choice, nobody is there to answer,
and the card fizzles with "bez efektu".

Those two call sites pass ``can_ask=False``.  A handler that would ask must
fall back to something legal instead.  Speedrun declines the reversal, which is
always legal because the mod only ever OFFERS one.


THE ORDER OF THE QUESTIONS IS PART OF THE RULES
-----------------------------------------------
    1. Speedrun's direction
    2. which pawn (if the card asks)
    3. which half of a widened row (if the destination is doubled)

Speedrun is resolved at the TOP of ``_move_pawn``, before ``_movement_target``.
It can be: the card's printed direction is all it needs.  Asking it after the
pawn would mean the player choosing a pawn to move backwards and only then
being told it could go forwards.  Do not "tidy" it back down next to the other
step handling.

Only ``direction: "backward"`` asks.  ``direction: "either"`` does NOT — those
cards already let the player choose the way, and a second question about the
same decision would be asked and answered twice.

Plagiat! moves backwards through ``_move_pawns``, a SEPARATE handler, and gets
the same treatment there.  Any rule added to one handler has to be added to the
other or the two paths drift apart.


HALLOWEEN AND THE FIZZLE
------------------------
A pinned pawn does not refuse the card.  The card is played, discarded, the
hand refills and the turn passes; only the movement does nothing.

That cannot be an empty Plan.  ``Plan.ok`` is ``bool(self.operations)``, so an
empty one reads as a REFUSAL everywhere and the card would stay in the hand.
The idiom is Thunderfuck's, which has had the same problem since stage 21: emit
a REAL operation whose executor does nothing.  Here that is ``Fizzle`` →
``_op_fizzle`` → ``MoveFizzled``, which also tells the player why, because a
card that silently did nothing looks like a bug.

Neighbour means the position immediately ahead or behind is occupied.  Sharing
a field is NOT being a neighbour: a tower is one field.

**THE CAMP IS ONE CLUSTER.  This is a decision, not a reading of the rule, and
it is load-bearing.**  Every pawn starts in the camp at ``CAMP_INDEX``.  Taken
literally nobody there has a neighbour, so Halloween reaching the rack while
the field is still waiting — which ``mod_round_first: 1`` allows — would freeze
the board for the rest of the match: nothing moves, nobody reaches the finish,
no tower is ever built, so NEITHER FACTION CAN WIN.  Pawns waiting shoulder to
shoulder in the camp are neighbours of one another.  If the owner wants the
literal rule instead, the deadlock has to be solved some other way first.

On a multi-pawn card each pawn is judged against the board THAT move sees, via
``MoveProjection.positions``, because an earlier move can give a later pawn its
neighbour or take it away.


SESJA NA PG PRESERVES CHARGES
-----------------------------
Refused in ``_use_ability`` BEFORE anything resolves, so ``spend_use()`` is
never reached and the remaining uses survive the mod untouched.  In the engine
and not only the interface: a client that does not grey the button must still
be unable to act.

The button greys exactly the way a spent ability does (``enabled=False``) but
reads "SESJA NA PG", not "ZUŻYTE" — locked is not spent, and the caption must
not say something false about charges that are coming back.


THE DECK AND ITS COMPOSITION
----------------------------
Thirteen cards.  Every title appears ONCE in cards.json and copies come from
``count`` — never repeat the entry.

``SessionConfig.mod_counts`` overrides the counts by title and
``DeckDef.with_counts()`` builds the result.  Two properties are load-bearing:

  * an ABSENT title keeps its printed count, so an empty mapping means "the
    real deck".  That is what every existing test sends, and what a client on
    an older build sends;
  * the CARD ORDER stays the JSON's.  A shuffle is a permutation of a list, so
    a list assembled in the mapping's iteration order shuffles differently on
    two machines from one seed.  Do not rebuild it from the mapping.

``clamp_mod_counts`` (config/settings.py) is shared by the config, the lobby
and the server so all three agree what a payload means, and it returns the
pairs SORTED because the mapping travels inside the lobby snapshot clients
compare against the host's.

``room.set_settings`` MERGES incoming counts rather than replacing them: the
panel sends only the titles it knows about, and a replace would let a host on
an older build empty the deck.

A deck too small to deal ``mod_choices`` cards to both factions simply does not
pause the round — ``_open_mod_selection`` already declined on an exhausted
deck, and the panel warns before it gets that far.


THE PANEL
---------
``ui/settings_panel.py`` (was ``mod_counts_panel.py`` until stage 26), shared by
the hot-seat menu and the network host screen.  ONE deck needs ONE place to
describe it.  It has four tabs now — see "THE SETTINGS PANEL" below.

It is an overlay, not eight more settings rows, and that is not cosmetic: both
screens lay themselves out by measuring rows and shrinking the gaps until they
fit, and stage 21 already records a FIFTH row pushing the Start button off a
1280×760 window.  Eight more do not fit at any gap on either screen.  If you
are tempted to inline them, run tests/test_menu_layout.py at 1280×760 first.

Two things it gets right that are easy to get wrong again:

  * the opening button sits BESIDE the Mod Patusa row, not under it.  Under, it
    lands on the "pola podwójne" label at 1280×760.
  * the steppers are MEASURED and placed by their right edge.  ``Stepper``
    sizes its buttons to its own labels at the current type scale, so any
    hard-coded inset is wrong at some window size.

Both are pinned by tests in tests/test_mod_counts_ui.py.


================================================================================
THE THREE MODS THAT ACT AT A MOMENT  (stage 25)
================================================================================

Paczka, Squid Game and Shady are NOT pure passives, and that is the only thing
that makes them different from the stage 24 four.  A passive answers a question
about the rack as it is now.  These three act at a MOMENT: Paczka when it
arrives, Squid Game at the start of every round after it arrives, Shady when it
arrives and again a round later.

``GameState.armed_mods`` maps a mod's card uid to the round it entered the
rack, and ``_sync_mod_states()`` computes the TRANSITIONS in and out of it.
Call it from anything that changes ``mod_slots`` — the selection, PlaceMod and
Thunderfuck all do.  A fourth way into the rack that forgets to would silently
skip every arrival.

THE DEPARTURE HALF IS NOT OPTIONAL.  A mod that leaves must leave NOTHING
behind: removing Shady puts its pawn back immediately (otherwise replacing it
mid-round strands a pawn nowhere for the rest of the match) and removing Squid
Game drops the pending check and restores ordinary checking.  The rack is the
only thing keeping any of these rules alive.

Arming is keyed on the declared ``passive``, never on the title (N98).


PACZKA, AND THE ONE SANCTIONED BREACH OF N81
--------------------------------------------
``ChestCardsRevealed`` carries card TITLES to the whole table.  N81 forbids
exactly that, and this is the ONE place allowed the exception: the rule exists
because normally only one player is entitled to look, and here the card IS the
entitlement — Paczka's whole text is that the Chest is public.  Any OTHER event
carrying Chest titles is still a bug.

Every machine builds the same list from its own replica and shows its own
window; each player dismisses their own with OK.  Nothing is synchronised
because nothing changes, and no command is logged.

The window is a LIST, not a row of card faces — six players holding two cards
each is twelve faces and no lineup fits at 1280×760 — but it is built from the
card picker's furniture so it reads as the same family of window.  It declares
``blocks_keyboard`` in the modal stack so Esc closes it rather than opening the
pause menu, and it consumes only the input that dismisses it.

IT DOES NOT OPEN DURING A MOD SELECTION (stage 44).  ``_arm_mod`` used to fire
``ChestCardsRevealed`` the moment ONE faction settled, putting a window in
front of four hunters who still had to vote.  A passive listed in
``GameState._MOD_FOLLOWUP_PASSIVES`` is one whose arrival opens a WINDOW; while
a selection is open the arrival is recorded on ``ModSelection.followup_uids``
and ``_finish_mod_selection`` replays it — ``ModSelectionFinished`` first, the
window after.  It is rebuilt FROM THE RACK, so a mod chosen and then replaced
before the pause lifted owes nothing.  ``followup_uids`` is in the snapshot.

A mod that only changes the board (Shady) or the statuses (Sesja na PG variant
2) is NOT one of these and still lands the instant it arrives.  Outside a
selection — PlaceMod, Thunderfuck, Rage Quit — nothing waits.

THE GENERAL RULE: a secondary window whose meaning depends on a phase being
complete must not interrupt that phase.  Add the passive to
``_MOD_FOLLOWUP_PASSIVES`` and the window to ``_mod_followup_events``; do not
invent a second deferral mechanism.


SQUID GAME: WHO IS CHECKED IS PUBLIC, WHAT IT MEANS IS NOT
----------------------------------------------------------
THE SPLIT IS THE WHOLE DESIGN, and getting it wrong is either a desync or a
client deciding a winner.

  ARMING   ``_arm_lead_check`` runs in ``_begin_round`` on EVERY machine.  The
           single furthest pawn is public information, so every replica names
           the same colour and stores it in ``pending_lead_check``.
  JUDGING  ``victory.review`` runs on the AUTHORITY ONLY and turns that colour
           into the existing ``EliminatePawn`` or ``DeclareVictory``.  It needs
           the hidden colour, which is why it cannot be anywhere else.

NO NEW COMMAND.  The verdict travels the road every other verdict travels, so
logging, broadcast, replay, reconnection and the fingerprint keep working.

``pending_lead_check`` is cleared by the COMMAND that settles it, in
``_eliminate_pawn`` and ``_declare_victory``.  Not by the code that armed it:
the authority would clear its own copy and every replica would go on waiting.

The other half of the card is that the ORDINARY check is switched off — while
``lead_check_only`` is true, ``review`` never looks at a gathering tile at all.
Both halves read the same rule so they cannot get out of step.

A SHARED LEAD IS SKIPPED, NOT TIE-BROKEN, and the skip is announced: a round
where nothing happens is otherwise indistinguishable from a broken mod.  A
colour already crossed off is not checked twice.  Piotrek reaching the meta
still wins — this mod replaces CHECKING, not the other ending.


SHADY: ONE PAWN, IGNORED BY FIVE THINGS
---------------------------------------
``StatusKind.HIDDEN`` on the pawn (N18), carrying the riders and the round, so
snapshot, serialisation and reconnection are free.

The pawn taken is the BOTTOM of the furthest OCCUPIED FIELD.  That is a
different question from Squid Game's and has its own function: the lead mod
skips a shared lead, Shady is told what to do about one.

Once hidden it is ignored by ``hindmost``/``foremost`` (one filter in
``_ordered_pawns``), by ``pawn_options``, by ``MoveProjection.positions`` and
by ``has_neighbour`` — all four go through ``effects.is_hidden`` /
``live_pawns``, so "ignore it" is one decision rather than six.  A card that
names it anyway RESOLVES, is discarded and does nothing: ``Fizzle``, the
Halloween idiom (N99), in ``_move_pawn``, ``_move_pawns`` AND ``_stack_pawn``
(N104).

ON SCREEN IT DISAPPEARS THROUGH ONE OMISSION, in ``BoardView._draw_order``.
That list is what gets painted AND what ``token_at`` hit-tests, so the pawn
stops being drawn and stops being clickable in the same line.
``BoardView.forget_pawn`` drops its animation state, or it slides the length of
the board when it reappears somewhere it has never been.

CHECKING COUNTS THE PAWNS ON THE TABLE, not the palette — ``visible_pawns`` —
which is the whole of the exception.  Five onto one field while a pawn is away,
six again once it is back.

Restored at the START OF THE NEXT ROUND, on top of the rearmost pawn, before
anything else in ``_begin_round`` sees the board.  It does NOT go back where it
came from; that is the point of the card.  ONE-TIME: it stays on display and
never fires again, because arming is a transition.  A SECOND Shady is a new
arrival and does hide a pawn.

THE BRIEF'S ARITHMETIC.  The brief says checking normally needs "all five
remaining pawns" and four while Shady is active.  The palette has SIX pawns, so
that reads as off by one; it is consistent if the count is pawns stacked ONTO A
BASE PAWN (five onto one is all six, four onto one is all five), which also
matches the brief's own "since one pawn is absent".  Do not "fix" the code to
literal fives and fours.


================================================================================
HOW THE CHEST CARD EFFECTS WORK  (stage 27)
================================================================================

Four of the eight Karty Skrzyni do something now.  READ THIS BEFORE ADDING THE
OTHER FOUR — most of the machinery they will want is already here, and two of
the four below exist only because a Chest card needed something the movement
deck never did.

A CHEST CARD IS NOT A MOVEMENT CARD, and that is load-bearing rather than
incidental.  Masa solna, Halloween and Gambit Patusa are all gated on
``ctx.from_movement_card`` (N103), so none of them touches anything below by
construction.  A mod that shortens the movement deck must not silently shorten
the Chest as well, and there is a test for it.

None of the four added a command, a message type or a snapshot field.  They are
ordinary ``PlayCard``s whose decisions ride in ``choices``, which is why none of
them can desync and why reconnection needed nothing.


DZIECKOROLKA — THE CARD THAT MADE WIDENED ROWS A DECISION
---------------------------------------------------------
``move_and_collect`` → the ``MoveAndCollect`` operation.  Three fields forward,
taking the TOP pawn off every field walked THROUGH.

**Every widened position on the route is a question.**  This is the one place
D8a's shortcut does not apply.  Everywhere else an intermediate 12a/12b is
settled by taking the nearer half, because nothing depends on where a pawn
merely passed; here WHICH HALF IS WALKED DECIDES WHICH PAWN IS SWEPT, so each
one is asked separately.  The keys are ``branch0``, ``branch1``, ``branch2``,
numbered by STEP along the route rather than by position, so they are stable
across resubmissions and no answer can overwrite another.  The destination is
asked the same way rather than through the usual ``tile`` key — from the
player's side it is the same question.

**THE ORDER OF THE FINISHED TOWER IS THE RULE.**  A tile's ``stack`` is stored
BOTTOM FIRST, and the tower read DOWNWARDS from the mover has to be the journey
in order.  So, onto whoever was already standing on the destination:

    1. the collected pawns in REVERSE travel order, last met lowest;
    2. the mover;
    3. anything that was riding on the mover when it set off.

``MoveAndCollect.collected`` is in TRAVEL order and the executor reverses it.
That single reversal is the whole subtlety.  The brief's example — green
through blue-with-pink-on-top, then red, landing on yellow — is
``[yellow, red, pink, green]`` bottom-first.

This is not cosmetic.  **THE HUNTERS WIN BY CHECKING THE PAWN AT THE BOTTOM OF A
TOWER**, so two machines with the same pawns in a different order disagree about
who wins the match.  The multiplayer test asserts the whole board, not the
positions.

Collected pawns go UNDERNEATH the mover; riders stay ABOVE it.  Two different
relationships to the same pawn, and getting them the same way round puts a
passenger below the pawn carrying it.

Taking the top can never break a tower — nothing is standing on it.  A field
whose top pawn is FROZEN therefore yields NOTHING rather than the sweep reaching
past it: "always take the top pawn" is the rule and digging underneath one would
take a tower apart.

Each collected pawn gets its OWN ``TokenWalked`` over the TAIL of the route,
because it joins part way along.  Putting it in the leader's ``carried`` would
send it backwards to the start of the route first — riders share every waypoint.


RAGE QUIT
---------
``replace_mods`` → the ``ReplaceMods`` operation.  Thunderfuck's neighbour, and
deliberately NOT Thunderfuck's operation: that one PUSHES a card in from the
left and lets the rack shift, which is right for "draw a new mod" and wrong for
"replace the ones in play".  Each occupied slot is written IN PLACE, the way
``_settle_mod_side`` does, because the two factions own one slot each for the
rest of the game (N85).

**THE DRAWS HAPPEN BEFORE THE DISCARDS.**  A deck whose draw pile has run dry
reshuffles its discard pile, so returning the outgoing mods first lets the card
hand back the very cards it was played to be rid of.

An EMPTY RACK does nothing at all and says so (N86, N99): the card exchanges
what is ACTIVE, and before the first selection there is nothing to exchange.  An
empty SLOT stays empty for the same reason — the rack must never be seeded with
a mod nobody chose.

``_sync_mod_states`` afterwards is not optional (N106/N107).  Two mods leave and
two arrive in one command.


BALBINKA
--------
``move_all_pawns``, built out of ``MoveBySteps`` with two new flags.

**NOBODY IS CARRIED** (``carry_riders=False``, defaulting True so Plagiat! is
untouched).  The tower rule would move a rider twice — once inside its tower and
once in its own right — and the card says two fields, not four.

That makes the ORDER load-bearing:

  * going FORWARD the furthest pawn moves first, going BACKWARD the rearmost
    does, so a pawn never lands on a field whose occupant has not yet left;
  * within one field the BOTTOM pawn moves first, so a tower arrives in the
    order it left.

Only pawns already sharing a field can converge — two pawns a field apart stay a
field apart when both move the same distance — so those two rules are the whole
of it, except at the finish and the start where movement clamps and the pawn
behind ends up on top.  That is ordinary stacking doing what it always does.

**THE RANDOM HALF IS ROLLED BY THE EXECUTOR**, never the handler (N78):
``MoveBySteps.random_branch`` → ``GameState._random_half``, from ``state.rng``.
A die rolled in a handler would be re-rolled by every preview frame while the
card was being dragged.  Only the DESTINATION is randomised; intermediate halves
keep the nearer-half rule and consume no randomness at all, which is also what
keeps the RNG in step across a table where nobody is crossing a widened row.


GAMBIT PATUSA, AND WHY A ROUND IS NOT A NUMBER OF TURNS
-------------------------------------------------------
``reverse_movement`` → ``StatusKind.MOVEMENT_REVERSED``, on the TABLE.

Piotrek takes every third slot, so ROUNDS DIFFER IN LENGTH and
``Status.expires_after_turn`` — which is measured in turns — cannot express
"next round, for one round".  The status therefore carries a round NUMBER in its
payload and ``StatusTracker.movement_reversed_in(round)`` compares it.
``_begin_round`` retires the ones already behind through
``_expire_round_statuses``, and ``_set_round`` walks every round it crosses, so
a jump cannot skip the retirement either.

Granted with ``stack=True``.  Replacing would let a Gambit played DURING a
reversed round cancel the reversal it was played under — they are separate
promises about separate rounds.

Applied in ``_move_pawn`` AND ``_move_pawns`` (N104).

**SPEEDRUN ASKS ABOUT THE EFFECTIVE DIRECTION.**  A backward card under a Gambit
is already travelling forwards, so offering to turn it round would be offering
to undo the Gambit while describing it as undoing the card.  The order of the
questions is now:

    1. Gambit Patusa            (silent — a fact about the round)
    2. Speedrun's direction     (only when the EFFECTIVE direction is backward)
    3. which pawn
    4. which half of a widened row

and the sign is settled as: distance → Masa solna's cap → Gambit's flip →
Speedrun's ``abs()``.  ``direction: "either"`` still never asks Speedrun.


A DEPARTURE IS ABOUT THE CARD THAT LEFT
---------------------------------------
Stage 27 found this through Rage Quit and it is worth keeping.
``_sync_mod_states`` used to ask *"does anything still hide?"* rather than *"did
THIS mod leave?"*.  Rage Quit can replace a Shady with a Shady — there are two
in the deck — and when it did, the first pawn stayed off the map FOR THE REST OF
THE MATCH while the new arrival took a second one.  A stranded pawn cannot be
moved, checked or won with.

The ``HIDDEN`` status now records ``mod_uid`` and ``_restore_pawns_hidden_by``
returns the pawns of departed mods BEFORE the arming loop, so a replacement
picks its target from a complete board.  The blanket restore stays as the
catch-all for a status recorded before the mod was noted on it.

The bug was PRE-EXISTING — Thunderfuck could reach it too — and it is N107 with
the wrong question asked.  The next mod that holds something will have the same
shape.

GEJTOS, ALTER EGO, AND CARDS WITHOUT RULES YET  (stage 28)
================================================================================

EVERY CARD NEEDS AN EFFECT, EVEN AN EMPTY ONE
---------------------------------------------
``Card.is_playable`` returns ``definition.effect is not None``.  A card with no
``effect`` field CANNOT BE CLICKED — it sits in the hand refusing to be played
or discarded, and at the chest limit that means a player holding two undesigned
cards is holding two dead cards.

So a card whose rule is still settled at the table declares ``manual``.  It
shows, it resolves to nothing, it goes to the discard pile, and its printed text
reaches the status bar so the table knows what to do about it.

``manual`` returns a ``Fizzle``, NOT an ``Announce``.  ``_op_announce`` emits
``ActionRejected``, and ``_play_card`` reads a rejection as "the play did not
happen" and leaves the card in the hand — which is the exact bug ``manual``
exists to fix.  ``Fizzle`` is the idiom for *played, did nothing, said so*
(N99).  This was written the wrong way round first and a test caught it.


GEJTOS
------
``gejtos`` → the ``TransferStack`` operation.  Mężczyzna pulls both
neighbouring fields onto the chosen pawn; Kobieta pushes them one field further
away.  Mirror images, so they share one handler.

**THE OPTION IS ASKED FIRST.**  Kobieta needs a destination beyond each
neighbour and Mężczyzna does not, so settling the option after the pawn would
change what the widened-row questions mean halfway through a resubmission.

``TransferStack`` is deliberately NOT built out of the movement system.  The
pawns are not walking a route — a tower is picked up whole and put down — so
there is no distance, no direction, no widened row to settle on the way and
nothing for a Mod Patusa to shorten.  It reads ``Tile.stack`` into a list BEFORE
placing anything, because that list is live and walking it while placing out of
it skips every other pawn.

**THE CHOSEN PAWN NEVER MOVES.**  It is the anchor the rule is measured from,
and its own field is not a question: the pawn is STANDING on it, so which half
of a widened row it occupies is a fact.  Only neighbours ask.

**KOBIETA REFUSES RATHER THAN CLAMPING** when pawns would go before field one.
Every other backward move in the game clamps, and that is right for a card that
says "move back".  Here the card names the case, and a card that silently did
three quarters of its rule would be worse than one that would not be played.
Past the FAR end it does clamp, because the card does not name that edge and
every other rule in the game clamps there.


ALTER EGO, KINGMAKER, AND HOW TO TOUCH THE HIDDEN IDENTITY
------------------------------------------------------------
READ THIS BEFORE GOING NEAR THE SECRET.  It is the most delicate thing in the
project.  Alter Ego (stage 28) built the machinery; Kingmaker (stage 46) reused
it without changing a line of it, which is what the rest of this section is
really evidence for.

The hidden colour is the one fact the server has and the clients do not (N71,
N73).  Alter Ego makes it change hands mid-match.

**THE HANDLER NEVER TOUCHES THE COLOUR, AND CANNOT.**  It runs on every replica
to build the plan, and every replica but the authority's and Piotrek's own holds
``None`` for the secret all match.  A handler that read it would build a
different plan on a machine that knows it than on one that does not, and the
table would split on the one card that must not split it.

The flow, and every step of it exists for a reason:

  1. ``swap_identity`` → ``RequestIdentitySwap``, which raises a COLOURLESS
     public flag (``identity_swap = "revealing"``) and stops the table.
  2. The AUTHORITY answers through ``victory.review`` — the same hook that
     decides an elimination, and for the same reason (N72) — returning
     ``RevealIdentity``.  Carrying the colour there is safe because by the time
     it is broadcast it is not a secret: Piotrek has given it up.
  3. ``RevealIdentity`` wipes the notepad down to that one colour, clears the
     secret, and moves the flag to ``"choosing"``.
  4. The room re-sends ``identity_required`` to Piotrek's peer alone, minus the
     colour he just gave up.  SAME MESSAGE, SAME OVERLAY — nothing new was
     built on either side, which is what "reuse the existing UI" means here.
  5. ``set_identity`` accepts the second choice and issues
     ``FinishIdentitySwap``, so every replica leaves the pause on the same
     command rather than each guessing that the answer arrived.

**THE OLD CROSSINGS GO.**  They were evidence about an identity that no longer
exists, and the rule turns on it: Piotrek may move to a colour the hunters had
already ruled out, which is only possible because ruling it out is void.  What
survives is the colour he just left.

``eliminated_pawns[-1]`` IS the "which colour may he not pick" answer.  There is
deliberately no second copy of it to fall out of step.

**BETWEEN THE REVEAL AND THE CHOICE THERE IS NO HIDDEN IDENTITY AT ALL**,
including on the authority.  ``victory.review`` reads exactly that and declines
to judge, which is what stops a tower being checked against nobody mid-swap.
The pause itself goes through ``_phase_refusal`` — the one gate the opening
pause already used — rather than a second one.

``identity_swap`` is in the snapshot because the table is STOPPED and every
machine has to agree that it is.  It never names a colour, which is what lets a
replica hold the same value as the authority.


KINGMAKER: THE ROLE CHANGES SEATS  (stage 46)
---------------------------------------------
The other face of the same physical card.  Piotrek's copy is Alter Ego and
keeps the role where it is; a hunter's copy is Kingmaker and MOVES it, and the
new Piotrek then picks a colour through the five steps above, unaltered.

THE ROLE IS THE CHARACTER CARD AND NOTHING ELSE.  ``Player.role`` is derived
from ``character.is_piotrek`` — ``players/roles.py`` exists so the question is
asked once — so the whole of the role swap is:

    piotrek.character, challenger.character = challenger.character, piotrek.character

and every rule that reads the role moves with it, none of them touched:
``piotrek_seat``, ``seat_order`` (the every-third-slot cadence), the chest
limit, the Mod Patusa factions, the win conditions, ``swap_identity``'s own
"only Piotrek" refusal, the right-hand panel, the ability button and the
hunter notepad.  If you are ever tempted to add a "who is Piotrek" flag, this
is the reason not to: there is one fact and everything reads it.

THE TWO OPERATIONS ARE ORDERED, AND THE ORDER IS THE DESIGN::

    Plan((SwapPiotrekRole(hunter_seat=actor.index), RequestIdentitySwap()), ...)

The role moves FIRST.  Raise the pause first and ``victory.review`` answers it
about the man who is about to stop being Piotrek, and ``ask_for_new_identity``
sends ``identity_required`` to the wrong peer.  Because the role has already
moved, both of them read ``state.piotrek_seat`` and both find the new owner —
which is why neither ``victory.py`` nor ``server/room.py`` needed a line.

THE SECRET IS MOVED WITHOUT BEING READ.  ``SwapPiotrekRole`` swaps
``secret_pawn`` between the two seats.  On a replica that is ``None`` onto
``None``; on the authority and on the outgoing Piotrek's own machine it is the
real colour.  The operation never NAMES a colour, so it builds identically
everywhere (N72), and the colour it moved is given up a moment later by
``RevealIdentity``.  IT IS NEVER INHERITED — the new Piotrek chooses his own,
and the one just published is refused him by ``swap_forbidden_pawn``.

WHAT TRAVELS WITH THE ROLE:

  character   yes — it IS the role.
  skill       yes — the Umiejętność Piotrka belongs to the Piotrek seat.  The
              hunter had none, so the outgoing Piotrek correctly ends with
              none.  The CARD is handed over rather than a fresh one dealt,
              because uses are counted on the physical card (base_card.py).
  marks       NO.  The notepad is the player's own working-out.  Swapping it
              would put one player's private deductions on another player's
              screen — from the one card whose whole job is to move hidden
              information about carefully.  This is a rule, not an omission.

WHY WHO-PIOTREK-IS IS PUBLIC AND THE COLOUR IS NOT.  These are two different
facts with two different protections, and conflating them is the mistake to
avoid here.  The turn order announces Piotrek every round, so the role moving
is ordinary shared state and rides the ``play_card`` command every replica
replays.  The colour has never been in a command and still is not.

``piotrek_seat`` IS IN THE SNAPSHOT, and had to be added for this.  Nothing
else in the fingerprint notices the role moving: ``piotrek_name`` is a
character TITLE and the titles in play are identical before and after,
``has_character`` is true on both seats either way, and ``ability_uses`` is
keyed by seat.  Two machines that disagreed about who Piotrek is used to agree
about every field in the fingerprint.

NO UI WAS WRITTEN, and none should be.  ``CharacterPanel`` branches on
``player.is_piotrek``, so the identity badge, the skill card, the Umiejętności
deck section, the ability button's source and the hunter pawn grid all follow
the role by themselves.  A "Kingmaker panel" would be a bug.

NOT UNDOABLE.  A checkpoint holds hands, piles, charges and a listed set of
scalars; it does not hold who has which character card, and ``secret_pawn`` was
never in it either.  ``SwapPiotrekRole`` closes the turn window outright, and
``can_undo`` additionally refuses while ``awaiting_identity`` — which also
closes the same hole under Alter Ego, where an undo mid-swap already lost a
secret that had been given up.

THE REVEAL IS A RULE OF THE CARD.  Its printed text says the identity is
revealed (``tożsamość Piotrka jest odkryta``), so the exchange goes through
``RevealIdentity`` and the notepad resets to the published colour exactly as
under Alter Ego.  A silent exchange would need a second way of giving up an
identity, which is the one thing this design exists to avoid.  If the rule at
the table turns out to be a silent swap, that is the decision to revisit and
the tests name it.

THE SETTINGS PANEL  (stage 26)
================================================================================

``ui/settings_panel.py``, class ``GameSettingsPanel``, used by BOTH settings
screens.  Four tabs: Karty ruchu, Mody Patusa, Karty Skrzyni, Umiejętności.

A tab is a ``SettingsTab`` RECORD, not a subclass: where its rows come from,
what bounds them, what its numbers add up to, and what is wrong with them.
Nothing else differs between the four, so a FIFTH CATEGORY IS ONE ENTRY in
``_build_tabs`` and no new drawing code.  Every title and default is read from
the loaded data — never a list in this file.

THREE THINGS THAT ARE EASY TO GET WRONG HERE:

  * THE STEPPERS ARE PER VISIBLE ROW, not per title.  The movement deck is
    thirty titles and no window is tall enough, so the list scrolls and the
    steppers are zipped against ``visible_titles``.  Indexing them by a title's
    position in the deck edits the wrong card.
  * THE ABILITY TAB IS CHARGES, not copies, and it lists BOTH ability decks —
    a Piotrek skill is an ability with uses exactly as a character's is.  Cards
    with no ability are excluded, because a charge counter on a card that can
    never spend one is a control that does nothing.
  * ``Domyślne`` RESETS THE VISIBLE TAB ONLY, and the button sits under the tab
    it undoes.  One button that silently discards four tabs of work is the kind
    of thing noticed once.

Buttons are placed by their BOTTOM edge against the panel's, not by a top edge
a fixed distance up from it: ``Button.fit`` sizes itself to its caption at the
current type scale, and at 1280×760 a 49 px button hung past a panel that had
budgeted 47 for it.  This is N46 in a new costume.

THE NUMBERS UNDERNEATH.  ``SessionConfig`` carries FOUR mappings —
``movement_counts``, ``mod_counts``, ``chest_counts``, ``ability_uses`` — as
separate fields rather than one nested one, because ``mod_counts`` is already
on the wire and in a dozen tests.  All four mean "as printed" when empty.
``DeckDef.with_counts`` and ``with_uses`` apply them BEFORE the shuffle, and
both keep the JSON's card ORDER for the reason stage 24 records: a shuffle is a
permutation of a list.  ``clamp_title_map`` is the one clamp with three thin
wrappers, and ``room.set_settings`` merges the three newer mappings in ONE loop
— four hand-written merges differing only in their clamp is how one of them
quietly stops merging.


================================================================================
HOW SCALING WORKS  (stages 26 and 29)
================================================================================

THERE ARE THREE SCALES, NOT ONE.  Until stage 28 a single ``ui_scale``
multiplied everything, which is a zoom control, not a responsive layout: a
1920x1200 laptop was simply a 2560x1440 desktop at 83%, the board kept its
share, the hand kept its share, and the movement cards became narrow strips of
unreadable type.  All three live on ``Layout`` and are set in ``resize``:

    ui_scale     GENERAL GEOMETRY.  Anything that is a distance rather than a
                 letter.  clamp(window_height / 1000, 0.85, 1.8) — unchanged
                 since stage 26, including the reference of 1000 rather than
                 1080 (at 1080 a 1920x1200 laptop ran at 1.11 and was reported
                 as hard to read while 2560x1440 was comfortable at 1.33).

    type_scale   WHAT FONTBOOK IS SET TO.  Equal to ui_scale at and above the
                 reference display; below it, decays MORE SLOWLY, so a caption
                 keeps its absolute size while the furniture gives ground.

    panel_scale  THE SIDE COLUMNS ONLY.  Decays FASTER than ui_scale.  A deck
                 pile is reference material — legible, but nobody reads it the
                 way they read the card they are about to play.

Plus a fourth case that uses NONE of them: type on a card is a fraction of the
CARD.  See "CARD TYPE" below.


WHICH SCALE DOES A NUMBER BELONG TO?
------------------------------------
Ask this about every pixel number before writing it.  Getting it wrong is what
stages 26 and 29 both had to fix.

  a distance, a gap, a radius, a button ....... ui_scale
  a band that exists to hold a line of text ... type_scale
  a side-column floor or inner margin ......... panel_scale
  anything drawn on a card face ............... the card's own height


THE REFERENCE DISPLAY IS 2560x1440, AND IT MUST NOT MOVE
--------------------------------------------------------
``REFERENCE_WINDOW``.  Every responsive rule collapses to the stage-28
behaviour there: ``compact`` is 0, ``type_boost`` is 1, and type_scale and
panel_scale both equal ui_scale.  This is the owner's own monitor, and a "fix"
that redesigns it is not a fix.  ``test_the_reference_display_is_untouched``
writes the expected numbers out LITERALLY rather than recomputing them, because
a formula that is wrong in the same way in the test and in the code agrees with
itself.  If you change a responsive rule, that test is the one that tells you
whether you changed the desktop too.


BREAKPOINTS, AND WHY THE PIXELS STILL INTERPOLATE
-------------------------------------------------
``BREAKPOINTS`` is keyed on ``room = min(w/2560, h/1440)`` — the axis that fell
furthest behind the reference.  WIDTH IS IN THERE ON PURPOSE: 1920x1200 has 83%
of the reference height but only 75% of its width, and it was the width that
squeezed the hand.

    wide     room >= 0.95   2560x1440 and up — the reference layout, untouched
    medium   room >= 0.66   1920x1200, 1920x1080 — the laptop band
    compact  room >= 0.00   1600x900 down to MIN_WINDOW

The NAMES are for decisions — a rule that is either on or off.  The PIXELS
interpolate continuously through ``compact`` (0 at the reference, 1 at
``TIGHT_WINDOW`` and below), because a window being dragged across a breakpoint
must not make the board jump.  Every adaptive number goes through
``Layout._lerp(roomy, tight)``, so "what changes on a smaller screen" is one
readable pair of numbers at the call site rather than a formula to decode.


THE PRIORITY ORDER WHEN SPACE RUNS SHORT
----------------------------------------
The movement cards are what the player READS, so they are protected and
everything else is a source of pixels for them, in this order:

  1. the BOARD gives up height — it is the only region with height to give;
  2. the SIDE COLUMNS shrink (panel_scale, tighter width caps, and the
     panel-card ceiling falling from hand_h*0.78 to hand_h*0.62);
  3. MARGINS AND GAPS shrink (pad, section_gap, r_gap);
  4. the HAND shrinks last — and in fact GROWS, its share of the window
     running 0.245 at the reference to 0.300 when tight.

Step 2's falling ceiling is not cosmetic.  ``hand_h`` now grows its share on a
small screen, so leaving the panel cap at a fixed 0.78 would have handed the
side columns the very pixels the hand had just won.

The board is repaid in WIDTH what it gave up in height: at 1920x1200 it goes
from 1281x642 to 1328x618, which is the same area to within 0.2%.  Doing all
four priorities rather than only the first is what buys that.


VERTICAL_ROOM: ADAPTATION HAS TO BE PAID FOR OUT OF SOMETHING
-------------------------------------------------------------
``compact`` is usually decided by the WIDTH.  But giving the hand a bigger
share spends HEIGHT, and a 760-tall window has none to spend: every pixel the
shelf takes there comes straight out of a side column already down to four
stacked cards.  So the hand's share increase AND the type boost are both
tapered by ``vertical_room`` (0 below 800px tall, 1 at 1200 and above).

This is the rule that makes the scheme coherent rather than a pile of tuned
constants.  AT MIN_WINDOW THERE IS NO SLACK ANYWHERE: the menu rows are already
at ``MIN_ROW_GAP`` and the panel bands at their floor, so lifting type there
does not make anything more readable — it pushes captions out of boxes that
cannot grow.  The smallest window leans on ``MIN_HAND_CARD_W`` instead, which
is what a floor is for.

``TYPE_BOOST_MAX`` bounds how much bigger type may get RELATIVE to the
furniture around it.  Type has to win space on a small screen or it is not
readable, but a box quoted in ui_scale can only absorb so much before its
caption touches the edges.


CARD TYPE — NEVER QUOTE IT AGAINST THE INTERFACE SCALE
------------------------------------------------------
This was the whole of the stage 29 report.  ``CardRenderer._font`` sized type
as a fraction of the card's HEIGHT — and the card's height already tracks
ui_scale, because a bigger window makes a bigger card — and then
``FontBook.get`` multiplied by ui_scale AGAIN.  **Card type moved with the
interface scale SQUARED.**  Between 2560x1440 and 1920x1200 that turned a 17%
smaller card into 32% smaller type printed on it, so descriptions collapsed
into stacks of two-word lines.  The same line broke 4K the other way: type so
large it crowded the description out.

``_font`` now divides the font scale back out and anchors the ratio to
``CARD_TYPE_ANCHOR`` (= 1.44, the scale at the reference display).  The
arithmetic cancels exactly there, and:

    A CARD OF A GIVEN PIXEL SIZE NOW RENDERS IDENTICALLY ON EVERY MONITOR.

That invariant is worth protecting — it means a card face can be reasoned about
from its size alone.  ``CARD_TYPE_ANCHOR`` (render) and ``TYPE_ANCHOR`` (ui)
must stay equal; they live in packages that must not import each other, so a
test asserts it.

CARD TITLES SHRINK TO KEEP A WORD WHOLE.  ``Renderer.wrap_lines`` breaks
mid-word when a word cannot fit — right for a paragraph, wrong for a title.
"Thunderfuck" rendered as "Thunderfuc" over "k" at every resolution.
``_title_font`` steps the size down until the longest word fits, to three
quarters, past which the mid-word break is allowed again because an unreadable
whole word is not a win.


NEVER GIVE A CARD OR A SHELF A FLAT PIXEL CEILING
-------------------------------------------------
``hand_h`` was capped at 300 and the hand card at 260, so a 4K screen showed
exactly the same 182x260 card as a 1920x1200 laptop and every pixel past ~1224
tall became margin.  Both ceilings are multiplied by ``ui_scale``.

The same mistake in a different box caused BOTH bugs stage 26 had to fix: a
label band capped at a flat 26 px while the type inside it kept growing, and a
deck heading with no idea how much room its counters had left it.  If a box
holds text, either the box scales or the text is fitted to the box — see
``Renderer.fitted_font`` / ``fit_text``.  Stage 29 moved those bands onto
``type_scale``, which is the same rule one step further out.

ENLARGED PREVIEWS ARE ALREADY CORRECT and were verified rather than changed:
``CardRenderer.quantised`` has no ceiling, so a hovered card is REPAINTED at its
enlarged size rather than zoomed from the small one.  That is stage 9's work;
do not add a cap to it.


A TEST CAN PASS BECAUSE OF A BUG — CHECK IT AT THE REFERENCE DISPLAY
-------------------------------------------------------------------
``test_an_enlarged_card_is_redrawn_not_zoomed`` demanded a 1.25 ratio of MEAN
edge energy between a redrawn card and a zoomed one, and passed for eight
stages.  Measured under the old code at 2560x1440 it scores 1.089: IT WOULD
HAVE FAILED ON THE OWNER'S OWN MONITOR.  It passed only because it hardcoded
1920x1080 and one card size, where the double-scaling made the small card's
type about 8 px and zooming it produced mush.  The test was measuring the bug.

The mean averages over the whole card, most of which is flat parchment.  What
enlargement destroys is the PEAK, so it now measures 99th-percentile contrast
(1.96-2.28 everywhere) and is parametrized over four card sizes and four window
sizes.  If a visual test has one hardcoded size in it, run it at the others
before trusting it.


================================================================================
HOW SIGNATURE CARDS WORK  (stage 30 — read before touching CardRenderer.face)
================================================================================

A SIGNATURE CARD is a card with its own full-card artwork.  Every other card is
a STANDARD card and is painted exactly as it was before this existed.  The
whole feature is one branch on the first line of ``CardRenderer.face``:

    Card ──> art.surface(definition)
               │
               ├── None ──────> the parchment face (unchanged since stage 29)
               └── a Surface ─> _signature_face()

THE POINT IS THAT IT IS OPTIONAL.  The owner illustrates cards ONE AT A TIME,
over months.  A change that converts every card, or that makes an unillustrated
card look even slightly different, has missed the requirement entirely.


WHY face() IS THE RIGHT PLACE AND THE ONLY PLACE
------------------------------------------------
Every card drawn anywhere in the game goes through ``face()`` — the hand fan
via ``draw_transformed``, every panel via ``draw_in``, the overlays, the
RecentlyPlayed preview.  Branching there means NOTHING ELSE had to learn about
artwork.  If you find yourself adding a second entry point, or an ``if
card.has_art`` in ui/, the branch has been put in the wrong place.


THE REVEAL, AND WHY IT DEFAULTS FROM ``highlighted``
----------------------------------------------------
``face(..., reveal=None)``.  0 is the resting state (artwork + title low), 1 is
the hovered state (artwork darkened, title lifted, description under it).

``None`` derives it as ``1.0 if highlighted else 0.0``.  That default is doing
real work: every panel and overlay in the game ALREADY passes ``highlighted``,
so all of them got both states without a single edit.  Only ``hand_fan``, which
owns a smooth ``slot.hover`` of its own, passes a float — which is what makes
the transition glide rather than snap.

Reveal is QUANTISED to ``REVEAL_STEPS`` before it reaches the cache key, for
the same reason ``SIZE_STEP`` exists: a continuous 0..1 would paint a new face
every frame of the hover.


THE TWO STATES ARE ONE LAYOUT
-----------------------------
Do not position the title twice.  The title, divider and description are laid
out as ONE BLOCK anchored to the bottom margin, and the resting title position
is that same block with the description removed:

    resting_top = h - bottom_pad - title_h
    opened_top  = h - bottom_pad - desc_h - 2*gap - title_h
    title_top   = lerp(resting_top, opened_top, reveal)

So the title rises by EXACTLY the height of the description appearing under it,
at any card size, for any length of text, with no tuned offsets that will be
wrong on a monitor nobody tested on.  This is the same reasoning as stage 29's
``_lerp(roomy, tight)``: one readable pair of numbers, not a formula to decode.


EVERYTHING ON THE FACE IS A FRACTION OF THE CARD
------------------------------------------------
``SIGNATURE_TITLE_FRACTION``, ``SIGNATURE_TEXT_FRACTION``, the scrim heights,
the outline thickness — all of the card's HEIGHT, never of ``ui_scale``.  This
is stage 29's invariant and it now covers the new face as well:

    A CARD OF A GIVEN PIXEL SIZE RENDERS IDENTICALLY ON EVERY MONITOR.

``test_a_card_of_a_given_size_renders_identically_on_every_monitor`` pins it
across six font scales.  If you add a number to ``_signature_face``, it goes
through ``_font`` or it is a fraction of ``h``.  Nothing else.

LONG POLISH RULES TEXT IS CUT, NOT SPILLED.  ``_description_font`` shrinks the
type and then TRUNCATES the lines that still do not fit.  Below about 110x157
the font floor in ``_font`` takes over and shrinking stops helping; without the
truncation the description ran off the bottom of a panel card.  This is what
``Renderer.draw_wrapped`` already does to a standard card's body.


NOTHING IS DRAWN ON TOP OF THE ARTWORK  (stage 31)
--------------------------------------------------
Inside its own border a Signature card is the picture, the title and the
description.  That is the whole list.

Two things used to be painted over it and were removed:

  * the INSET BRASS RULE, one pixel in from ``max(3, h*0.028)``, which read as
    a scratch across an illustration rather than a frame around it.  It was
    drawn in three places — ``face``, ``_signature_face`` and the reveal
    overlay's own card — and all three went at once.  ``theme.card_frame`` was
    retired with them, so putting the line back means adding a colour first.
  * the STATUS PIPS, ``_playable_marker`` and ``_locked_marker`` in
    ``ui/hand_fan.py``: a green circle for "the engine can resolve this" and a
    pale one for "locked".  Both sat ten pixels below the card's top edge — on
    Troll, exactly on the gem the artwork draws there.

The pips were DISPLAY ONLY.  Nothing hit-tested them and no rule read them, and
both questions they answered are still answered by the engine: ``is_playable``
still gates ``_activate``, ``GameState`` still refuses to play or discard a
locked card, a drag still colours the card's own border from
``effects.preview``, and clicking a locked card still says so in the status
bar.  If an anticipatory cue is ever wanted back, put it anywhere except the
card face.


THE ARTWORK IS COVER-SCALED, NEVER STRETCHED
--------------------------------------------
``_cover`` scales by the LARGER of the two ratios and crops the overflow.
Fitting would letterbox the picture and show the parchment it was supposed to
replace; stretching would distort it.  Both are explicitly forbidden.


HOW A FILE FINDS ITS CARD  (render/card_art.py)
-----------------------------------------------
Filename and card title are both put through ``slugify`` and compared, so
``Troll.png``, ``troll.PNG`` and ``TROLL.jpg`` are one name, and
``Stanczyk.png`` finds "Stańczyk" (the ``ł``/diacritic fold is explicit —
Unicode will not decompose ``ł`` for us).

TITLES ARE NOT UNIQUE ACROSS DECKS.  "Shady" is both a Mod Patusa and a Chest
card and they are different pictures.  A file in a subfolder named after a deck
is scoped to it and tried first; a bare name that two subfolders both claim is
AMBIGUOUS and is dropped rather than guessed at.  Do not "simplify" that into
first-match-wins — it resolves differently on different filesystems.

Three states of ``CardDef.art``, and the third one matters:

    None   derive the name from the title  (the default, and almost always right)
    "x"    use exactly that name
    ""     NO artwork, do not go looking   (the opt-out)

The opt-out exists because a test that builds a throwaway card out of a real
definition has to be able to strip the artwork the way it already strips
``image`` — ``test_a_two_pawn_badge_is_wider_than_a_one_pawn_badge`` builds on
``movement.cards[0]``, which is Troll, which has a picture and therefore no
badge strip at all.


THIS IS NOT N7
--------------
N7 forbids inferring card BEHAVIOUR from titles.  Matching a picture to a title
is not behaviour: the worst a rename can do is un-match the artwork, and an
un-matched card is a standard card — the same fallback a missing file gets.
Nothing about how the card plays can change.  Pin it with ``"art"`` where that
matters.  Do NOT extend this reasoning to anything the engine reads.


art IS NOT image
----------------
``image`` is a small illustration drawn INSIDE the parchment body of a standard
card, addressed by a path under ``assets/images``.  ``art`` REPLACES the face
and is addressed by NAME under ``assets/card_art``.  They are different
folders, different fields and different pictures.  A card may declare both, in
which case the Signature face wins and ``image`` is unused.


NOTHING HERE MAY RAISE
----------------------
The card-art folder is filled in by hand over months.  It WILL be incomplete,
one file WILL be a renamed .psd, and none of that may reach the frame loop.
Every failure path in ``card_art.py`` answers ``None`` and the card renders as
it always did.  ``load_image`` catches broadly ON PURPOSE — pygame raises
``pygame.error`` for a truncated PNG, ``FileNotFoundError`` for a file deleted
since the scan, and ``ValueError`` for a file whose extension lies — and the
answer to all three is the same one.  It is module level and card backs use it
too (stage 47): one loader with one failure policy, so a picture cannot become
able to crash the game by arriving through a different door.


TYPOGRAPHY
----------
Signature titles ask ``FontBook`` for a DISPLAY face and fall back to the bold
UI face when none is installed.  The project ships without one: dropping
``assets/fonts/Display-Bold.ttf`` in restyles every artwork card at once.  Do
not bundle anything proprietary — the project is non-commercial and
redistributed from GitHub.  Whatever goes in must cover Latin Extended-A or
Polish titles render as boxes.


================================================================================
CARD BACKS  (stage 47 — the other side of the card)
================================================================================

Every deck's back is a PICTURE in ``assets/card_backs``.  Five decks, five
files, and one table that says which is which:

    settings.CARD_BACKS = {
        DECK_MOVEMENT:  "movement.png",        # Karty Ruchu
        DECK_MODS:      "mods.png",            # Mody Patusa
        DECK_CHEST:     "chest.png",           # Karty Skrzyni
        DECK_SKILLS:    "piotrek_skills.png",  # Umiejętności Piotrka
        DECK_CHARACTERS:"characters.png",      # the character-EXCHANGE deck
    }

THAT TABLE IS THE WHOLE CONFIGURATION.  Replacing one back is overwriting a
file, or changing one line to point at a different one.  ``test_card_backs.py``
does exactly that and asserts the other four decks come out byte-identical, and
a second test greps ``render/`` and ``ui/`` for the file names to prove none of
them leaked into rendering code.  If you ever want to name a card-back file
outside ``settings.CARD_BACKS``, the answer is no — that is the whole feature.

A TABLE, NOT A SCAN.  ``card_art.py`` scans its folder and folds filenames to
card titles, because cards are numerous, arrive over months and are addressed
by a name the owner already knows.  Decks are five constants that will not
change, so a scan would have meant inventing a second name-folding convention
and making "which file is the chest back?" a question you answer by listing a
directory.  Do not "unify" the two systems on the grounds that both load
pictures.

``DECK_CHARACTERS`` IS A DECK.  Its back is the back of the character-changing
cards.  It is not a character, not a role, and says nothing about who anybody
is — a picture on a pile that every player can see cannot carry hidden
information, and nothing about HIDDEN INFORMATION is touched by any of this.

WHERE IT IS DRAWN
-----------------
One place, and it was one place before this stage too:

    ui/hud.py::_deck_section  ->  cards.draw_pile(..., deck_id=deck.id)
                              ->  draw_back(..., deck_id)
                              ->  CardRenderer.back(size, color, brightness, deck_id)

``_deck_section`` is shared by the left column (movement, mods, chest) and the
character panel (piotrek_skills, characters), so all five decks are covered by
that one call.  Nothing else in the game draws a card back: hands, the card
library, the reveal overlay and the mod rack all draw FRONTS through
``draw_in``/``face``.  If a new screen ever needs a back, it passes a deck id
and gets the right picture with no branch of its own — the same contract
``face()`` gives Signature Cards.

``back()`` BRANCHES ON ITS FIRST LINE, like ``face()``.  Picture present ->
``_picture_back``; otherwise the drawn cover it has always painted.  Callers do
not know which they got.

WHAT HAPPENS TO THE PICTURE, AND WHAT DOES NOT
----------------------------------------------
``_picture_back`` does three things and nothing else:

  1. COVER-SCALES through the same ``_cover`` a Signature face uses — cropped,
     never letterboxed and never stretched.  The shipped backs are ~1060x1490
     against a card's 1:1.43, so a few pixels come off the long edge.
  2. Rounds the corners to the card's own radius, so a back has the same
     silhouette as every other card.
  3. Adds ``BACK_HOVER_LIGHT * brightness`` to every channel for the deck-panel
     hover — ADDITIVE, because unlike the drawn back there is no base colour to
     ``lighten``.

NOTHING IS DRAWN OVER A CARD BACK.  No title, no count, no badge, no frame.
A back carries no state, which is why it needs none of ``_signature_face``'s
layout.  The counts live in the panel heading above the pile, where they were.

CACHING
-------
``_picture_back`` caches into ``_faces`` under ``("picture_back", deck_id,
size, step)``, and ``CardBackLibrary`` caches the loaded file per deck.  The
deck id is in the key because it is what chose the picture: without it the
movement and chest piles, identical in size, would share one surface.  A pile
draws three backs and the frame loop draws it sixty times a second; all of that
is one blit each and zero disk reads after the first.

THE DRAWN BACK IS NOW THE FALLBACK
----------------------------------
The bound-cover drawing — gradient, brass frame, corner diamonds, emblem, all
in ``theme.deck_colors[deck_id]`` — is still there and still tested, but it is
no longer what the game shows.  It is what a deck gets when its file is missing
or will not load, and what a clean checkout with no binary assets shows, which
is the project's standing rule for everything under ``assets/``.  Do not delete
it, and do not "simplify" ``back()`` by removing the colour parameter: the
fallback needs it, and ``theme.deck_colors`` is still doing four other jobs
(discard borders, reveal rings, the recently-played strip).

THE CARD LIBRARY  (stage 32 — read before touching deck quantities mid-match)
================================================================================

``ui/card_library.py``, opened by the BOOK in ``layout.card_library_button``.
Four tabs, three of which are table decks and one of which is not a deck at all.

WHAT IT DOES NOT CONTAIN, and every one of these is load-bearing:

  * no card renderer.  Every card goes through ``CardRenderer.draw_in(...,
    reveal=hover)``, so a Signature card is a Signature card here with no
    branch in this file.  Stage 30 put that branch on the first line of
    ``face()`` exactly so a new screen would not need one.  If you find
    yourself writing ``if entry.card.art`` in ui/, it is in the wrong place.
  * no card definitions.  The tabs are built from ``ContentLibrary``, so a card
    added to cards.json appears with no code change.
  * NO NUMBERS OF ITS OWN.  Every quantity drawn is read from the live
    ``GameState`` on the frame it is drawn, and every change leaves as a
    Command.  There is nothing here to fall out of step with the game.
  * nothing on a card face.  See N36d — the controls go UNDER the card.


WHAT A QUANTITY MEANS ONCE THE MATCH HAS STARTED
------------------------------------------------
The lobby's ``[-] n [+]`` sets how many copies are printed INTO a deck before
the shuffle.  From the first deal onwards those copies are spread across a draw
pile, a discard pile, several hands, the mod rack and possibly an open Mod
Patusa selection.  ``GameState.deck_card_count`` counts ALL OF THEM, so the
library's number and the lobby's number mean the same thing and cannot
disagree.  Counting the draw pile alone would have made the library contradict
the lobby the instant anybody drew a card.

``config.movement_counts`` and friends are NOT consulted and NOT updated.  They
are what the match was built from; they say nothing about what has happened
since.  The cards are the only honest answer, and mirroring them into a mapping
would be the two-sources-of-truth problem the feature exists to avoid.


EDITING A LIVE DECK, AND THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------
    +   a NEW Card with a fresh deterministic uid, inserted at
        ``deck.rng.randrange(len(draw_pile) + 1)``
    -   the DRAW pile first, the DISCARD pile second, and NOWHERE ELSE

A hand, the mod rack and an open selection hold cards that are out on the table
in front of somebody.  When every remaining copy is out there, ``-`` is REFUSED
rather than reaching into a hand.  Never "fix" that by taking the card back: it
deletes a card a player is holding, possibly one they are about to play.

NOT ON TOP OF THE PILE either.  A card conjured onto the top of the deck is the
next card somebody draws, which is a way to hand a chosen card to the next
player.  ``deck.rng`` has been advanced by exactly the same shuffles on every
machine, so a seeded position is agreed on without being predictable.

``_next_card_uid`` is one past the highest uid the deck currently holds.  A uid
from the global counter would differ on every machine, and a command that names
a card by uid would then name a different card on each of them.


DEFAULT USES vs CURRENT USES — THE DISTINCTION THE ABILITY TAB RESTS ON
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Both already existed; the library invented no storage:

    CardDef.uses     the configured DEFAULT.  Already the lobby's number,
                     because ``DeckDef.with_uses`` rewrote the definition
                     before the deck was built.
    Card.uses_left   the runtime counter, on the physical card, so it travels
                     with the card if it changes hands.

``RestoreAbilityUses`` is therefore one assignment of the first onto the second
and remembers nothing.  ``AdjustAbilityUses`` has a floor of zero and NO
CEILING — a table may deliberately give an ability more charges than it was
printed with, and a restore afterwards returns it to the PRINTED default, not
to the inflated number.  Do not add an upper bound; it is the requirement.

``GameState.ability_card(title)`` finds the one physical copy wherever it is —
on a player, or still in its deck — searching seats first and then decks, in a
fixed order, so every replica finds the same card.

The owner shown above each card is READ FROM THE DATA: a character card IS its
own ability card, so the owner is its title; one of Piotrek's skills belongs to
whichever character card declares ``"role": "piotrek"``.  Never type those
relationships into the interface.


AUTHORITY, AND THE FIELD THESE COMMANDS DELIBERATELY LACK
----------------------------------------------------------
    AdjustDeckCount(deck_id, title, delta)
    AdjustAbilityUses(title, delta)
    RestoreAbilityUses(title)

NONE of them carries ``player_index``.  They are consequently in neither
``_OWNED_BY_PLAYER`` nor ``_TURN_BOUND``, ``authorise_remote`` has no seat to
compare against, and ANY PLAYER MAY RESTORE ANY CHARACTER'S ABILITY AT ANY
TIME — which is the requirement.  Adding a ``player_index`` "for tidiness"
would silently restrict every one of them to its own seat.

``delta`` rather than an absolute count, so two players clicking ``+`` at the
same moment add two cards: the server applies both in order and neither is a
stale absolute overwriting the other's work.

The snapshot carries ``deck_composition`` and ``ability_charges``.  Both are
needed: the older ``decks`` entry holds pile SIZES, which would not notice two
machines adding copies of different titles, and the older ``ability_uses``
covers only cards that have been DEALT — the library can top up a character
nobody is playing.


"DOBIERZ KARTĘ": ONE NAMED CARD OUT OF ONE NAMED DECK  (stage 33)
------------------------------------------------------------------
    DrawTitledCard(player_index, deck_id, title)

Under every Movement / Mod / Chest card, never under an ability.  It exists so
a tester can get a particular card in front of them without drawing thirty
others first, and it is built out of things that already existed:

  * ``Deck.take_titled`` was already there for ``setup``'s character picks.  It
    gained ONE flag, ``include_discard``, and the default keeps setup's old
    draw-pile-only behaviour.
  * ``_draw_one``'s tail became ``_deliver_card``, and ``_draw_card`` and
    ``_draw_one`` BOTH go through it.  A card that arrives by name owes exactly
    the same debts as one off the top: reported as ``CardDrawn``, and it ACTS
    on the way in through ``_after_draw``.  Troll still draws its replacement
    when it is fetched by name.  Two routes into a hand that each did the
    arrival themselves is how one of them quietly stops running the other's
    rules — N104's lesson on a different pair of paths.

THE SEARCH IS SCOPED TO ONE DECK.  Card identity here is (deck_id, title); the
deck comes from the TAB the button sat under and only that deck is looked in.
No title is printed in two decks today, and the day one is, this still works.

The discard pile counts as part of the deck, because ``take_card`` reshuffles
it back in the moment the draw pile runs dry — refusing a card that is one
shuffle from being drawn anyway would be a lie told by an off-by-one pile.

NOTHING IS EVER FABRICATED.  No copy in the deck means ``ActionRejected`` and
an untouched hand.  This command carries a ``player_index`` — unlike the
library's other three — because it IS about a particular player, so the
existing ``_OWNED_BY_PLAYER`` check is exactly the authorisation wanted.  It is
NOT ``_TURN_BOUND``: fetching yourself a card to try is not a move.

It does not, and must not, move the library's QUANTITY.  That number is copies
in the match, and a card in a hand is still in the match.


THE GRID: FOUR ACROSS, AND WHY THE PANEL'S WIDTH FOLLOWS THE CARDS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
``card_library_panel`` is measured from the grid rather than taken as a share of
the window.  A share gives a content area so much wider than it is tall that
four or five columns always fit and the cards end up sized by the leftovers.
The card is sized against the viewport's HEIGHT — a card is tall, and a row must
leave room for the controls under it — and the panel is wrapped around three of
them, which makes "three big cards per row" a consequence rather than a number
written down somewhere.

Stage 33 retuned it to FOUR across — the owner wanted more of the collection
visible at once — by moving two numbers and letting everything else follow: the
card's height share and ``CARD_LIBRARY_COLUMNS``.  Measured: four columns at all
five reference resolutions, cards from 184x262 at 1280x760 to 336x480 at 4K.

``card_library_columns`` still MEASURES whether four fit rather than asserting
it, so a genuinely narrow window gets three or two instead of four overlapping
cards.

The height share runs BACKWARDS on purpose — a small window spends a bigger
share (0.50) than a large one (0.46).  At 1280x760 the flat share left a 154px
card, smaller than the one in the player's own hand, and a library card nobody
can read at rest is the thumbnail gallery the grid is explicitly not.  A big
window can afford to spend its height on more ROWS instead.

Scrolling is in PIXELS, not rows: a row here is a card and a card is most of the
viewport, so a row-quantised scroll would move the grid by more than a screen.
The grid is CLIPPED with ``surface.set_clip`` — the same call the board view
uses for its particles — because the row being scrolled into view is always half
outside the viewport and has to be cut off rather than left to paint over the
tab strip.  ``_visible_indices`` is the hit test as well as the painter's list,
so a card scrolled out of sight is out of reach and not merely invisible.

THE HOVER FOLLOWS THE CELL, not the card (stage 33).  Keying it to the card was
right while nothing sat under it; with 'Dobierz kartę' there, reaching for the
button dropped the reveal on the way down and the description flickered shut
just as the player went to act on it.  A cell is one entry.

STEPPERS ARE BUILT ON DEMAND from the cell they belong to, by the same function
the painter and the hit test both call.  The settings panel keeps one per
VISIBLE ROW for the same reason; this is that rule with a finer-grained scroll.


LIMITATIONS TO KNOW ABOUT BEFORE "FIXING" THEM
-----------------------------------------------
  * The library's commands are refused before the match begins and after it
    ends, because ``_phase_refusal`` gates every non-authority command and they
    are not exempt.  The book opens and its buttons do nothing while Piotrek is
    choosing a colour.  Exempting them is a one-line change IF the owner wants
    it — it was deliberately not invented.
  * ``-`` can refuse for a reason the player cannot fully see: the refusal names
    the card but not who is holding the last copies, because saying so would
    leak hidden information.
  * The scrollbar is drawn but not draggable (the wheel, the arrows and
    Home/End scroll).  Same bargain the settings panel makes.
  * The display cards are throwaway ``Card`` objects built from definitions and
    take uids from the global counter.  They are never submitted in a command
    and never enter a deck — commands address cards by TITLE.

NIE MASZ ROSJI  (stage 36 — read before touching the turn loop or _play_card)
================================================================================

    variant 1  "two_rounds"    two full rounds
    variant 2  "one_round"     one full round

One status (``StatusKind.MOVEMENT_VETO``), ``charges=1``.  The single use IS
the charge: the tracker drops a status whose last charge is spent, so nothing
has to remember that a veto was used.


A FULL ROUND IS NOT THE ROUND COUNTER
-------------------------------------
``_note_turn_completed`` runs at the top of ``_end_turn`` and strikes the
finishing seat off the veto's ``pending`` set.  Empty set = one full round.

``pending`` EXCLUDES THE OWNER, and that is what makes the owner's example
right: the round is up when the turn comes back to them, which is the moment
everybody else has had one.  It is also why Piotrek's second slot in a round
ends nothing — he holds every third slot, so "the round counter ticked" and
"everybody has had a turn" are different events on this table.  Do not
re-implement this on ``round_number``.


THE PAUSE — THERE IS NO ROLLBACK, AND THERE MUST NOT BE ONE
------------------------------------------------------------
``_play_card`` resolves the plan and then SETS IT ASIDE.  The card stays in the
hand, the board is untouched, ``_after_play`` is never reached.

    play → resolve → blockers? → HOLD → accept → replay the same command
                                     → block  → discard, never run it

A blocked movement therefore cannot have built a tower, triggered an identity
check or won anybody the game: it did not happen.  Anything that "performs the
move and undoes it afterwards" is a regression of the whole design.

Accepting replays the ORIGINAL command through the ordinary path;
``_resolved_movement`` stops the replay being held a second time by the very
veto that let it through.  Every other command is refused while a decision is
open — the table is genuinely stopped.


WHO MAY BLOCK
-------------
``are_opponents(a, b)`` is ``is_piotrek != is_piotrek``.  Read from the roles
the game already has; no character title appears anywhere in this feature.

BLOCKABLE = a played card from the MOVEMENT or CHEST deck whose RESOLVED PLAN
contains a movement operation (``effects.plan_moves_pawns``).  Asking the plan
rather than the card means a card that gains movement later is blockable the
day it does.  A character ability, a Mod, and a dragged pawn are none of them
played cards coming through ``_play_card``, so all three are outside by
construction rather than by a list.


THE CLOCK BELONGS TO THE AUTHORITY
-----------------------------------
    engine   how LONG the window is        (config, snapshot, everywhere equal)
    authority WHEN it started              (wall clock, one machine only)

``Room.expire_decisions()`` on the hub tick online; ``LocalSession.tick(now)``
hot-seat, with ``now`` passed IN so nothing depends on a frame rate and nothing
sleeps.  ``ExpireMovementDecision`` is AUTHORITY_ONLY.  A client's countdown is
a PICTURE of that command arriving — if it reaches zero early the buttons stop
responding and the table waits, which is correct.


THE AUTOMATIC FINAL BLOCK
-------------------------
``_veto_is_last_chance`` simulates the rest of the effect's life against the
real cadence and asks whether any LATER turn belongs to an opponent while the
effect is still alive.  If none does, the block fires with no window: the card
promised one block, and this was the last movement it could ever stop.


SEVERAL VETOES
--------------
Every eligible opponent is a blocker.  The movement runs only when EVERY
blocker has accepted — one hunter's acceptance must not spend Piotrek's chance
— and the FIRST block the authority applies wins; the second finds nothing to
answer.  One authority applying commands in order is the entire mechanism; no
client ever cancels anything locally.


THE INTERFACE OWNS NOTHING
---------------------------
``ui/movement_decision.py`` reads the state every frame and answers with
Commands.  Whether there is a decision, who may answer it and whether an answer
is still valid all come from ``GameState``.  The confirmation dialog draws the
card with the ordinary ``CardRenderer.draw_in`` and the ordinary reveal, so a
Signature card is a Signature card there too.

The cooldown is a row in the settings panel's "Zasady" tab — a number per TABLE
rather than per card.  In the panel and not as another row on the two setup
screens, for the 1280x760 reason recorded in stage 21.

AKO: THE PAWN THAT COMES ALONG  (stage 35 — read before touching movement)
================================================================================

    variant 1  "with_stack"   {"carry_neighbour": true}
    variant 2  "alone"        {"carry_neighbour": true,
                               "carry_neighbour_alone": true}

Read by ``GameState.carries_neighbour`` / ``carries_neighbour_alone``, which are
``mod_rule`` wrappers like every other.  Nothing asks the mod what it is called.

WHERE IT JOINS THE PIPELINE: LAST.  The order of the questions is already fixed
(Gambit Patusa → Speedrun's direction → which pawn → which half of a widened
row); AKO is appended after all of them, because the passenger is a detail of a
move that has already been decided.  The mover's operation goes into the plan
FIRST so the companion walks into a field the mover has already left.

Joining after the distance is settled is what makes it compose: Masa solna
shortens the companion too, a ChatGPT bonus stretches it, Gambit and Speedrun
decide its direction.  Do not move the call earlier to "tidy" it.

``ako_companion()`` IS CALLED FROM BOTH MOVEMENT HANDLERS — ``_move_pawn`` and
``_move_pawns`` — because a rule added to one and not the other drifts, and the
difference only ever shows on the one card that takes the other path.  On the
multi-pawn path it is keyed per move (``pawns_ako0``…) and judged against
``MoveProjection.positions``, exactly as Halloween is.


WHICH PAWN, AND WHY IT IS A SIGN RATHER THAN TWO CASES
-------------------------------------------------------
    neighbour_side(steps) = -1 forward, +1 backward

THE COMPANION FOLLOWS THE MOVER INTO THE SPACE IT LEAVES.  Both of the owner's
examples are that one rule — green forward from 3 takes blue from 2, green
backward from 3 takes yellow from 4.  Never hard-code "the one in front".  It is
a POSITION INDEX, never a screen coordinate.

``neighbour_candidates`` offers everything standing on that one position: both
halves of a widened row, and every pawn of a tower there — the owner's example
picks the pawn at the BOTTOM of one.  Nothing else on the board is eligible.
Frozen pawns are excluded (a freeze refuses a move everywhere else, so offering
one would offer a choice that cannot be carried out); hidden pawns are excluded
by ``live_pawns`` without this knowing Shady exists.

One candidate is taken silently.  Several open the ORDINARY ``Choice(kind=
"pawn")``, so the existing overlay, the resubmission path and the network need
no changes at all.  ``can_ask=False`` takes the first candidate in PALETTE ORDER
— AKO has no "decline" the way Speedrun does, and fizzling a card played by
another card is exactly what the ``can_ask`` note warns about.

THE COMPANION MAKES THE SAME MOVE: the same signed distance, after every
modifier.  Not "one field into the gap".  On a one-field card the two readings
coincide; this one keeps the pair adjacent on a longer card.


VARIANT 2 NEEDED NO NEW MOVEMENT PATH — DO NOT WRITE ONE
---------------------------------------------------------
``MoveBySteps.carry_riders`` already existed for Balbinka, and
``_op_move_by_steps`` already honours it by handing the executor an empty
``carried`` tuple.  ``board.place_pawn`` then lifts that one pawn out of its
tile's stack and leaves the rest standing — a valid board, with nothing
reconstructed afterwards.

Variant 1 passes ``carry_riders=True`` and gets the ordinary tower rule,
including Ondrej's Radar links, because ``travellers`` does the work and AKO
never touches it.

``MoveProjection.move(..., with_riders=False)`` is the same distinction seen
from the projection's side, for the multi-pawn path.


THINGS THAT ARE DELIBERATE
--------------------------
  * Gated on ``ctx.from_movement_card`` (N103), so abilities and Chest cards
    are untouched.  The card says "wszystkie ruchy"; this is the convention
    every movement mod already follows.  One line and one test would change it.
  * The companion takes the NEARER half of a widened destination rather than
    being asked — it is dragged, not steered (D8a).
  * A companion with nowhere to go is simply not brought; the mover still
    moves.  Not a refusal, not a fizzle.
  * Two copies in the rack bring ONE neighbour.  ``mod_rule`` reads a rule, it
    does not accumulate.


================================================================================
CARD VARIANTS  (stage 34 — read before giving a card a second reading)
================================================================================

A card with VARIANTS is one card that can be played more than one way.  It is
not two cards, and every rule below follows from that sentence.

    cards.json                 this match                  another match
    Sesja na PG ─┬─ lock       "Sesja na PG" → lock        → lock_and_cancel
                 └─ lock_and_cancel

WHAT A VARIANT MAY CHANGE, and it is exactly three things:

    text        what the card says
    passive     what holding it does
    effect      what playing it does

WHAT IT MAY NOT TOUCH — the card's IDENTITY:

    title       artwork (art / image)       count       deck_id

That list is the whole design.  A variant that could change the title or the
picture would be a second card wearing the first one's name, and the deck, the
rack, the hand, the library and every command that addresses a card by title
would all have to learn about it.  None of them does.

Anything a variant leaves out is INHERITED, so the smallest useful variant is
an id plus one key.

``CardDef.with_variant`` always resolves against ``CardDef.printed`` (the
pristine definition, kept in ``base``).  Do not "simplify" that away: without
it, 1 → 2 → 1 inherits variant 2's sentence wherever variant 1 declares none,
and the card ends up saying something no variant of it says.


WHERE THE CHOICE LIVES
----------------------
    SessionConfig.card_variants     title -> variant id
    LobbyState.card_variants        the same, on the wire
    GameState.card_variants         (deck_id, title) -> variant id

THE FIFTH MEMBER OF THE ``mod_counts`` FAMILY, and it obeys the family's rules:
EMPTY MEANS AS PRINTED.  A host on an older build, or any of the hundreds of
tests that pass no mapping, gets each card's FIRST variant — which is the card
that shipped before variants existed.  An id a card does not declare is dropped
rather than stored.

``DeckDef.with_variants`` is applied in ``setup.build_decks`` BEFORE the
shuffle, beside ``with_counts`` and ``with_uses``.  A variant cannot change a
``count``, so the pile is the same permutation whichever variant is chosen —
which is what keeps two machines building the same deck from one seed, and is
asserted by a test rather than assumed.

The chosen variants are IN THE SNAPSHOT.  They have to be: a table that agrees
about every count, every pile size and every uid, and disagrees about what one
of its cards DOES, is a desync nothing else in that dictionary would notice.


CHANGING IT DURING A MATCH
--------------------------
    SetCardVariant(deck_id, title, variant)

No ``player_index`` — like ``AdjustDeckCount`` and friends it is table
bookkeeping, so it is in neither ``_OWNED_BY_PLAYER`` nor ``_TURN_BOUND`` and
ANY seat may issue it at any time.  Adding a seat "for tidiness" would silently
restrict it, exactly as the stage 32 note warns.

An ABSOLUTE id, never "next variant": the control cycles, the command names
what it wants, so two players clicking at the same moment cannot land on a
variant neither of them chose.

Applying it rewrites ``card.definition`` on EVERY copy in the match
(``_reread_copies``).  That is what keeps two physical copies of a card one
LOGICAL card.  ``_adjust_deck_count`` builds new copies through
``variant_definition`` for the same reason — a copy conjured from the printed
definition while the rest of the deck plays another variant is the same failure
arriving an hour later.

THE BASE DEFINITION IS NEVER EDITED.  cards.json is content; a variant is a
setting of one match.


HOW AN ABILITY EFFECT IS IDENTIFIED  (the part to reuse)
--------------------------------------------------------
``Status.origin`` — ``"ability"``, ``"card"``, ``"on_draw"`` or empty.

Stamped in ONE place, ``effects.resolve_spec``, from the ``EffectContext.origin``
that already existed and already reached every handler.  So an effect written
next year gets it without knowing it exists.  Do not stamp it in the handlers:
a rule that only holds for the ones somebody remembered to edit is not a rule.

    source      a NAME, for humans ("Granny Costume")
    origin      the machine's answer to "is this an ability effect?"

Empty means the ENGINE attached the status directly — a pawn hidden by Obóz
Harcerski, a queued turn interrupt — and that is not anybody's ability.

NEVER SELECT ABILITY EFFECTS BY KIND.  A frozen pawn may be Granny Costume's or
a Chest card's, and from the outside they are identical.


SESJA NA PG'S TWO VARIANTS
--------------------------
    lock              abilities_locked                        (what shipped)
    lock_and_cancel   abilities_locked + cancel_ability_effects

The second one runs from ``_arm_mod``, keyed on the declared ``passive`` and
never on the title (N98), so it is a TRANSITION — the moment the card becomes
active — and not a sweep that runs every frame.  ``_cancel_ability_effects``
drops ``statuses.cancel_origin("ability")`` and NOTHING else: Mod rules, Chest
promises, movement statuses and engine-attached statuses all survive.  It is
emphatically not a "clear everything".

Changing the variant of a card ALREADY IN THE RACK asks whether the rack's
answer changed (``cancels_ability_effects`` — an ordinary ``mod_rule``) and
cancels if it did.  The card does not have to be played again.

THERE IS NO DEPARTURE HALF, and that is deliberate — unlike Shady and Squid
Game, which must leave nothing behind.  A cancelled effect does not come back
when the mod leaves: it was cancelled, not suspended.  The ability lock is a
passive and lifts by itself.  2 → 1 restores nothing either.


THE TWO PLACES A VARIANT IS CHOSEN
----------------------------------
LOBBY — a fifth tab, "Warianty", in the shared ``GameSettingsPanel``, so both
setup screens get it at once (the bargain stage 26 struck).  Only cards with
TWO OR MORE variants appear; the rows are gathered from every deck, so ``AKO``
and ``Nie masz Rosji`` will appear with no code change.  ``SettingsTab.options``
turns the tab's number into an INDEX into named choices, which is what let
bump / clamp / reset / merge be reused whole instead of forked.  Each row shows
the variant's own DESCRIPTION: reading the difference is the point of the
control.

CARD LIBRARY — a button under 'Dobierz kartę' reading ``WARIANT 2  (2/2)``.
UNDER the card and never on it (N36d).  THE ROW'S HEIGHT IS A PROPERTY OF THE
TAB, not of the card: a grid whose cells were each as tall as their own
contents would throw every column out of line the moment one card in a row had
variants.  So the tab reserves the room for all of its cells and only the cards
that have variants draw in it.  Display cards follow the live state through
``_refresh_variants``, so the face — and therefore the hover description — is
always the variant actually in force.


LIMITATIONS TO KNOW ABOUT BEFORE "FIXING" THEM
-----------------------------------------------
  * ``SessionConfig.card_variants`` is keyed by TITLE, ``GameState`` by
    (deck, title).  One title with variants in two decks would take the same
    variant in both.  No title is in two decks today; widen the config key the
    day one is.
  * The library's variant button inherits stage 32's gate: refused before the
    match begins and after it ends.
  * A variant may not change artwork.  That is the requirement, not an
    oversight — ``art`` is one of the identity fields a variant cannot reach.
  * The extra row makes the Mody Patusa tab scroll one row sooner.  A control
    scrolled out of sight is out of REACH (``_visible_indices`` is the hit test
    as well as the painter's list), which is stage 32's intended behaviour — a
    test that clicks it has to scroll first, as a player does.


================================================================================
HOW THE PLAYER SYSTEM WORKS
================================================================================

Player is a passive dataclass: name, hand, character card, skill card, marks
(crossed-off colours), secret_pawn, owner_id (for future networking).

Role comes from the character card's JSON "role": "piotrek" field, never from
comparing titles.

to_public_dict() / to_private_dict() exist from the start. Public data is safe
to broadcast; private data is not. The whole game hinges on hidden information,
so keep that split even while single-machine play makes it look pointless.


================================================================================
SEATS: OWNED, ACTIVE, VIEWED  (three different things — do not conflate them)
================================================================================

  OWNED   ``state.local_seat``            — the seat this machine plays.  Fixed
                                            for the whole match.
  ACTIVE  ``state.active_player_index``   — whose turn it is.  The same on every
                                            machine.
  VIEWED  ``GameScreen.view_seat``        — whose hand, character and notepad are
                                            on screen.

The multiplayer ownership bug came from having only the first two: every screen
drew ``state.active_player``'s hand, so both machines believed they were playing
the host's seat.  Rules for the third:

  * a network game starts VIEWED = OWNED; a hot-seat game starts VIEWED = ACTIVE
    and follows it, because the person at the keyboard really is playing it;
  * ``GameScreen.may_view(seat)`` decides whether another seat may be looked at:
    only in hot-seat (edit mode) or with the development option on.  NEVER in an
    ordinary network match;
  * looking is not playing.  ``controls_view`` is what gates the hand fan and
    the ability button; the engine refuses anything that slips through anyway;
  * there is ALWAYS a way back: the Home key and a button over the board, and
    the tile marked "twoje miejsce".  If you add a way to change ``view_seat``,
    make sure the way back still works.

HudContext carries ``view_index`` and ``can_act``; panels use ``ctx.player``,
never ``state.active_player``.


================================================================================
THE TURN LOOP  (stage 8)
================================================================================

A turn is "resolve one movement card".  ``GameState._after_play`` runs when one
is played *or discarded* by the active player:

    1. ``_refill_movement_hand`` — draw back up to ``setup.starting_hand_size``
       (so Piotrek's bigger hand and ChatGPT's smaller one are respected);
    2. ``_end_turn`` — hand the turn to ``next_seat()``;
    3. if the round's slots ran out, ``_begin_round`` bumps the round and
       ``_distribute_chest_card`` deals that round's chest card, through the
       ordinary draw path so the hand limit and its prompt behave normally.

Why it is all in one command: it stays atomic, so one action replays to the same
table on every machine with no extra round trips and nothing to get out of
order.  The view animates afterwards — the state does not wait for animation,
because that would make the outcome depend on frame rate.

ENDING A TURN BY HAND: ``cmd.EndTurn`` runs exactly ``_refill_movement_hand``
then ``_end_turn`` — the same ending the automatic path reaches, so the two
cannot drift apart.  It is refused when it is not your turn or while a chest
choice is pending.

DISCARDING IS HOW YOU PASS.  A hand where nothing is legal would otherwise leave
the table stuck for ever.  Keep it that way.

``RULES.auto_turn_flow`` switches the whole thing off for tests or a variant.

THE ROUND COUNTER DEALS CHEST CARDS TOO.  ``_set_round`` steps through every
round it crosses and calls ``_begin_round`` for each, because each of those
rounds owes a hunter a card.  Jumping straight to the new number was why some
players were fed and others never were.

Seats, not names: ``seat_order()`` and ``chest_recipient_seat()`` wrap
``turn_order.py``.  ``hunter_names`` holds *character titles*, and matching those
against player names silently found nobody — which is why the chest card was
promised for rounds and never handed out.

The game now starts on whichever seat the cadence puts first (Piotrek), not
always seat 0.  With a single hunter (the two-player development option) the
cadence gives that hunter a round of their own, so the same seat can legitimately
play twice running — assert that a turn was *consumed*, not that the seat changed.


================================================================================
HOW THE CHEST ROTA WORKS
================================================================================

Three separate questions, and keeping them apart is the whole of stages 22-23:

    chest_is_open           has the chest started at all?  round >= open_round.
    chest_awards_cards()    does THIS round actually deal?
    chest_recipient_seats() if it does, to whom?  TWO SEATS, not one.

Before stage 22 the first two were the same question.  They are not.


WHO IS DEALT A CARD — PIOTREK IS ONE OF THEM
--------------------------------------------
A dealing round feeds TWO seats:

    1. PIOTREK, every single dealing round.  He is not in the rotation.
    2. ONE HUNTER, whichever the rota has reached.

``chest_recipient_seats()`` returns exactly that, Piotrek first, and both the
engine and the ribbon read it.  ``chest_recipient_seat()`` (singular) is still
the HUNTER rota alone and is unchanged — do not confuse the two.

THIS WAS BROKEN FROM THE START AND NOBODY NOTICED UNTIL STAGE 23.  The ribbon
had drawn a dot under Piotrek's first slot since the chest existed, but
``_distribute_chest_card`` dealt only to ``chest_recipient_seat()``, which is
hunters-only by construction.  His marker promised a card that never arrived,
at every table size — it had nothing to do with the stage 22 timing work, which
is simply when somebody looked closely enough to see it.  A test even asserted
the broken behaviour outright (``assert not ...is_piotrek``), which is why the
suite stayed green through it (N94).


THE CADENCE
-----------
    5 or 6 players   a card every eligible round.  UNCHANGED.
    4 or fewer       a card every SECOND eligible round.

Small tables have a short rota, so "every eligible round" brought the chest
back round to the same hunter almost immediately and it stopped being an event.
The threshold and the interval are RULES.chest_sparse_max_players (4) and
RULES.chest_sparse_interval (2).

    round:      1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8  9
    <=4 players .  .  F  o  F  o  F  o  F
    5-6 players .  .  F  F  F  F  F  F  F

    .  before the chest opens     F  deals     o  eligible but skipped

The chest ALWAYS deals on the round it opens, whatever the table size, because
the count runs from the opening round: (round - open) % interval == 0.


THE ROTA STEPS ONCE PER HAND-OUT, NOT ONCE PER ROUND
----------------------------------------------------
READ THIS BEFORE CHANGING chest_recipient_for_round.

The obvious implementation — leave the rota alone and just skip every other
deal — is WRONG, and wrong in a way that is invisible at four players and fatal
at three.  With TWO hunters and a rota that steps every round, the dealing
rounds all land on the same half of it: hunter A takes every card in the game
and hunter B is never dealt anything at all, for the whole match.

So the rota steps once per HAND-OUT.  The index is a ceiling division:

    index = ceil((round - open) / interval) % n_hunters

An awarding round takes its own place in the rota; a skipped round borrows the
place of the award that FOLLOWS it, which is what moves the marker onto the
next hunter a round early.  Three players, chest opening on round 3:

    round 3   FILLED    Norbur   Norbur is dealt a card
    round 4   OUTLINED  Lubin    marker moves; nobody is dealt anything
    round 5   FILLED    Lubin    Lubin is dealt a card
    round 6   OUTLINED  Norbur   marker moves; nobody is dealt anything
    round 7   FILLED    Norbur   ...and so on

With interval 1 the ceiling division reduces to (round - open) % n, which is
exactly what the function computed before an interval existed — so five and six
players are unaffected card for card.  tests/test_chest_cadence.py asserts that
equivalence directly; do not "simplify" it back into a subtraction.


THE INDICATOR
-------------
The dot under a portrait in the turn ribbon (ui/hud.py, _draw_turn_map):

    FILLED    a card arrives this round
    OUTLINED  no card this round — either the chest has not opened yet, or
              this is a skipped round on a small table

Both of those are the same statement to a player ("not this round"), so both
draw the hollow marker.  THE MARKER STILL MOVES ON A SKIPPED ROUND; only its
fill changes.  Its position comes from chest_recipient() exactly as before.

The fill is drawn from state.chest_awards_cards() and the POSITIONS from
chest_recipient_seats() — the same two calls the engine deals on — so a filled
marker cannot promise a card that never arrives.  If you add another rule about
when or to whom the chest deals, put it in those methods and both the engine
and the ribbon will follow it.

Two dots are drawn on a dealing round: one under Piotrek's first slot and one
under the scheduled hunter.  That is not decoration; both of those seats are
dealt a card.


THE PENDING-CHOICE QUEUE
------------------------
Two seats are fed per dealing round, so TWO of them can go over the chest limit
on the same round.  ``pending_chest_choice`` used to be a single slot and the
second overflow silently overwrote the first: that player stayed over the limit
and their extra card was out of circulation for good.  With eight chest cards
the deck bled dry within a few rounds and hunters stopped being dealt anything,
which looks exactly like a distribution bug and is not one.

It is a QUEUE now (``_pending_chest_choices``).  ``pending_chest_choice``
survives as a property returning the oldest entry, so every existing caller and
test still sees one prompt at a time; ``queue_chest_choice`` adds and
``resolve_chest_choice`` removes one seat's entry.  In the interface,
``_on_chest_limit`` refuses to replace a prompt that is already up, and
``_open_next_chest_choice`` brings up the next one as each is answered.


DECK CAPACITY — WHY THE CHEST DECK IS SIXTEEN CARDS
----------------------------------------------------
Eight cards, one per title, could not supply the table once TWO cards went out
per dealing round.  At the limit a table holds Piotrek's 2 plus one per hunter:

    players   held   circulating (deck of 8)   circulating (deck of 16)
    2         3      5                         13
    3         4      4                         12
    4         5      3                         11
    5         6      2                         10
    6         7      1                          9

A dealing round wants two.  With eight cards a six-player table had ONE, and
since Piotrek is dealt first the HUNTER was the one who went without — a full
table stopped feeding hunters entirely once everybody sat at their limit (short
in 25 of 30 rounds in simulation).

Fixed in stage 23 by giving every chest title ``"count": 2``.  DOUBLED
UNIFORMLY, on purpose: bumping a single title to 2 would have reached the
minimum workable size of 9 while making that one card twice as likely as the
other seven.  Doubling everything leaves the relative odds exactly as they
were, and leaves nine cards circulating at a full table instead of a bare two.

If you change these counts, re-run the sweep: every table size from 2 to 6 must
show zero short rounds.  ``test_the_deck_can_supply_a_full_table_indefinitely``
checks it over forty rounds, and ``test_no_card_leaks_out_of_the_chest_deck``
checks the accounting.


MULTIPLAYER
-----------
NO NEW COMMANDS, NO NEW EVENTS, NOTHING IN THE SNAPSHOT.  Both the cadence and
the recipient list are pure functions of the round number, the opening round,
the table size and which seat holds Piotrek — all of which every replica
already agrees on and none of which can drift.  They consume no RNG, so they
cannot shift a deck shuffle out of step either, and a skipped round takes no
card off the pile.  The server stays the only authority: it applies
``_begin_round`` like any other command and broadcasts the result.

The two cards are drawn in a FIXED ORDER, Piotrek first, because two draws off
the same pile in a different order on two machines is a desync.  Do not sort
the recipients, and do not make the order depend on anything local.


================================================================================
VISUAL IDENTITY  (stage 10, extended in stage 12 — read before drawing anything)
================================================================================

The look comes from a concept image the owner supplied: a near-black table,
panels of dark slate edged in worn brass, parchment cards, one warm gold for
anything the eye should land on, and one green accent for "this is yours / this
is now".  Everything on screen must belong to that one language.

WHERE THE STYLE LIVES
  * ``config/theme.py`` — every colour.  NOTHING outside it should contain a
    literal RGB triple; ``tests/test_visual_style.py`` counts them and fails if
    they creep back.  A second skin is a second ``Theme``, not a search.
  * ``render/highlight.py`` — every NUMBER that says what an interaction looks
    like: hover lighten amounts, the lift, the pressed drop, glow strengths,
    shadow depths, and how far a bloom may reach.  See THE HIGHLIGHT SYSTEM
    below; this file is the reason the interface has one hover and not five.
  * ``render/renderer.py`` — the shared chrome:
      ``table_background()``  the dark wash with its vignette, cached per size;
      ``premium_panel()``     shadow + gradient body + top-edge bevel + brass
                              border + corner brackets.  USE THIS, never a flat
                              ``panel()``, for anything that holds content;
      ``interactive_panel()`` the same, for anything the mouse can TOUCH — it
                              takes an ``Emphasis`` and applies the lift, the
                              press and the glow for you;
      ``inset_well()``        the opposite bevel — empty slots, notepads, the
                              recesses decks sit in;
      ``section_heading()``   upper-case letter-spaced brass with rules and
                              diamonds; every sidebar section uses it;
      ``spaced_text()``       letter-spaced type (pygame has no tracking), cached
                              per word;
      ``fit_spaced_text()`` / ``fit_text()``  the same, shrunk to the box given;
      ``circle_button()``     round brass-rimmed buttons.
  * ``ui/widgets.py`` — ``Button`` has idle / hover / pressed states: hover
    lightens and lifts, pressed drops and loses its shadow.  Depth, not a colour
    swap.  ``primary=True`` is the one-per-screen call to action.  It also
    MEASURES ITSELF: see BUTTONS SIZE THEMSELVES below.

THE HIGHLIGHT SYSTEM  (stage 12 — the rule, and why it exists)

    A HIGHLIGHT FOLLOWS THE SHAPE OF THE THING IT HIGHLIGHTS.

There used to be one ``Renderer.glow(centre, radius, colour)`` drawing a RADIAL
DISC, and each of its nine callers chose a radius by eye — from 1.15× to 2.6×
the component.  The worst was inside ``premium_panel``, which meant every panel
asking for emphasis got a disc 0.75 × its longest side: on a 300×70 player tile,
a 225-pixel glowing circle lying across the whole panel behind it.  That single
line is what made the interface look unfinished.

``glow()` no longer exists.  Its replacements take the COMPONENT'S OWN GEOMETRY
instead of a multiplier, so the old mistake cannot be written:

  * ``shape_glow(rect, colour, radius=, strength=)`` — rounded-rect bloom for
    anything rectangular.  Capped at ``BLOOM_MAX`` px past the edge however
    large the panel;
  * ``ring_glow(centre, radius, colour, strength=)`` — for things that really
    ARE round: pawns, turn-order portraits, board fields, the slider knob.
    ``radius`` is the COMPONENT's radius; the light reaches ``BLOOM`` of it
    further out.  Passing a radius you already multiplied by 2.6 is exactly
    what this signature exists to prevent.

FIVE STATES, ONE LANGUAGE.  hover / selection / focus / active / pressed all
come from ``highlight.emphasis(theme, ...)``, which returns an ``Emphasis``
(fill, border, text, glow, glow strength, shadow, vertical offset).  Hand it to
``interactive_panel``.  Do NOT work out your own hover colours in a panel — a
line like ``lighten(fill, 0.18 if hovered else 0.0)`` is how the interface grew
five slightly different hovers in the first place.

BUTTONS SIZE THEMSELVES  (stage 12)
  * ``Button.natural_size(r)`` measures the caption; ``Button.fit(r, ...)``
    grows the box to hold it; ``fit_buttons(r, [...])`` sizes a GROUP to the
    widest of them, so a menu column stays a column with no ragged edge.
  * No screen quotes a button width any more.  "Gra lokalna (hot-seat)" needed
    350 px of text in a hard-coded 320 px box at 1440p, and
    "UŻYJ UMIEJĘTNOŚCI (1/1)" overflowed its button by 41 px at the same size.
  * ``Stepper`` measures its own ± labels rather than assuming a 40 px square,
    and keeps its row height when its width grows.
  * A button that swaps captions is measured against the LONGER one (the
    lobby's "Jestem gotowy" / "Czekam na hosta"), not whichever is showing when
    the screen is laid out.
  * ``fit_spaced_text`` / ``fit_text`` shrink type to its box as a last resort,
    so even a layout that insists on a small control cannot clip a label.

RULES
  * A CARD FACE CARRIES ONE BORDER AND NOTHING ELSE ON TOP (stage 31).  The
    concept's double frame — an outer edge plus an inset brass rule — and the
    two status pips the hand fan painted at the top edge of every card are
    gone.  They were harmless on plain parchment and became damage the moment a
    card could be a full-bleed illustration: the pip landed in the middle of
    the picture.  Do not put either back.  The information the pips carried has
    other channels; see HOW SIGNATURE CARDS WORK.
  * a panel is never a filled rectangle: use ``premium_panel`` or ``inset_well``;
  * anything the mouse can touch goes through ``interactive_panel``;
  * headings are upper case, letter-spaced, brass — never sentence-case labels;
  * one accent (``theme.accent``) marks the active player and legal drops; one
    prompt colour (``theme.prompt``) marks anything the engine is asking about;
  * the board keeps its own art.  Only its FRAME belongs to the interface.

================================================================================
RENDERING QUALITY  (stage 9 — read before drawing anything scalable)
================================================================================

THE RULE: never enlarge something that has already been rasterised.  Regenerate
it at the size it will be seen.  Blurry text, blurry cards and blurry icons all
come from breaking this in one place or another.

  * ``CardRenderer.face(card, size)`` paints the card AT that size — text laid
    out again, not scaled.  ``quantised(size, scale)`` gives the size to paint
    at, rounded to ``SIZE_STEP`` so a smoothly growing card repaints a handful
    of times rather than every frame.
  * ``draw_transformed`` applies the scale BEFORE painting and only rotates
    afterwards.  A hovered or dragged card therefore gets sharper as it grows.
  * ``RecentlyPlayed`` does the same for its hover preview.  It used to
    ``rotozoom`` a 95×137 face by 2.15 — which is exactly what made the one card
    the player wanted to read the least readable thing on screen.
  * ``FontBook.set_scale`` multiplies every requested size by
    ``Layout.ui_scale`` (window height / 1080, clamped).  Bigger displays get
    bigger GLYPHS, not stretched ones.  ``App._apply_font_scale`` sets it and
    clears the renderer caches, because surfaces rasterised at the old scale
    would otherwise share the screen with the new ones.
  * The board's world surface is still scaled by the camera — that is a map
    being zoomed, which is the one place where scaling is the right answer.

LAYOUT AND SPACE
  * ``Layout.ui_scale`` is the factor for anything quoted in pixels.  New
    constants should be multiplied by it or derived from a measured font.
  * Column widths are MEASURED, not guessed: ``_fit_left_column`` /
    ``_fit_right_column`` size the cards from the height available, make the
    column exactly wide enough to hold them (with a floor for the labels and a
    ceiling of a card in hand), and everything left goes to the board.  The
    board holds ~60-76% of the window width across supported resolutions.
  * The right column is sized PER CASE: Piotrek has a skill row, a hunter has
    the colour notepad instead.  Sizing both for the worst case left a hunter's
    panel a third empty.
  * ``tests/test_rendering_quality.py`` measures crispness by comparing the
    contrast between neighbouring pixels of a redrawn card against a zoomed one.
    Keep it: one stray ``rotozoom`` and everything is mush again.


================================================================================
MENU LAYOUT AND TEXT ENTRY  (read before touching a menu)
================================================================================

LAYOUT.  Every menu places its contents in ONE downward pass from measured font
heights, not from fractions of the window.  Fractions were close enough at 1080p
and wrong below it.  The rules:

  * start from ``headings.content_top(r, layout)`` — never a magic fraction;
  * measure text with ``r.fonts.get(size).get_height()`` rather than assuming;
  * anchor the bottom block (buttons, error line) to the bottom margin and grow
    the middle to fit;
  * when it will not fit, SHRINK THE GAPS (host screen) or the row height
    (lobby seats, character rows) — never let something fall off the screen.

tests/test_menu_layout.py wraps ``Renderer.text``, records every string a screen
draws, and asserts that no two overlap and none escapes the window, at four
resolutions.  Add a screen, add it to that test.

TEXT ENTRY (``ui/widgets.py``, class TextInput).  Two traps, both hit once:

  * SDL sends BOTH a KEYDOWN carrying ``unicode`` and a TEXTINPUT for the same
    keystroke.  Take the character from TEXTINPUT ONLY — handling both inserts
    everything twice, and TEXTINPUT is the one that understands dead keys and
    the Polish letters this game is full of;
  * held keys do not repeat by themselves.  ``pygame.key.set_repeat`` would also
    spam every in-game shortcut, so the field runs its own timer in ``update``:
    one delete immediately, a pause, then a steady stream.  Screens MUST call
    ``field.update(mouse, dt)`` every frame or held backspace does nothing;
  * a click is NOT a selection.  Leaving a zero-length one behind made the first
    character typed "selected", so the second replaced it.

TextInput supports what a native box does: caret placement by clicking, drag to
select, Shift+arrows, Home/End, Ctrl+A/C/X/V, and typing or Backspace replacing
a selection.  ``ui/clipboard.py`` reaches the real system clipboard and falls
back to an internal buffer only where that is impossible — it never raises.
See CLIPBOARD below; it is not the thin wrapper it looks like, and it must not
be simplified back into one.
Placeholder text is drawn in a dimmer grey than anything the player can type and
is never part of ``value``.


================================================================================
CLIPBOARD
================================================================================

``ui/clipboard.py``.  Three functions — ``copy``, ``paste``, ``reset`` — and a
lot of comment, for a reason.

THE SYSTEM CLIPBOARD IS THE CLIPBOARD.  Copying in the game lands in Discord, a
browser or Notepad; anything copied there pastes into the game.  The in-process
buffer exists ONLY for a machine where the system clipboard genuinely cannot be
reached, and it must stay invisible.

Why the module is not three lines:

* ``pygame.scrap`` is two different APIs.  ``put_text`` / ``get_text`` exist in
  pygame-ce only.  Upstream pygame 2.6 — what ``requirements.txt`` asks for —
  has neither, and calling them raises ``AttributeError``;
* the ``put`` / ``get`` pair takes a type string, and upstream pygame's SDL2
  backend accepts exactly one: ``"text/plain;charset=utf-8"``.
  ``pygame.SCRAP_TEXT`` is ``"text/plain"`` and is refused with "content could
  not be placed in clipboard"; other backends and older builds want
  ``UTF8_STRING`` or ``TEXT``;
* **and the type decides the ENCODING.**  pygame does not use SDL on Windows:
  ``src_c/scrap.c`` selects the SDL2 backend with ``#if !defined(__WIN32__)``,
  so Windows compiles ``scrap_win.c``, which maps

      "text/plain;charset=utf-8"  ->  CF_UNICODETEXT  (Windows reads UTF-16LE)
      "text/plain"                ->  CF_TEXT         (Windows reads ANSI)

  and then ``memcpy``s the bytes it was handed, converting NOTHING.  UTF-8 sent
  to the first of those is read back as UTF-16: "sdh" arrives in Discord as
  摳.  ``_TEXT_FORMATS`` therefore carries (type, encoding, terminator)
  together and the Windows table is a different table, not a different order.
  CF_UNICODETEXT also wants a two-byte terminator and ``pygame_scrap_put``
  allocates exactly ``srclen + 1``, so the second zero byte is appended here.

So both call styles are tried, the type is discovered on first use and then
remembered.  Delete either branch and the game works on one installation and
silently stops talking to the desktop on another.

Rules the code depends on:

* the system is attempted FIRST, every time.  ``_fallback`` is consulted only
  when the clipboard could not be READ at all — not when it is empty, because
  an empty clipboard is an answer (N67);
* ``_ensure_ready()`` caches only a definite answer.  "No display yet" and "no
  window yet" leave the question open (N62) — some backends need a window
  before ``scrap.init()`` succeeds, and a probe made in between must not decide
  the session;
* nothing raises.  A clipboard is not worth a stack trace in a menu;
* pasted text is flattened to one line (CRLF first, or Windows text arrives
  with doubled spaces).  These are single-line fields;
* UTF-16 is decoded from the WHOLE buffer, never from one stripped of trailing
  NULs: the high byte of a final ASCII character IS a NUL, and stripping it
  turned "ABCD12" into "ABCD1".  The terminator is cut after decoding, the
  way Windows does it;
* ``reset()`` is a TEST entry point and it blanks the system clipboard too —
  otherwise a value copied by one test is still readable in the next and tests
  pass or fail by order.

Testing it is the subtle part, and it caught this module out twice.  Every
clipboard test written before stage 15 went through ``copy()`` and ``paste()``,
which agreed with each other perfectly while nothing reached the operating
system.  Stage 16 was the same trap one level down: on Windows pygame returns
the program's own cached bytes while it still owns the clipboard, so text
written in the wrong encoding still pastes back correctly *inside the game*.
The answer is ``_WinScrap`` in ``tests/test_usability.py`` — a stand-in that
reproduces ``scrap_win.c``'s format mapping, its single trailing NUL and its
ownership cache, and can be asked what another application would see.  A test proves something here only
if it reaches PAST the module — put with ``pygame.scrap.put``, read back with
``clipboard.paste()``, and the other way round (N66).  Even then, SDL's dummy
driver keeps clipboard text inside the process, so the last mile — a real
window manager, a real other application — is ``tools/clipboard_check.py``, by
hand, on a desktop.

THE MODAL STACK (stage 44 — READ THIS BEFORE ADDING A WINDOW)
-------------------------------------------------------------
There is ONE ordered list of every window that can own input, in
``ui/modals.py``, registered in ``GameScreen._register_modals``.

    REGISTRATION ORDER  ==  PRIORITY  ==  PAINT ORDER

``draw()`` walks it upwards; ``handle_event`` offers the event to the TOPMOST
ACTIVE modal and to NO OTHER.

    INVARIANT: the visually topmost active modal receives input, and no other
               modal receives any.

Bottom to top:

    reveal · pending choice · chest limit · MOD PATUSA SELECTION · card picker
    · Paczka · card library · movement / breakup / check decisions
    · match start · victory · pause menu

WHY IT EXISTS.  Until stage 44 the same order was written down TWICE — the
chain of ``if ....active:`` tests and the sequence of ``.draw()`` calls — with
nothing tying them together, so they drifted.  A drift between them is never
cosmetic: it is a window painted on top while the window underneath answers
the click.  Round 7 is where it was caught (the Mod selection drawn over the
chest limit, the chest limit eating the clicks) but the Card Library and the
reveal overlay had drifted too.

RULES THE STACK ENFORCES:
  * A lower modal is PENDING — still drawn, still holding its state, NOT
    actionable.  That is what turns two clickable windows into a queue.
  * ``blocking`` says whether a modal swallows everything (chest limit, card
    picker, library, the two endings) or lets navigation through (the mod
    vote, a pending choice).  The mod pause was never a blindfold.
  * A NON-blocking modal lets the wheel and the middle drag past, but never a
    CLICK onto another window: a press inside its own panel, or inside any
    pending modal beneath it, stops there.
  * ``blocks_keyboard`` is separate, so S / F / Tab keep reaching the game
    under the dialogs that always allowed them.
  * ``resolve`` is Esc's valid-random fallback — see ESC below.

TO ADD A WINDOW: register a ``Modal`` in ``_register_modals``.  Do NOT add a
branch to ``handle_event`` and do NOT add a ``.draw()`` call to ``draw``;
that is precisely how the two orders came apart.

Announcements nobody clicks — the elimination notice, the connection banner,
the debug panel — are NOT modals and are not registered.


ESC: RESOLVE, NEVER CANCEL (stage 44)
-------------------------------------
Esc ANSWERS the active window with a valid random choice and closes it; the
queue then advances.  It is not a cancel and not a "hide all dialogs".

The valid set is ALWAYS the one the interface itself would have accepted a
click on: the three mods actually dealt, the cards actually in the prompt,
``choice.options`` / ``card_options``, the breakup tiles, Block only while the
card still has its use, Refuse only while Ice Block has one.  ONE option means
that option.  ZERO options means Esc keeps its old meaning rather than
inventing an answer.

  * The modal's OWN handler sees Esc first, so meanings that already existed
    survive: backing out of a Block confirmation, closing the Library.
  * ``victory`` and ``pause_menu`` declare NO resolver.  "Quit the
    application" is not a choice a die gets to make.
  * CANCELLING IS THE RIGHT BUTTON.  It always was, in
    ``_handle_choice_event`` and the card picker; Esc and right-click simply
    no longer mean the same thing.
  * The draw comes from a UI-LOCAL RNG, deliberately outside R4.  It happens
    on one machine at a moment no other machine knows about, so pulling from
    the shared seeded stream would leave every replica one number behind.
    What travels is the COMMAND it produces, exactly as a mouse click does.

Gestures:
  hand card, left click ....... play it if playable, otherwise discard
  hand card, drag to board .... play it (preview shown while dragging)
  hand card, right click ...... stage for the mod rack, then click a slot
  hand card, middle click ..... discard
  mod slot, right click ....... discard the mod
  pawn, drag .................. free move, snaps to a nearby field
  pawn token in a prompt ...... answers a "which pawn?" question (so does
                                clicking the pawn out on the board)
  empty board, drag ........... pan the map (pawns win the gesture)
  tower, hover ................ fans out so each pawn is clickable
  played card, hover .......... enlarges above everything and stops fading
  ability button .............. activates the character ability / Piotrek skill,
                                showing remaining uses from the JSON "uses"
  book, bottom-right .......... opens the Card Library
  Ctrl+wheel / middle drag .... zoom / pan
  S / F / Tab ................. snapping toggle / fit view / next player
  Esc ......................... resolve the active window with a valid random
                                choice; with nothing open, the pause menu
  right click (in a prompt) ... cancel the choice, and it costs nothing

Panels implement draw(ctx) and optionally handle_click(ctx, button, ui) which
returns a list of Commands. `ui` is the GameScreen (for pending_mod_uid and
start_rename). HudContext carries everything a panel needs for one frame.


================================================================================
TEXT FIELDS (stage 14 — read before adding one)
================================================================================

ONE editor, two presentations.  ui/widgets.TextEditor holds the string, the
caret, the selection and the held-key repeat; it draws nothing.  Wrap it:

  TextInput   the standing form boxes (host/join: nickname, server, code).
              Owns a rect, so it also does mouse click-to-place and drag
              selection.  Five instances.
  TextField   the inline rename editor the HUD draws over a player tile.
              Owns no rect and no font.  One instance.

A NEW FIELD WRAPS TextEditor.  It does not reimplement editing, and it does not
subclass TextInput just to get a different look.

WHAT A WRAPPER MUST DO, or the field is quietly broken:
  - call ``editor.tick(dt)`` once a frame, or holding Backspace deletes exactly
    one character and nothing says why.  This is precisely what was wrong with
    the rename box until stage 14: clearing an eleven-letter name took eleven
    presses;
  - call ``editor.release()`` when focus is lost, or a key repeats for ever —
    a focus change can eat the KEYUP;
  - let TEXTINPUT events reach ``editor.insert``; KEYDOWN handles the rest.
    handle_key returns False for Enter and Tab, because those belong to the
    screen and only the screen knows what they mean;
  - draw the placeholder in darken(theme.text_dim, 0.72).  A hint is not
    content and must never be mistakable for it.  TextField exposes
    ``showing_placeholder`` because display_text() returns one string either
    way and only the HUD picks the colour.

KNOWN GAP: TextField has no MOUSE selection.  It owns no geometry, and
GameScreen deliberately treats any mouse press during a rename as "confirm".
Giving it drag-selection means handing it a rect and a font AND changing what a
click means mid-rename.  Keyboard selection (Shift+arrows, Shift+Home/End,
Ctrl+A) all works.  Do not fix this by bolting a rect onto TextField in
passing: it is a UI change, not a patch.

TRANSIENT CONFIRMATIONS
  ui/network_screens.CopyNotice — "show this for two seconds", written once,
  rather than three screens each keeping a string and a float that drift apart.
  It fades over its last third; vanishing between frames reads as a glitch.
  It is NOT a general toast system.  When a third kind of transient message
  turns up, that is the moment to generalise it — not before.


HOW THE RENDERER WORKS
================================================================================

- The static world (ground, road, rivers, bridges, props, fields, camp) is
  painted ONCE into a world-sized surface by BoardRenderer.build(). Each frame
  only the visible slice is scaled and blitted, so cost scales with the window,
  not with board size. build() needs a live display surface — call it from
  Screen.on_enter, not from __init__.
- Card faces are painted at their FINAL size (not scaled up from 140×200) and
  cached by (content, size, state). CardRenderer.clear_cache() on window resize.
- The fan uses draw_transformed() = rotozoom of a cached face.  Its shadow is
  the ROTATED silhouette (RGB multiplied to zero keeps the alpha), softened by
  a scale-down/scale-up pass and cached by quantised angle and scale.
- Deck hover brightens the top card of the pile itself (CardRenderer.back takes
  a ``brightness``) instead of drawing a halo around it.
- BoardRenderer draws a tiled countryside backdrop under the board so the
  viewport is never bare panel colour, with 0.45 parallax.
- Renderer caches text surfaces, drop shadows and glows by key.
- All drawing helpers take an explicit `surface`; passing None uses the surface
  from Renderer.begin(). Prefer passing it explicitly inside board_renderer.


================================================================================
HOW ANIMATIONS WORK
================================================================================

Two mechanisms, both frame-rate independent:

1. engine/animation.py — Tween (start, end, duration, delay, easing, callbacks)
   managed by Animator under a string key; adding the same key replaces the
   running tween. Plus approach()/approach_point() for exponential chasing,
   which is what the fan and the camera use.

2. board_view.Walk — a pawn following a list of waypoints one segment at a
   time, with a hop per field. Created from a TokenWalked event. This is what
   makes a played card walk the pawn through every intermediate field instead
   of sliding it. Riders get the same walk with a delay so a tower moves like
   a tower.

Rule: animation state lives in the VIEW (board_view.visual, hand_fan.slots),
never in GameState. GameState positions are authoritative and instantaneous;
the view lags behind them on purpose.


================================================================================
IMPORTANT DESIGN DECISIONS
================================================================================

D1. COMMANDS AND EVENTS. Every mutation is a serialisable Command; every
    consequence is an Event. This gives multiplayer, replays, undo and testing
    for free. Do not add "just this one" direct mutation.

D2. ONE SEED. SessionConfig.seed drives board generation, every deck shuffle
    (each deck gets random.Random(seed + n) so adding a movement card does not
    change the chest shuffle) and character dealing. Two machines with the same
    seed build identical games before any packet is sent.

D3. THE BOARD SPACING GUARANTEE. Fields used to overlap on tight bends. The fix
    is derived, not tuned:
      - on a curve of radius R the inner lane's spacing is compressed by
        (R - lane_offset)/R;
      - requiring the compressed spacing to stay above
        2*tile_radius + min_tile_gap gives BoardLayout.min_turn_radius;
      - for a sine of amplitude A the tightest radius is L²/(4π²A), so the
        wavelength is chosen from the amplitude, not the other way round.
    The generated road is then MEASURED (curvature via circumscribed circles,
    self-approach via resampled point pairs). If it fails, amplitude drops 12%
    and it retries; amplitude 0 is a straight road, so the loop terminates.
    DO NOT "fix" board looks by removing these checks.

D4. ARC-LENGTH PARAMETRISATION. Path2D interpolates by DISTANCE, not by spline
    parameter, so field spacing is constant however curvy the road is.

D5. RESPONSIVE, NOT SCALED. The game draws at the window's real resolution and
    Layout recomputes on resize. The old fixed-canvas-plus-scale approach made
    big monitors blurry and gave the board no extra room.

D5a. RESPONSIVE MEANS REALLOCATING, NOT SHRINKING (stage 29).  One number
    multiplying everything is a zoom control: every region keeps its share and
    a smaller screen is just a smaller picture of the same layout, which is
    what made a 1920x1200 laptop unreadable while 2560x1440 was fine.  A
    responsive layout decides what gives ground and in what order.  Here that
    order is fixed and deliberate — board height, then side columns, then
    margins, and the hand last — because the movement cards are the one thing
    on screen that is actually being READ.  See "HOW SCALING WORKS".

D6. EFFECTS AS DATA. Card behaviour lives in JSON. Balancing is the whole point
    of the project, and balancing must not require editing Python.

D7. THE ENGINE RESOLVES TARGETS. PlayCard carries the card uid, not the outcome.
    A future client can say which card it played, never what that card does.

D12. TRANSFORMATION IS REAL, NOT COSMETIC.  Card.transform() swaps the
    definition and keeps the uid, so the hand, the animation and the played-card
    strip all follow the same object.  Deck.return_card() restores the original,
    which is what keeps a Gamechanger in the chest deck rather than slowly
    turning the deck into Kingmakers.

D13. EDIT MODE IS AN ENGINE RULE, NOT A UI ONE.  GameState.may_control() gates
    every player-owned command.  Putting it in the interface would mean a
    client could simply ask nicely; putting it here means the host decides.

D9. THE ENGINE ASKS, THE INTERFACE ANSWERS.  A handler that needs a decision
    returns a Choice describing it; the interface renders whatever it is given.
    Adding an effect that needs three decisions needs no UI work at all.

D10. A REFUSED ACTION COSTS NOTHING.  Abilities spend a use only after the plan
    executes, so a refusal (wrong pawn, not enough gap) leaves the count alone.
    NotAvailable is deliberately distinct from Refusal: "the rules for this do
    not exist yet" is a different thing from "you cannot do that".

D11. RANDOMNESS LIVES IN THE EXECUTOR.  Seks z pedałami draws from the seeded
    RNG in the operation, not in the handler, because a preview must not
    consume randomness — dragging a card over the board would otherwise change
    what it does.

D8a. A DOUBLED POSITION IS A DECISION, NOT A LABEL.  The engine refuses the
    play until the choice arrives, rather than picking a half itself, because
    which half a pawn stands on decides who is on top of whom in a tower — and
    towers are how hunters win.  Intermediate positions are chosen
    automatically (nearer half) since nothing depends on them.

D8. HIDDEN INFORMATION SPLIT EXISTS ALREADY (to_public_dict / to_private_dict)
    even though nothing uses it yet, because retrofitting it later would mean
    auditing every send site.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

- The in-game rename box (ui/widgets.TextField) supports every KEYBOARD editing
  behaviour but no MOUSE selection: it owns no rect or font, and GameScreen
  treats a mouse press during a rename as "confirm". See TEXT FIELDS for why
  this is a UI decision rather than a missing patch.

- THE CHARACTER NAME OVERRUNS ITS ABILITY CARD on large displays: about 7 px at
  2560x1440 and 24 px at 4K.  DIAGNOSED, not mysterious — hud.py draws it with
  ``fonts.get(int(21 * ui_scale))`` and FontBook multiplies by the scale again,
  so the type moves with ui_scale SQUARED while the room under it
  (``r_name_h``) moves with ui_scale.  It is the same double-scaling stage 29
  removed from card faces, in the one place that was not part of the report.
  Left alone because widening the band moves the reference display's right-hand
  column and stage 29 promised not to touch it, so the fix is a small stage of
  its own rather than a patch in passing.
  ``test_the_character_name_does_not_run_into_its_ability_card`` pins it: no
  overhang at all at or below 1920x1200, and the known overhang may not exceed
  24 px.  Whoever fixes it should re-run that test AND
  ``test_the_reference_display_is_untouched``, which is the one that will
  object.

L23. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Gejtos clamps at the FAR end of the board while
    refusing at the near one.  The card names only the start area ("przed polem
    1"), so the finish keeps the rule every other movement in the game uses.
    Asymmetric on purpose; if the owner wants it symmetric it is one branch.

L24. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Alter Ego may be played on any turn Piotrek holds
    the card, including one where the hunters are about to check a tower — the
    swap stops the table and voids a pending Squid Game check.  Nothing in the
    text limits WHEN it may be played, and limiting it would be inventing a
    rule.  Worth raising with the owner: it is the strongest defensive use of
    the card and it is currently unrestricted.

L25. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: after a swap the hunters keep no record that the
    OLD identity was ever ruled out beyond the single crossing Alter Ego leaves.
    A hunter who eliminated four colours before the swap loses that work
    entirely, which is what "the colour panel resets" says — but it is a large
    swing and the owner may want the old crossings kept on a separate line.

L20. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Dzieckorolka does NOT sweep the field it LANDS on,
    only the fields it walks through ("po drodze").  It makes no observable
    difference today — a collected destination pawn would be re-inserted exactly
    where it already was, since the collected group goes underneath the mover
    and on top of whoever was there — so this is the reading that stays true if
    the insertion rule ever changes rather than a behaviour the owner has to
    confirm.  Worth knowing before anybody "fixes" it.

L21. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Dzieckorolka does not consume ChatGPT's movement
    bonus, and a frozen TOP pawn makes its field yield nothing rather than the
    sweep reaching to the pawn beneath.  The first because the collection is
    written against a route of exactly the printed length and a card whose
    distance a skill could stretch would sweep a field its own text never
    promised; the second because "always take the TOP pawn" is the rule and
    digging underneath one takes a tower apart.  Both are one-line changes.

L22. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Gambit Patusa reverses a ``direction: "either"``
    card too, which is very nearly a no-op — the player simply picks the other
    way at the prompt and gets what they wanted.  No card in the movement deck
    declares ``either`` today, so nothing exercises it; if one ever does, the
    question is whether the reversal should be described in the prompt or
    skipped for cards that already let the player choose.

L17. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Shady removes ONE pawn, and anything riding it
    stays where it was and settles onto the field. The brief says to remove
    "the BOTTOM pawn of that stack" and its own example removes one pawn (pink
    standing on green: "green disappears"), but it also says to store and
    RESTORE "its carried stack", which only means something if the tower
    travels too. Those conflict. One pawn leaves, because the brief elsewhere
    says "since one pawn is absent" and because the checking exception is
    written for exactly one absent pawn. The carried stack IS recorded in the
    status and in PawnHidden, so the other reading is a small change rather
    than a rewrite. The owner should confirm.

L18. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Squid Game's automatic check runs at the start of
    every round from the one AFTER it arrived, and the pawn it inspects is
    chosen by board POSITION. Two pawns on one field are two pawns at the
    front, so a tower at the head of the road skips the round exactly as two
    separate pawns level with each other do. The brief says "exactly one pawn
    occupies the furthest position", which is what that implements, but a
    reading where the tower's bottom pawn is checked is equally defensible and
    would be one call to change.

L19. Paczka's window is driven by an EVENT, so a player who reconnects and
    replays a long command log can see it open for a mod that arrived several
    rounds ago. It is dismissed with one click and changes nothing, so this is
    a wart rather than a bug — but it is the same class of thing N74 exists
    for, and if it ever matters the fix is to drive it from ``armed_mods`` and
    the state the way the match overlays are driven.
    NARROWED IN STAGE 44: the window is now DEFERRED while a Mod selection is
    open (see PACZKA), and ``GameScreen`` holds a ``ChestCardsRevealed`` that
    arrives during one anyway — so the replay can no longer drop a window on
    top of a live selection. The general wart, a window for a mod that arrived
    rounds ago, is unchanged and still wants the state-driven fix.

L14. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Halloween treats the CAMP as one cluster — pawns
    waiting there are neighbours of one another. The brief says only "one in
    front or one behind", which taken literally means no pawn in the camp ever
    has a neighbour; since every pawn starts there and the lobby allows
    ``mod_round_first: 1``, that reading can freeze the board for the whole
    match and make both victory conditions unreachable. The owner should
    confirm. If the literal rule is wanted, the deadlock needs solving first —
    exempting camp pawns entirely is the obvious alternative.

L15. DECIDED, NOT SPECIFIED: Masa solna caps the distance PRINTED ON THE CARD,
    and ChatGPT's +1 movement bonus still applies on top, so a capped two-field
    card plus the bonus moves two. The brief says movement cards move one
    field; the bonus is a status somebody spent an ability to get, not a
    movement card, and swallowing it would have rewritten a skill this stage
    was not asked to touch. Trivial to change — the cap is applied in
    ``_capped_steps`` before the bonus is added — if the owner wants the mod to
    win outright.

L16. The Mod Patusa counts are the first setting that is a MAPPING rather than a
    number, and the first that can make a deck too small to use. The panel
    warns and ``_open_mod_selection`` already declines on an exhausted deck, so
    a table with no mods plays on without pausing — correct, but it means a
    misconfigured lobby fails quietly rather than loudly. If mods ever become
    load-bearing for balance, consider refusing to start instead.

L1. STILL OPEN. A client rebuilds the FULL state from the seed and the command
    log, so a modified client could read another player's hand. The server now
    checks *permissions* (whose seat, whose turn) but does not yet filter what
    it broadcasts. Acceptable for friends on an unmodified build; must be fixed
    before any public release. ``to_public_dict()`` / ``to_private_dict()`` on
    Player exist for exactly this and are still unused.
L1a. The fix is not free: filtering means the server can no longer broadcast one
    command to everyone, and a client can no longer verify the fingerprint
    against a state it does not fully have. Expect per-seat views and a
    per-seat fingerprint.
L2. FIXED IN STAGE 11 — reconnection works, seats are held for
    ``reconnect.grace_period`` (180 s), and STATE_SYNC replays the log.
L3. No "return to lobby" after a match starts; leaving returns that player to
    the main menu. The others carry on, since the match lives on the server —
    but there is no way to un-start a room.
L3d. A player whose grace period expires leaves an EMPTY SEAT: their pawn stays
    where it is and their turn is skipped by the ordinary turn rules. Nobody
    takes over their hand. This is the least disruptive behaviour available, not
    a designed rule.
L3e. The server keeps the whole command log in memory for as long as a room
    lives. A very long match on a very small host would grow; nothing prunes it,
    because pruning would break the replay that reconnection depends on.
L2a. The Debug Version option is a development aid: it only relaxes the player
    minimum to two.  A two-player game is playable but not balanced — one hunter
    against Piotrek is not the game the design document describes.
L3b. OUT OF DATE: win conditions landed in stage 17; a match ends and offers

VICTORY AND THE HIDDEN IDENTITY  (stage 17)
================================================================================

HOW A MATCH IS DECIDED
  ``engine/victory.py``.  One function — ``review(state)`` — is the whole of it.
  It is called by the AUTHORITY after every accepted command and answers with a
  list of commands, almost always empty:

    * Piotrek's colour is standing on the FINISH tile        → DeclareVictory
    * every pawn is on ONE field and the BOTTOM of that tower
      is his colour                                          → DeclareVictory
    * ...and the bottom is some other colour that has not
      been checked before                                    → EliminatePawn
    * anything else                                           → nothing

  A colour is checked ONCE.  Once it is in ``state.eliminated_pawns`` the same
  tower may sit there for ever and nothing more happens; the hunters have to
  rearrange the table to learn anything new.

  Why it returns commands instead of mutating: every other change in this game
  is a logged, broadcast command, and an ending has to be one too — otherwise a
  player who reconnects replays the log and lands in a match that never
  finished, and the fingerprint that catches desyncs stops covering the one
  fact everybody cares about.

  ``review`` is a pure function of the state, so it is safe to call anywhere —
  but it only ever DECIDES anything where the hidden colour is known, which is
  the authority and nowhere else.

WHERE THE IDENTITY LIVES
  ``Player.secret_pawn``, on the copy of the state that is entitled to it:

    server (online)       set by ``GameState.set_piotrek_pawn`` when the chosen
                          colour arrives.  This is THE copy that counts.
    Piotrek's client      set from IDENTITY_ACCEPTED — his own secret, and he
                          is allowed to see which pawn is his.
    every other client    ``None`` for the whole match, until DeclareVictory
                          reveals it.
    hot-seat game         dealt from the seed as before: one keyboard, no
                          secret to keep, nobody to ask.

  IT IS NOT IN ``snapshot()``.  That is what lets the server know something five
  clients do not without drifting from them: the fingerprint is computed over
  public state only, so a server that has just been told the colour still
  matches every replica.  There is a test that holds this down by comparing
  snapshots of the same state with three different secrets in it.

  IT IS NOT IN THE COMMAND LOG either — the log is replayed to anyone who
  resyncs.  The colour travels in IDENTITY_CHOSEN, addressed to one peer, and
  that message is never logged, never broadcast and never relayed.

  Which is also why ``setup.assign_secret_pawn`` is SKIPPED online.  It draws
  from the shared seed, so anything it picks can be recomputed by every client
  — a "secret" sitting in the open on five machines.

WHERE THE SELECTION BEGINS  (one flow, two sources of the question)
  ONLINE: START_GAME → the server builds the state, broadcasts GAME_START, and
  sends IDENTITY_REQUIRED to the one peer holding the Piotrek card.  Every
  machine is now in ``MatchPhase.STARTING`` and NOTHING is playable: the engine
  refuses every command that is not authority-issued, so a client that skips
  the overlay gets nowhere.  IDENTITY_CHOSEN comes back, the server stores it
  with ``set_piotrek_pawn`` and appends BeginMatch to the log — one broadcast,
  so everybody starts at the same moment.  If Piotrek drops while choosing,
  ``catch_up`` asks again; if he drops AFTER choosing, ``catch_up`` sends
  IDENTITY_ACCEPTED again, because his rebuilt replica gets the colour from
  neither the seed nor the log.

  HOT-SEAT: the same overlay, asked by ``GameScreen._identity_question`` from
  the library instead of from a server, answered into ``set_piotrek_pawn`` and
  followed by a locally submitted BeginMatch.  Everyone is at one keyboard, so
  there is nobody to hide it from and nobody else to wait for.

  THE RANDOM DRAW IS NOT A PRODUCTION PATH.  ``config.piotrek_picks_pawn`` is
  true for BOTH real entry points — ``lobby.to_config`` and ``MenuScreen`` — and
  ``setup.assign_secret_pawn`` only runs when it is false, which now means
  ``--players`` and ``--selftest``: a table with no interface and nobody to
  click.  Stage 17 left the local menu on the old path, which is why the picker
  looked as though it never appeared to anyone testing alone.

THE END OF A MATCH
  DeclareVictory sets ``state.victory`` (a ``Verdict``: outcome, colour, seat,
  name) and the phase to ENDED.  Applying it also writes the revealed colour
  into the player, because that is the moment it stops being a secret and a
  client has no other way to learn it.  Nothing is playable afterwards.

  GameScreen watches the STATE, not the events — ``_sync_match_overlays`` runs
  every frame — so a player replaying twenty commands after a reconnection ends
  up looking at the right screen rather than at whichever event went past last.

  THE THREE WAYS OUT, and they are not variations of each other:

    Wróć do poczekalni  keeps the connection, keeps the room, keeps the seats.
                        RETURN_TO_LOBBY → the server throws the match away and
                        broadcasts LOBBY_STATE; every client drops its session
                        on that message and lands back in the poczekalnia.  The
                        host can press Start again immediately — nobody
                        reconnects, nobody re-joins.
    Menu główne         CLOSES the connection and leaves the room, so the seat
                        is freed instead of being held open behind a grace
                        period nobody is waiting through.
    Wyjdź z gry         closes the application.

  A hot-seat match has no room to keep, so it offers only the last two.

  WHAT A REMATCH INHERITS: NOTHING.  ``return_to_lobby`` drops the GameState,
  the command log, the fingerprint and the hidden colour, and the next START
  builds a brand-new game from a NEW seed — board, decks, discard piles, hands,
  mods, statuses, ability counters, round, turn, active seat and pawn positions
  all come from ``create_game`` rather than from being cleared one by one.
  Clients throw their replica away on LOBBY_STATE and ``_on_game_start`` clears
  ``identity_pawn``, or the old colour would be written into the new replica.
  What DOES survive is what belongs to the lobby: the people, their nicknames,
  their character picks and the table settings.

  "Wróć do poczekalni" sends RETURN_TO_LOBBY.  The server drops the match
  (state, log, fingerprint, hidden colour), sets the lobby back and broadcasts
  LOBBY_STATE; every client sees ``started`` false, drops its session, and every
  GameScreen goes back to the poczekalnia on the SAME message.  Refused while a
  match is still running — otherwise one player could end everybody else's.
  A hot-seat game has no lobby to return to and does not offer the button.

PIOTREK'S OWN REMINDER  (stage 18)
  A filled circle and the colour name, in the right-hand panel above "Twoja
  Postać" (``CharacterPanel._draw_identity_badge``).  Two independent reasons it
  cannot leak: the row is only drawn when ``show_skill`` — a Piotrek panel, and
  a client may not look at anybody else's — and a hunter's replica has no
  colour in it to draw.  The row is RESERVED whenever the panel is Piotrek's,
  colour chosen or not, so the geometry depends on one thing both the drawing
  and the click handling already have; making it appear when the colour arrives
  would shift every rect below it by a few pixels and put the ability card just
  outside its own rectangle.  ``Layout.r_identity_h`` is in the right column's
  vertical budget, so it costs the card height rather than overflowing at
  1280×760.

THE NOTEPAD IS NOT A NOTEPAD ANY MORE
  "Kolory Piotrka" draws ``state.eliminated_pawns`` and is not clickable.  A
  colour is crossed off when a check failed, which is something only the
  authority knows; a hunter's private hunch on a panel that looks shared is
  worse than no panel.  ``ToggleMark`` and ``Player.marks`` still exist and
  still work — nothing issues them.

HOW MULTIPLAYER WORKS  (rewritten in stage 11)
================================================================================

Model: DEDICATED AUTHORITATIVE SERVER + DETERMINISTIC REPLICATION.

WHY THE HOST IS NO LONGER THE SERVER.  A machine behind a home router does not
accept incoming connections — that is NAT, not a bug — so "one player hosts"
could only ever work on a LAN or with port forwarding. The requirement was two
friends in different countries with no router configuration, and the only thing
that satisfies it is: everybody connects OUTWARDS to a machine with a public
address. No client-side trick avoids this. The server therefore has to be
deployed somewhere (docs/SERWER.md); the game ships pointing at localhost.

THE FLOW
    MainMenuScreen ──► HostSetupScreen ──► LobbyScreen ──► GameScreen
                   └─► JoinScreen      ──► LobbyScreen ──► GameScreen
                   └─► local hot-seat  ──► MenuScreen  ──► GameScreen

LAYERS (and they do not leak)
    ui/  ──►  net/service  ──►  net/client  ──►  net/transport
                                                       │  WebSocket
    server/app (asyncio)  ──►  server/hub  ──►  server/room  ──►  engine/

  * net/config.py    every networking number; data/network.json; env overrides
  * net/protocol.py  message vocabulary + JSON.  WebSockets frame messages, so
                     there is no line framing any more (LineBuffer is GONE)
  * net/transport.py Transport ABC, ConnectionState, Loopback, Null
  * net/websocket.py THE ONLY CLIENT FILE THAT KNOWS A SOCKET EXISTS
  * net/client.py    GameClient: handshake, lobby mirror, resync, reconnection
  * net/service.py   HostService / ClientService — all the UI is allowed to see
  * server/hub.py    routing, identities, resume tokens.  NO asyncio, NO sockets
  * server/room.py   the authoritative GameState, seats, command log, THE
                     HIDDEN COLOUR, and the victory review after every command
  * server/app.py    ~200 lines of asyncio.  NO game rules.

  server/hub, room and registry are SYNCHRONOUS AND FREE OF I/O on purpose:
  all of multiplayer is testable in milliseconds without a network. Keep it so.

THE HANDSHAKE GATE (stage 13 — read this before touching net/client.py)
  Nothing but HELLO, PING and PONG may leave the client until the server has
  greeted THIS CONNECTION.  ``GameClient._send`` holds everything else in
  ``_pending``; ``_on_welcome`` releases it, intent first.

  Why it has to be a gate and not care: the screens act the moment the player
  clicks, and ``HostSetupScreen.confirm`` sends the table settings in the same
  function that opens the connection — before the game loop has polled once, so
  before HELLO has been sent at all.  The transport flushes its queue as soon as
  the socket opens, so those settings arrived FIRST.  The server refused them
  (correctly), the refusal was fatal (wrongly), and hosting failed 100% of the
  time with "Najpierw przywitanie (hello)" on screen.  The lost message also
  carried ``debug_version``, which is why Debug Mode never gave 2-player games.

  The gate is keyed on ``Transport.generation``, not on "are we connected": a
  fresh socket after a drop is connected and completely unknown to the server.
  That is also what makes a click during a reconnection get replayed instead of
  refused.

FRIENDLY ERRORS (stage 13 — net/messages.py)
  Three sources of failure text and only one of them is ours: the server writes
  Polish sentences, ``websockets`` writes English with socket jargon, and Python
  writes ``ConnectionRefusedError(111)``.  A raise site cannot know which it is
  passing on, so all three go through ``friendly()``.

  It is deliberately NARROW.  The server's own prose is more specific than any
  constant could be — "Nie ma pokoju o kodzie ZZZZZZ" names the code the player
  typed — so it passes through untouched.  The table catches only text that was
  never written for a player.  Three tests failed when this was got wrong; if
  you add a pattern, check you are not replacing a better sentence.

WHERE THE SERVER ADDRESS COMES FROM (stage 14 — four layers, later wins)
  1. the defaults in net/config.py — enough to play on this machine;
  2. data/network.json — what the OWNER edits after deploying.  Part of the
     installation: shipped, hand-edited, possibly read-only, possibly inside a
     PyInstaller temp directory that is deleted on exit;
  3. the remembered address, in the USER's own config directory
     (%APPDATA% / ~/Library/Application Support / $XDG_CONFIG_HOME).  Written by
     the game, not by a person.  Above the shipped file so an address is never
     typed twice; below the environment so automation still wins;
  4. environment variables and --server.

  REMEMBERED WHEN A ROOM ACTUALLY EXISTS, not when the address is typed.  The
  distinction is the whole value of the feature: remembering on connection
  re-fills the field with whatever typo just failed.

  ``is_usable_url`` guards what may be saved, and it is not a formality.  What
  the lobby offers is whatever the transport calls itself, and the in-process
  transport calls itself "in-process:c3".  Saved unchecked that became
  ``ws://in-process:c3:51337``, whose port is not a number, and every later
  ``urlparse(...).port`` raised — including the one drawing the main menu.  One
  bad string in a preferences file broke the game's first screen.  Hence also:
  ``describe_target`` never raises.

TWO IDENTITIES, AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME
  * connection id = one socket.  Dies with the WiFi.
  * peer id       = one player.  Survives a reconnection.
  Joined by a RESUME TOKEN minted on the first HELLO. Without this split,
  "reconnect" can only mean "join again as a stranger" — which is what stage 6
  did and why a dropped player lost their hand.

LOBBY (net/lobby.py, replicated whole — small, changes rarely)
  * rooms are found by a six-character CODE from an unambiguous alphabet (no
    0/O, no 1/I/L: it gets read aloud and typed by someone not looking at it);
  * a client may *ask* for a nickname or character; the SERVER decides. Taken
    characters are refused server-side, not merely hidden client-side;
  * host-only: table settings and Start, both enforced by the server;
  * if the host drops in the lobby, promote_new_host() hands the role on — a
    room nobody can start is worse than mild unfairness;
  * DEBUG VERSION drops the minimum to RULES.debug_min_players (2). Nothing else.

STARTING (Room.start)
  * the server builds a SessionConfig — CRUCIALLY INCLUDING THE SEED — and
    broadcasts it with the seat map;
  * every client then builds the game *itself*. No card ever crosses the wire.

THE COMMAND PIPELINE (Room.submit) — the heart of it
    seated?  →  authorise_remote(cmd, seat)  →  apply(cmd, local=False)
       │                    │                         │
       └── COMMAND_REJECTED ┘                         ├─ ActionRejected  → sender
                                                      ├─ ChoiceRequired  → sender
                                                      │    (NOT logged, NOT
                                                      │     broadcast — nothing
                                                      │     changed)
                                                      └─ applied → log + broadcast
                                                         COMMAND_ACCEPTED with a
                                                         state FINGERPRINT
  * clients apply broadcast commands with ``apply(command, local=False)``, which
    skips local authorisation — re-judging somebody else's legal move against
    this machine's seat would reject everything and desync;
  * NO optimistic prediction. A wrong guess would have to be rolled back, and in
    this game a rollback means having shown a hand that should have stayed
    hidden. The animation covers the round trip instead.

DESYNC IS SELF-HEALING
  Every COMMAND_ACCEPTED carries the server's fingerprint. A client whose own
  fingerprint differs, or which sees a gap in the sequence, asks for STATE_SYNC
  (rate-limited to once a second) and rebuilds from seed + log. STATE_SYNC is
  the ONLY message carrying a match's worth of data and is sent twice in a
  normal game's life: on reconnection, and never otherwise.

DISCONNECTION
  * every failure is a *state*, not an exception. WebSocketTransport sets
    ConnectionState and a readable Polish ``error``;
  * the transport reconnects the SOCKET with exponential backoff; GameClient
    reconnects the PLAYER by watching ``Transport.generation`` and re-sending
    HELLO with the resume token;
  * the game keeps drawing throughout — a banner, not a trip to the menu;
  * the server holds the seat for reconnect.grace_period, then releases it and
    the match carries on with an empty seat;
  * a second connection claiming the same player wins; the older is told why.

CARD UIDS ARE DETERMINISTIC (engine/setup.build_decks). A command names a card
by uid, so the numbering has to match on every machine. Uids are assigned from
deck position, not from the process-global counter they default to.

OWNERSHIP AND TURNS (engine/game_state.py) — unchanged by stage 11
  * ``may_control(seat)``   — edit mode, or your own seat;
  * ``may_act(seat)``       — edit mode, or it is your turn;
  * ``authorise_remote(command, seat)`` — what the SERVER runs for incoming
    commands. ``_OWNED_BY_PLAYER`` and ``_TURN_BOUND`` say which rule covers
    which command. Private bookkeeping (the colour notepad, renaming, answering
    the chest limit) is deliberately NOT turn-bound.

DEBUG PANEL (F3, ui/debug_panel.py): server, room code, connection state, ping,
sent/received counters and last action each way, in-flight actions, sequence
number, reconnect/resync counts, turn/round, and a SHA-1 fingerprint of the
snapshot — two machines showing the same fingerprint are in sync.

RUNNING A SERVER  (the owner's copy of this is SERVER_SETUP.md — send them there)
    python -m pedzacy_piotrek.server               # 0.0.0.0:51337
    python -m pedzacy_piotrek.server --rooms 20    # many lobbies
    python run_game.py --serve                     # same, from the game
  Reads PORT from the environment (every PaaS injects it; ignoring it is the
  single most common reason a deployment answers nothing). TLS is terminated by
  a proxy in front, never here.

  DEPLOYMENT FILES (stage 13), and why each exists:
    SERVER_SETUP.md          the owner's guide; assumes no backend experience
    railway.json             start command, /health check, restart policy
    requirements-server.txt  websockets ONLY.  requirements.txt pins pygame,
                             and installing it on a host drags in SDL to run a
                             process that never opens a window
    .railwayignore           omits ui/, render/, assets/, tests, tools, docs
    Procfile, Dockerfile     for platforms that read those instead

  THE HEALTH PAGE.  GET / (also /health, /healthz, /status) answers plain Polish
  text.  Without it the address returns ``426 Upgrade Required``, which is
  correct and reads exactly like a broken deployment to somebody who does not
  know what a WebSocket handshake is.  It is the only diagnosis tool a beginner
  reliably has, and it is what tells "server down" from "address wrong".
  Handles BOTH ``process_request`` signatures — websockets changed it at v14.

  THE SERVER IS A SEPARATE APPLICATION, and it is asserted, not merely claimed:
  ``test_the_server_needs_neither_pygame_nor_the_client_packages`` constructs it
  with pygame, ui/ and render/ all made unimportable.  Keep that test green and
  the lean deployment stays honest.

================================================================================
CODING GUIDELINES
================================================================================

- Python 3.12, `from __future__ import annotations` at the top of every module.
- Dataclasses for data; plain classes for behaviour.
- Type hints everywhere. Keep them accurate.
- Docstrings explain WHY, not what. If a decision looks odd, the docstring must
  say what it prevents.
- Polish for user-facing strings, English for code.
- No global mutable state except the deliberate feature switches in settings.
- Never call random.* directly; use an injected random.Random.
- Never use browser-style shortcuts like magic numbers in ui/ — use Layout.
- New user actions -> new Command + handler entry in GameState._HANDLERS +
  a registry entry in COMMAND_REGISTRY + a test.
- Run `python -m pytest -q` before declaring anything done. Add tests for new
  behaviour; the suite is the safety net that made two large refactors safe.

THINGS FUTURE LLMS SHOULD NEVER DO

N1. Never rewrite the project from scratch or "modernise" the architecture.
N2. Never import pygame into engine/ or put rules into render/ or ui/.
N3. Never mutate GameState from the UI. Add a Command.
N4. Never remove or simplify existing mechanics: the turn cadence, the two-slot

    manual round counter, renaming, free pawn dragging.
N5. Never relax the board spacing checks to make a board look curvier.
N6. Never replace the seeded RNG with global random.
N7. Never infer card behaviour from card titles (the prototype did; it broke as
    soon as a card was renamed).
N8. Never hard-code screen coordinates; go through Layout.
N9. Never implement multiplayer by broadcasting whole game states.
N10. Never leave "implement later" stubs. Either build it properly or leave it
     out and record it here under KNOWN LIMITATIONS.
N11. Never add libraries beyond pygame without a strong reason; the project
     must stay easy to package as a single .exe.
N12. Do not delete tests to make a change pass.
N13. Never number board fields sequentially again (1,2,3,...) — 12a/12b share a
     number on purpose, and movement counts positions, not fields.
N14. Never let a doubled destination pick its own half "to keep things simple".
N15. Never rebuild SessionConfig field by field (use dataclasses.replace); the
     old create_game did and silently dropped a setting between menu and board.
N16. Never write `if card.title == "..."` or an if/else chain over effect names
     outside the handler registry. That is the thing this architecture exists
     to prevent.
N17. Never parse gameplay numbers out of card descriptions ("2x", "dwa pola").
     Descriptions are for humans; add a structured field instead.
N18. Never add a boolean flag to GameState for a new mechanic — add a
     StatusKind, so expiry, display and serialisation come for free.
N19. Never spend a use, a card or a status before the plan has executed.
N20. Never build a bespoke selection interface for one card.  Return a Choice
     and let ChoicePrompt draw it.
N21. Never let a card that was revealed or played stay in the draw pile — the
     random reveal forgot to discard it and could turn the same card up twice.
N22. Never gate "who may act" in the interface alone; use may_control() and
     may_act(); the host must be able to refuse.
N23. Never synchronise game state during a match. Actions only. STATE_SYNC
     exists for reconnection and nothing else — if you reach for it during
     normal play, the design has gone wrong. The seed plus
     the command log is the state.
N24. Never re-authorise a command that arrived from the network — apply it with
     local=False. Re-judging it against the local seat desyncs instantly.
N25. Never trust ``player_index`` in a message; the SERVER maps peer → seat.
N26. Never import pygame into net/ or server/. The only client file allowed to
     know a socket exists is net/websocket.py; on the server it is
     server/app.py. server/hub.py, room.py and registry.py must stay
     synchronous and I/O-free — that is what makes multiplayer testable.
N27. Never let a network failure raise into the game loop; turn it into a state
     and a readable message.
N28. Never read typed characters from KEYDOWN.unicode — TEXTINPUT only.
N29. Never place a menu element at a fraction of the window height; measure the
     fonts and lay the screen out in one pass.
N30. Never ship the Debug Version option enabled by default; it is a testing
     aid and the real game needs RULES.min_players.
N31. Never draw a player's hand from ``state.active_player``.  Use the screen's
     ``view_seat`` / ``ctx.player``, or every client shows the host's cards.
N32. Never match players by name against ``hunter_names`` or ``piotrek_name``:
     those are CHARACTER titles.  Work in seats.
N33. Never scale a rasterised card, panel or glyph up.  Repaint it at the size
     it will be seen (``quantised``), or it will look like a stretched
     screenshot — which is what the whole of stage 9 was spent undoing.
N34. Never quote a new pixel size without ``ui_scale`` or a measured font.
N34a. Never write a literal colour outside ``config/theme.py``.  Add a named
     field instead — the style test counts the offenders.
N34b. Never draw a plain rectangle where content lives; ``premium_panel`` and

N35. Never widen a side column by hand; make its contents smaller or let
     ``_fit_*_column`` do it.  Every pixel a column takes is one the board loses.
N36a. Never make a card without artwork look different.  The Signature path is
     an OPTIONAL layer; the parchment face is the default and must stay
     byte-identical.  A change that "improves" both at once has broken the one
     requirement the feature exists to satisfy.
N36b. Never hard-code an artwork path in the renderer, and never write
     ``if card.title == "Troll"`` to give a card a picture.  The filename IS
     the configuration; ``card_art.py`` is the only file that resolves it.
N36d. Never paint anything on top of a card face.  One border, the card's own
     content, nothing else.  A badge, a pip, a counter or a frame added there

N36c. Never let a missing, renamed or corrupt card-art file raise.  It falls

N36. Never make the engine wait for an animation.  Apply the state change and
     let the view catch up, or two machines will disagree at different frame
     rates.

N37. Never make the host authoritative again. Every player is a client; a
     machine behind NAT cannot accept incoming connections, so a peer-hosted
     game can never work across the internet. The host's only privileges are
     the Start button and the table settings, and the SERVER enforces both.
N38. Never hard-code a networking value. Address, port, timeout, heartbeat,
     backoff, grace period, TLS — all of it lives in net/config.py and
     data/network.json. Read PORT from the environment or hosting breaks.
N39. Never block the game loop on the network. ``poll()`` returns immediately,
     connecting happens in a background thread, and a failure is a state with a
     Polish message — never an exception and never a wait.
N40. Never log or broadcast a command that produced only a ChoiceRequired. It
     changed nothing, so replaying it puts a modal on every screen asking a
     question one player can answer. Send CHOICE_REQUIRED to the asker alone.
N41. Never renumber seats once a match has started. The seat index is baked
     into every command already in the log; renumbering rewrites history.
N42. Never apply a command optimistically on a client. There is no rollback,
     and in this game rolling back means having shown hidden information.
N43. Never give a dataclass event a field that collides with a GameEvent
     property (``name``). It generates an __init__ that assigns over the
     property and every use of that event fails at runtime — this shipped
     broken in PlayerRenamed and nothing caught it for four stages.
N44. Never draw a highlight that does not follow the shape of what it
     highlights. No ``glow(centre, radius * 2.4, ...)`` discs: rectangles get
     ``shape_glow``, round things get ``ring_glow``, and both take the
     component's OWN geometry. A test greps for the old call and fails.
N45. Never write a control's hover colours by hand. Ask
     ``render/highlight.emphasis()`` and draw with ``interactive_panel``, or
     the interface goes back to having five slightly different hovers.
N46. Never quote a button's width or height. Call ``Button.fit`` (or
     ``fit_buttons`` for a group) and let the caption decide. A hard-coded
     320 px clipped "Gra lokalna (hot-seat)" at 1440p, and the same mistake in
     the right column clipped "UŻYJ UMIEJĘTNOŚCI (1/1)" by 41 px.
N47. Never size a control that swaps captions to the caption showing at layout
     time. Measure the longest one it can hold.

N48. Never send anything to the server before it has said WELCOME on the
     CURRENT connection. Use GameClient._send, which gates it; do not reach
     past it to transport.send. A screen acts before the first poll, so the
     handshake has not happened yet, and the transport flushes in order — this
     is how hosting was broken 100% of the time for two stages.
N49. Never make a protocol violation fatal. Dropping the message costs one
     action; dropping the player costs the match. The client is supposed to
     make mis-ordering impossible, so reaching that branch means an old build.
N50. Never put raw exception, library or protocol text where a player can read
     it. Everything goes through net/messages.friendly(). The details belong in
     the debug panel (F3), which is where somebody who wants them is looking.
N51. Never widen net/messages._PATTERNS to match the server's own Polish. Those
     sentences name the room code, the player and the action; a constant that
     replaces them loses information. The table is for text written for a
     machine, not for prose written for a person.
N52. Never write a second text-editing implementation. TextEditor is the one;
     wrap it as TextInput or TextField did. There were two, and only one of
     them could paste.
N53. Never assume a bare hostname means one thing. A dotted public name is a
     deployed server on wss://443; an IP or localhost is a home machine on
     ws://51337. normalise_url draws the line, and getting it backwards fails
     silently on every connection.
N54. Never import ui/ or render/ from server/ or net/, and never add pygame to
     requirements-server.txt. A test constructs the server with all three made
     unimportable; that test is what keeps the deployment small.
N55. Never point a hosting platform at requirements.txt. It pins pygame.

N56. Never write a text field that reimplements editing. Wrap TextEditor, and
     call tick(dt) every frame and release() on focus loss. A field that types
     but will not hold-to-delete is the symptom of forgetting tick().
N57. Never draw placeholder text in the same colour as typed text. It is
     darken(theme.text_dim, 0.72) everywhere, and a test measures it.
N58. Never write game-decided settings into data/network.json. That file is the
     OWNER's, shipped with the installation, and may be read-only or inside a
     PyInstaller temp directory. Per-user state goes through
     net/config.save_preferences().
N59. Never remember a server address that has not actually produced a room.
     Remembering on connection re-fills the field with the typo that failed.
N60. Never save an address without is_usable_url(). A transport's description
     is not always a URL: "in-process:c3" became ws://in-process:c3:51337,
     whose port is not a number, and it broke the main menu until the
     preferences file was deleted by hand.
N61. Never let a urlparse(...).port call reach a screen unguarded. It raises
     ValueError on a malformed address, and drawing code must shrug, not throw.
N62. Never cache "no display yet" as a clipboard answer. It pins the system
     clipboard off for the whole session and silently demotes every copy and
     paste to the internal buffer.
N63. Never copy decoration along with the payload. The room-code button copies
     K7M2QD, not "KOD POKOJU: K7M2QD" — it is going straight into a join
     field at the other end.
N64. Never assume which pygame.scrap API is present. put_text/get_text exist
     only in pygame-ce; upstream pygame 2.6 (what requirements.txt asks for)
     has neither, and calling them raises AttributeError. Stage 14 called
     exactly those, inside `except Exception: pass`, and the game spent a whole
     stage with a clipboard nothing outside it could see.
N65. Never pass pygame.SCRAP_TEXT and assume it works. It is "text/plain",
     which upstream pygame's SDL2 backend rejects; the only string that backend
     accepts is "text/plain;charset=utf-8". Try a list, remember what worked.
N66. Never swallow an exception around a clipboard call without a test that
     reaches past the module to pygame.scrap itself. Every clipboard test in
     the suite passed while nothing reached the operating system, because they
     all went through copy() and paste() — which agreed with each other.
N67. Never let the internal fallback answer when the system clipboard could be
     read. An empty system clipboard is an answer; pasting a stale in-process
     value over it is a lie about what the user copied.
N68. Never encode clipboard text without asking which FORMAT it is going into.
     pygame converts nothing on Windows: bytes handed to
     "text/plain;charset=utf-8" land in CF_UNICODETEXT and are read as
     UTF-16LE. UTF-8 there turns "sdh" into 摳. Encoding and type travel
     together in _TEXT_FORMATS.
N69. Never trust a clipboard round trip through pygame as proof that copying
     works. On Windows, scrap_win.c returns the program's OWN cached bytes
     while it still owns the clipboard, so a corrupt copy reads back perfectly
     inside the game. Only another application can tell you, or the
     scrap_win.c stand-in in tests/test_usability.py.
N70. Never rstrip NULs before decoding UTF-16. The high byte of a trailing
     ASCII character is one, and "ABCD12" loses its 2.
N71. Never put the hidden colour in snapshot(), in a command, or in the command
     log. The snapshot is the fingerprint (the server would drift from every
     client the moment it was told); the log is replayed to anyone who resyncs.
     It travels in one message, to one peer.
N72. Never let a client decide a winner. victory.review() runs on the authority
     only, and authorise_remote refuses BeginMatch, EliminatePawn and
     DeclareVictory from a client outright — sending one is claiming to have
     won.
N73. Never deal Piotrek's colour from the seed in an online match. Every client
     builds its state from that same number, so a dealt secret is not a secret.
N74. Never drive an end-of-match screen from an event alone. A reconnecting
     player replays the whole log in one frame; drive the overlays from the
     STATE, every frame, or they show whatever went past last.
N75. Never add a second way to decide the identity. If a screen skips the
     picker, fix the config flag; do not deal a colour "just for that case".
     Stage 17 shipped with the local menu on the old random path and the whole
     feature looked broken to anybody testing on one machine.
N76. Never carry anything across a rematch by hand. return_to_lobby DROPS the
     state and the next start builds a new one from a new seed. Clearing
     fields one at a time is how the eleventh one gets forgotten.
N77. Never re-apply a remembered secret to a freshly built replica without
     checking which match it belongs to. _on_game_start clears identity_pawn
     first, or the previous match's colour is written into the new one.


N78. Never let a handler consume randomness. The UI resolves handlers to draw
   previews while a card is being dragged, so a handler that rolled a die would
   change what the card does every frame. Randomness belongs to the executor —
   ForcedPlay and PlayRandomCard are both operations for this reason — and it
   must be drawn from state.rng, over a list with a REPLICA-STABLE order (sort
   by uid; hand order is not something two machines are obliged to agree on).

N79. Never mark a card "locked" without also keeping it out of the opening
   deal. Locked cards are armed by on_draw, which the opening deal does not
   run, so one dealt at setup can never be played, discarded or resolved and
   silently costs its owner a hand slot for the entire game. CardDef.opens_a_hand
   derives this from "locked" so it cannot be got wrong again — do not
   reintroduce a path that reads in_opening_hand directly.

N80. Never assume a status is enforced because something grants it. SKIP_TURN
   was granted by two abilities from stage 4 to stage 19 and read by nothing at
   all; both cards looked implemented and did nothing. When adding a StatusKind,
   add the code that CONSUMES it in the same change, and a test that proves the
   consumption happens.

N81. Never put a card title into an event that the whole table sees. CardStolen
   carries seats, a uid and a deck id, and that is deliberate — only the thief
   was shown the hand. The same goes for any future effect that reveals hidden
   information to one player.


N82. Never identify a turn by its seat. A seat can hold several slots in one
   round (Piotrek holds every third), so `order.index(seat)` finds the first of
   them and rewinds the round. The cursor is `turn_slot`, it is MOVED and never
   searched for, and the invariant is seat_order()[turn_slot] == active seat.

N83. Never test a schedule by testing the function that computes it. The order
   generator was correct and tested for sixteen stages while the game looped
   over three seats. Assert on the turns ACTUALLY TAKEN, by driving EndTurn, and
   check the walk reaches every seat.

N84. Never size a turn-order test so small that the bug cannot appear. Three
   players give Piotrek exactly one slot per round, so a repeat-slot bug is
   invisible; the online test uses six and asserts up front that he holds more
   than one.

N85. Never route the Mod Patusa selection through _install_mod. Each faction
   owns one slot for the whole game, so the selection WRITES its slot; pushing
   would make Piotrek's choice shunt the hunters' card along the rack. The push
   belongs to PlaceMod and Thunderfuck and to nothing else.

N86. Never give Thunderfuck a "prefer the first free slot" behaviour again. It
   filled the RIGHT slot while the LEFT one stayed put, which is the opposite
   of the rule and was invisible until the rack was half full. It always pushes
   from the left, and on an EMPTY rack it does nothing at all.

N87. Never break the leftmost tie-break by sorting the hunters' candidates. The
   rule is "the leftmost tied card wins", and it holds because `max` iterates
   the cards in DEALT order and keeps the first maximum. Reordering that list
   anywhere — including for display — changes the game rule.

N88. Never let a screen count the votes. ModVoteCast carries the engine's whole
   tally; a screen that recomputed it would be a second opinion about who won,
   and the two would drift the moment a vote was changed.

N89. Never assume the Mod Patusa overlay only has to serve one side. In edit
   mode one person owns every seat, so it hands the same player Piotrek's pick
   and then each hunter's vote in turn. The first version marked Piotrek's side
   "settled" and stopped there, and the selection could never finish — which
   also froze the table, because the pause only lifts when both sides have
   chosen.

N90. Never add a settings row without re-running tests/test_menu_layout.py at
   the smallest window. The menu's fitting loop accepted a layout on row height
   alone and had been quietly letting the footer hang off the bottom; a fifth
   row made it visible. It now checks the real bottom edge — keep it that way.

N91. Never make the chest skip rounds by leaving the rota stepping once per
   ROUND. With an even number of hunters every dealing round then lands on the
   same half of the rota and the other hunters are dealt NOTHING for the whole
   match — with two hunters, one of them takes every card in the game. The rota
   steps once per HAND-OUT (ceiling division by the interval). At three players
   this is the difference between a working chest and a dead one.

N92. Never draw the chest marker from chest_is_open. It answers "has the chest
   started", not "does a card arrive this round", and on a small table those
   differ every other round. Both the ribbon and the engine read
   chest_awards_cards(), so a filled dot always means a card is coming.

N93. Never give the chest cadence its own state, command or event. It is a pure
   function of round number, opening round and table size, all of which every
   replica already agrees on. Anything that needs syncing here is a sign the
   rule has been put in the wrong place.

N94. Never write a test that asserts a marker and its mechanic DISAGREE. The
   chest ribbon marked Piotrek from the day the chest existed and the engine
   never dealt to him, and the suite stayed green because a test asserted
   outright that he is never fed ("assert not ...is_piotrek"). A test can
   freeze a bug in place as easily as it can catch one: when a test encodes
   "this thing does not work", check that somebody decided that on purpose.

N95. Never assume only one player can be over the chest limit at a time. A
   dealing round feeds two seats and both can overflow on the same round. While
   pending_chest_choice was a single slot the second overflow erased the first,
   stranding a card in a hand for the rest of the match — with eight chest
   cards the deck bled dry and hunters stopped receiving. It is a queue; keep
   it one.

N96. Never reorder the chest recipients or make their order depend on anything
   local. Piotrek is dealt first and the hunter second, on every machine, because
   two draws off the same pile in a different order is a desync.

N97. Never cut the chest deck back to one copy per title. Two cards go out per
   dealing round, and eight cards cannot supply a six-player table that already
   holds seven of them at the limit — the hunter goes without, every round,
   for the rest of the match. It is sixteen (eight titles, two each) and the
   doubling is UNIFORM so that no title is more likely than another.

N98. Never ask a Mod Patusa what it is CALLED. Its rule is declared in
   cards.json as a `passive` and read through GameState.mod_rule. A chain of
   `if card.title == "Masa solna"` is the thing this project does not do, and
   a mod is not an exception to it. Adding a mod that caps movement, locks
   abilities or pins lone pawns is a JSON entry and no Python.

N99. Never express "this card did nothing" as an empty Plan. `Plan.ok` is
   `bool(self.operations)`, so an empty plan reads as a REFUSAL to every caller
   and the card stays in the hand — the opposite of Halloween's rule, which is
   that the card resolves and is discarded. Emit a real operation whose
   executor does nothing (`Fizzle`), the way Thunderfuck does for an empty
   rack, and say why: a card that silently did nothing looks like a bug.

N100. Never let a handler open a prompt without checking `ctx.can_ask`. Seks z
   pedałami and Troll's forced play choose from `resolves_without_asking`,
   which is computed from the PRINTED card and cannot know a mod has just given
   it a question. Without the check, every revealed backward card became "bez
   efektu" while Speedrun was in the rack. Fall back to something legal —
   Speedrun declines the reversal, which is always allowed because the mod only
   ever offers one.

N101. Never make Halloween's neighbour test literal for the camp. Every pawn
   starts at CAMP_INDEX, so under "one in front or one behind" nobody in the
   camp ever has a neighbour, and Halloween reaching the rack on round 1 —
   which the lobby allows — freezes the board permanently: nothing moves,
   nobody reaches the finish, no tower is built, so NEITHER FACTION CAN WIN.
   Pawns in the camp are neighbours of one another. This is a decision, not a
   reading; if it is ever reversed, the deadlock must be solved first.

N102. Never move the Mod Patusa counts into the settings rows. Both screens
   shrink their gaps until they fit and stage 21 already lost the Start button
   to a fifth row at 1280×760; eight more do not fit at any gap. They live in
   an overlay. The button that opens it goes BESIDE the Mod Patusa row, never
   under it, and the steppers inside are MEASURED rather than inset by a
   constant — Stepper sizes itself to its own labels at the current type scale,
   so any fixed inset is wrong at some window size.

N103. Never apply a movement mod to an ability or a chest card. Masa solna and
   Halloween are rules about MOVEMENT CARDS; Dziad's ability is an ordinary
   `move_pawn` and would be caught by both. `ctx.from_movement_card` is the
   test, and it is why EffectContext carries `origin` and `deck_id` at all.

N112. Never give a card, a shelf or a label band a FLAT pixel ceiling. Every
   one of them is quoted against ui_scale for a reason: a hand card capped at
   260 showed a 4K screen the same card as a 1920x1200 laptop, and a label band
   capped at 26 was overflowed by its own type at 1440p. If a number stops
   growing while what it contains does not, it is a bug waiting for a bigger
   monitor.

N113. Never draw a heading beside a number without measuring the number first.
   The counters are the DATA and cannot shrink; the heading can, and must.
   "UMIEJĘTNOŚCI PIOTRKA" ran straight into its own "2 / 0" the moment the type
   grew. Use fitted_font against the room the counters leave.

N114. Never test one column and assume the other. The deck-label collision test
   walked the LEFT column for four stages while the longest deck name in the
   game sat unmeasured in the RIGHT one. The regression test for it RENDERS and
   reads back what was actually drawn, which is the only kind that could have
   caught it.

N115. Never index the settings panel's steppers by a title's position in its
   deck. There is one stepper per VISIBLE row because the movement tab scrolls,
   and they are zipped against `visible_titles`. Off-by-a-scroll edits the
   wrong card silently.

N116. Never add a fifth settings mapping without adding it to ALL FOUR places
   it has to travel: SessionConfig (and normalised()), LobbyState (field,
   to_dict, from_dict, to_config), room.set_settings' merge loop, and
   setup.build_decks. Three of those are where the earlier ones were nearly
   dropped, and normalised() has silently eaten a setting twice already (N15).

N117. Never drive a transient ANNOUNCEMENT from the state the way N74 requires
   for a condition. The "nie Piotrek" card is a moment: driven from the state it
   would reappear for every colour a reconnecting client replays. Driven from
   the event, each show replaces the last and a replay ends on the right one.
   N74 still holds for anything that is a CONDITION — the match overlays.

N105. Never assume the checking mechanic is missing because this file used to
   say so. It has existed since stage 17 as engine/victory.review(): all pawns
   on one field, inspect the BOTTOM one, hunters win or the colour is crossed
   off. Four places in this file called it "the main blocker" for seven stages
   after it shipped, and stage 25 was one mistaken reading away from building a
   second one. When a note and the changelog disagree, the changelog is newer.

N106. Never let a mod that acts at a MOMENT be armed anywhere but
   `_sync_mod_states`. Arrival and departure are transitions of the rack, not
   properties of it, so they are computed by comparing `armed_mods` with
   `mod_slots` and nowhere else. A mod that arms itself from `_begin_round`, or
   from the selection only, is a mod Thunderfuck can install without it ever
   firing.

N107. Never leave a departing mod's effect behind. The rack is the only thing
   keeping a mod's rule alive, so removing Shady puts its pawn back at once and
   removing Squid Game drops the pending check. Without the departure half,
   replacing Shady mid-round strands a pawn nowhere for the rest of the match —
   and a stranded pawn cannot be moved, checked or won with.

N108. Never widen Paczka's exception to N81. `ChestCardsRevealed` carries card
   titles to the whole table because Paczka's text IS "the Chest is public" —
   the card is the entitlement. That is the ONE event allowed it. Any other
   event carrying Chest titles is a leak, and the fact that this one exists is
   not a precedent.

N109. Never let one machine decide WHO an automatic check inspects and another
   decide what it means. Squid Game arms on every replica from public
   information (`pending_lead_check`, in the snapshot) and is judged only by
   `victory.review` on the authority. Moving the judgement to the clients means
   a client declaring a winner (N72); moving the arming to the server alone
   means the server drifting from every replica.

N110. Never clear state that a command settles from the code that armed it.
   `pending_lead_check` is cleared inside `_eliminate_pawn` and
   `_declare_victory`, so every machine clears it at the same point in the log.
   Clearing it where the check was armed clears it on the authority only, and
   five replicas go on waiting for a check that already happened.

N111. Never remove a pawn from play without asking what still counts it.
   Shady's hidden pawn had to disappear from `_ordered_pawns`, `pawn_options`,
   `MoveProjection.positions`, `has_neighbour`, `victory.gathering_tile` and
   `BoardView._draw_order`. Miss the last and it is still clickable; miss the
   fifth and the hunters can never check again, because they are being asked to
   gather a pawn that is not on the board.

N118. Never let Dzieckorolka pick a widened half by itself. It is the ONE card
   where D8a does not apply: which half it walks through decides which pawn it
   sweeps up, so every doubled position on the route is a real question. The
   keys are numbered by STEP (`branch0`, `branch1`), not by position, so two
   questions cannot overwrite each other's answer.

N119. Never reverse the collected list twice, and never store it bottom-first.
   `MoveAndCollect.collected` is in TRAVEL order and the executor reverses it
   once on the way into the stack, because a stack is stored bottom-first and
   the rule is written downwards from the mover. Getting it the other way round
   is invisible with one collected pawn and wrong with two — and it decides who
   is at the BOTTOM of a tower, which is how the hunters win.

N120. Never carry riders on a card that moves EVERY pawn. Balbinka moves each
   pawn its own two fields, so a rider carried along AND moved in its own right
   travels four. `MoveBySteps.carry_riders` is False there, and the move ORDER
   (furthest first going forward, rearmost first going backward, bottom of a
   tower before what stands on it) is what stops a pawn landing on a field its
   occupant has not left yet.

N121. Never write the order of the windows down twice. The paint order and the
   input order ARE ``GameScreen._register_modals`` and nothing else. Adding an
   ``if ....active:`` branch to ``handle_event`` or a ``.draw()`` call to
   ``draw`` recreates the second list, and a second list drifts — which is a
   window painted on top while the window underneath answers the click. That
   is exactly what round 7 shipped (stage 44). Register a ``Modal``.

N122. Never open a secondary window from inside a phase that is still running.
   A Mod chosen by one faction must not put a window in front of the faction
   still choosing. The arrival is recorded on ``ModSelection.followup_uids``
   and replayed by ``_finish_mod_selection``, rebuilt FROM THE RACK so a mod
   already replaced owes nothing. This is a rule about WINDOWS, not about mods:
   a passive that only changes the board or the statuses still lands at once.

N123. Never make Esc a cancel again, and never make it a "hide all dialogs".
   It RESOLVES the active window with a choice drawn from the same valid set
   the interface would have accepted a click on, and the queue then advances.
   Hiding a dialog without resolving it leaves an invisible interaction holding
   the game, which is the failure this was added to prevent. Cancelling is the
   right button.

N124. Never draw Esc's fallback from the session RNG. It runs on ONE machine at
   a moment no other machine knows about, so the shared seeded stream would end
   up one number apart on every other replica (R4 exists to stop exactly that
   kind of divergence, and this is the case where obeying it literally causes
   it). What travels is the COMMAND, as it does for a mouse click.

N121. Never express "for one round" as a turn expiry. A round is a VARIABLE
   number of turns — Piotrek holds every third slot — so Gambit Patusa carries a
   round NUMBER in its payload and `_begin_round` retires the spent ones. A
   status that named a number of turns instead would end in the middle of the
   round it was supposed to cover, and by a different amount at every table
   size.

N122. Never stack-vs-replace a round-scoped status by habit. Gambit Patusa uses
   `stack=True` because a second one played DURING a reversed round is a promise
   about a DIFFERENT round; replacing would let it cancel the reversal it was
   played under. The default replace is still right for anything that refreshes
   (a freeze), which is the distinction to check.

N123. Never ask whether a RULE is still in the rack when what you mean is
   whether a CARD left. `_sync_mod_states` asked "does anything still hide?" and
   so a Rage Quit replacing a Shady with a Shady stranded the first pawn off the
   map for the whole match. A departure is about the card that left: the HIDDEN
   status records `mod_uid` and the restore matches on it.

N124. Never assume a failing suite means the code is wrong. Stage 27 opened with
   five failures on a tree nobody had touched, because the archive shipped a
   half-applied `data/cards.json` naming handlers that did not exist. Diff the
   tree against the archive before debugging.

N125. Never leave a card without an `effect` field. `Card.is_playable` reads
   exactly that, so a card whose rule is undesigned becomes UNCLICKABLE rather
   than merely ineffective — and at the chest limit a player can end up holding
   two dead cards. Undesigned cards declare `manual`.

N126. Never use `Announce` for a card that should still be discarded.
   `_op_announce` emits `ActionRejected`, and `_play_card` reads a rejection as
   "the play did not happen" and keeps the card in the hand. `Fizzle` is the
   idiom for *played, did nothing, said so* (N99).

N127. Never let a handler read the hidden colour. Handlers run on every replica
   and most of them hold `None` for it all match, so a handler that read it
   would build a different plan on different machines. A card that needs the
   secret raises a COLOURLESS flag and lets `victory.review` answer it on the
   authority — the road an elimination already takes (N72).

N128. Never ask a question about a field a pawn is already standing on. Gejtos
   asks which half of each widened NEIGHBOUR it means; the anchor's own half is
   a fact, not a choice, and asking would be a prompt with one real answer.

N129. Never build a "move several pawns as a block" card out of the movement
   system. Gejtos lifts a tower whole: no distance, no direction, no widened row
   on the way, and nothing for a Mod Patusa to shorten. `TransferStack` is that
   operation, and it reads `Tile.stack` into a list BEFORE placing anything,
   because that list is live and walking it while placing out of it skips every
   other pawn.

N130. Never index into a generated board in a multiplayer test. A room's seed is
   not the test's to choose, so `doubled[0]` means a different position from run
   to run: stage 27 shipped a test that passed when written, passed in
   isolation, and only failed once the suite grew enough to shift the order.
   Derive what the test needs from the board it was given.

N131. Never take a card back out of a HAND to satisfy a deck-quantity change.
   The Card Library's `-` removes from the draw pile, then the discard pile, and
   refuses when every remaining copy is out on the table. Reaching into a hand
   deletes a card somebody is holding — possibly the one they are about to play
   — and no "the count said five" is worth that. The same goes for the mod rack
   and an open Mod Patusa selection.

N132. Never insert a card onto the TOP of a draw pile. That is the next card
   somebody draws, so "add a copy" would become "hand a chosen card to the next
   player". Use the deck's own seeded rng for the position: it has been advanced
   by the same shuffles on every machine, so the place is agreed on without
   being predictable.

N133. Never give a table-bookkeeping command a `player_index`. `AdjustDeckCount`,
   `AdjustAbilityUses` and `RestoreAbilityUses` deliberately have none, which is
   what keeps them out of `_OWNED_BY_PLAYER` and `_TURN_BOUND` and lets ANY
   player restore ANY character's ability. Adding the field "for consistency"
   silently restricts every one of them to its own seat, and the requirement is
   the opposite.

N134. Never let a restore write the DEFAULT. `CardDef.uses` is the configured
   default and `Card.uses_left` is what is left of it; `+` may push the second
   past the first on purpose, and a restore returns it to the PRINTED number,
   not to the inflated one. A restore that copied current onto default would
   make every ability permanently as strong as the last person to click `+`.

N135. Never anchor a line of type to a button's edge. `Button.fit` sizes itself
   to its caption at the current type scale, so a label hung off `button.top`
   lands wherever the caption leaves it — at 2560x1440 that put the library's
   hint fourteen pixels inside the content rectangle, printed over the row of
   cards the clip had just cut off. Anchor to the region, and budget the band
   for everything in it. This is N46 in a third costume.

N136. Never paint UI in `theme.panel_bg` or `theme.background` inside the board
   viewport. Those two colours mean "nothing has been drawn here" to
   `test_the_world_fills_the_viewport_even_when_zoomed_right_out`, which samples
   the viewport for bare panel colour. The library's book icon used panel_bg for
   its pages and failed on six pixels. Draw with the control's own colours.

N137. Never take a card out of a deck and put it in a hand yourself. Go through
   `_deliver_card`: a card ACTS on the way into a hand (Troll draws its
   replacement, a chest card can trip the limit, the Gamechanger reveals
   itself), and a second route that does `hand.append` and an event of its own
   is a route where none of that runs. Stage 33 added a whole new way to draw
   and it owes every debt the old one does.

N138. Never search every deck for a card by title. Card identity in this project
   is (deck_id, title), and `take_titled` is scoped to one deck for that reason.
   Two decks are allowed to print the same title, and the day one does, a global
   search hands out whichever copy it found first.

N139. Never quote a library card's size as a flat share of the viewport. The
   share runs backwards on purpose — a SMALL window spends MORE of its height on
   the card (0.50 against 0.46), because the absolute pixels are what a reader
   needs and 1280x760 has few of them. A flat share left a card smaller than the
   one in the player's own hand.

N140. Never record who Piotrek is anywhere but the character card. The role is
   derived from `character.is_piotrek` and NOTHING else, which is the only
   reason Kingmaker could move it with one assignment and have the turn
   cadence, the chest limit, the Mod Patusa factions, the win conditions, every
   "only Piotrek" refusal and the whole right-hand panel follow for free. A
   cached `piotrek_seat`, a `role` field on Player or an `is_piotrek` boolean
   set at setup would each be a second copy to fall out of step with it — and
   the state it would disagree in is the one nobody would look at.

N141. Never move a role, a character or an ability without asking what is in the
   turn checkpoint. `undo.capture` stores hands, piles, charges, statuses and a
   LISTED set of scalars; it does not store who holds which character card, and
   never stored `secret_pawn`. Anything that changes those has to close the
   window (`_close_turn_window`, as GrantExtraTurn does) rather than leave one
   open over a state it cannot describe. `_SCALARS` is a list rather than a
   discovery for exactly this reason: a new field is a deliberate decision
   about whether a turn may change it.

N142. Never assume the fingerprint notices new shared state. It notices what
   `snapshot()` names, and nothing else. `piotrek_seat` had to be ADDED in stage
   46: the character titles in play, `has_character` and the seat-keyed
   `ability_uses` are all identical before and after a role swap, so two
   machines that disagreed about who Piotrek is would have agreed about every
   field in the dictionary. When a stage makes something CHANGEABLE that used
   to be fixed, that is the moment to ask whether the snapshot can tell.

N104. Never add a rule to `_move_pawn` without asking whether `_move_pawns`
   needs it too. Plagiat! moves backwards through the second handler, so
   Speedrun, Masa solna and Halloween all had to be applied twice. A rule added
   to one path only makes the two drift apart, and the difference is invisible
   until someone plays the one card that takes the other route.

================================================================================
HOW FUTURE PROMPTS SHOULD EXTEND THE PROJECT
================================================================================

A good prompt for this project:
- names the stage goal and what is explicitly OUT of scope;
- says which existing behaviour must be preserved;
- asks for complete code, no placeholders;
- asks for this file and CHANGELOG_LLM.md to be updated at the end.

A model working on this project should:
- read this file, then the modules it is about to touch (not the whole tree);
- prefer extending the Command/Event vocabulary over adding side channels;
- add tests alongside the feature;
- update KNOWN LIMITATIONS honestly rather than claiming completeness;
- append a new stage section to CHANGELOG_LLM.md and refresh the "CURRENT
  IMPLEMENTATION" and "RECOMMENDED ORDER" sections here.
